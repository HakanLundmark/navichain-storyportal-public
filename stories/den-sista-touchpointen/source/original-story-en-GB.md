# The Last Touchpoint — original storyboard (en-GB faithful translation)

**Source locale:** sv-SE  
**Locale:** en-GB  
**Translation type:** faithful  
**Narrative Master:** false

**Protagonist:** Susanne Wik, 39 — Head of Customer Service, Nordiska Hemvaror AB.  
**Supporting character:** Torbjörn Askeland — Head of Logistics.  
**Core conflict:** Customer service carries responsibility for the brand but depends on delayed information from an external 3PL.  
**Transformation:** Susanne goes from guessing for the customer to having current delivery status herself and reclaiming the final customer contact.  
**Continuity:** The same company as in Torbjörn’s story, but the story must remain strictly from Susanne’s customer-service perspective. No IT support. Navichain is the tool; the human is the hero.

## Cover
**Title:** THE LAST TOUCHPOINT  
**Subtitle:** Susanne & navichain’s control tower  
**Kicker:** “The delivery is your last meeting with the customer. The only question is who owns it.”  
**Motif:** Susanne in the foreground with a headset in her hand, relaxed and smiling. Behind her, a simple delivery overview with current status and ETA. A telephone handset resting in the background symbolises the call that never needed to come. The official Navichain logo appears discreetly in the corner of the screen.

## Panel 01
The customer-service room in the early morning. Susanne is seated with a headset, looking tired. Whiteboard behind her: **“WHERE IS MY DELIVERY?”** with twelve tally marks before ten o’clock.  
**Text:** “The same question, again and again. Susanne doesn’t have the answer at her fingertips — she has it as a guess.”

## Panel 02
Close-up of the NordCargo Solutions customer portal. **“Last updated: 07:15”**. The wall clock shows **11:20**.  
**Text:** “The portal she logs into was updated four hours ago. The customer wants to know the situation now.”

## Panel 03
Susanne on the phone gives an uncertain answer: **“should arrive today, I think”**. The phone rings again shortly afterwards; the same customer calls back because the delivery did not arrive.  
**Text:** “She promises something she cannot keep. Not because she is lying — because she knows no better than the portal does.”

## Panel 04
A printed forwarded email on Susanne’s desk about a bad customer review. The review text must be unreadable; **NORDISKA HEMVAROR** is clearly underlined in red.  
**Text:** “The criticism is aimed at Nordiska Hemvaror. No customer writes ‘bad 3PL partner’ in their review.”

## Panel 05
Susanne walks decisively to Torbjörn’s desk. A simple clipboard with ticked-off routes is visible.  
**Dialogue:** “Torbjörn — you said you own the data now. Can customer service see it too?”  
**Text:** “She has heard the rumour from the logistics department: apparently someone owns the data now. She wants to know whether that includes her too.”

## Panel 06
Torbjörn and Susanne at his screen with the delivery overview. The first discreet Navichain logo may be introduced here.  
**Dialogue:** “It isn’t my system. It’s ours.”  
**Text:** “Susanne gets access to the same delivery view.”

## Panel 07
Susanne back at her own desk with the same current delivery status. The old tally list has been erased; a new note reads: **“Everyone sees the same truth now.”**  
**Text:** “The same current status in the same system. No 3PL portal to wait for before she can answer.”

## Panel 08
The phone rings. Susanne answers calmly and reads the current status/ETA, for example **“ON THE WAY · ETA 13:40”**.  
**Text:** “The question has barely been asked before the answer is already there on the screen.”

## Panel 09
Susanne proactively contacts the customer herself through her usual customer-service flow, with the current status in front of her.  
**Text:** “Now Susanne can get in touch before the customer needs to ask — with the current status in front of her.”

## Panel 10
The whiteboard from panel 01 now shows **“CALLS THIS WEEK”** and a clearly shorter current bar compared with an old crossed-out one.  
**Text:** “Fewer calls, not because customers care less — but because they rarely need to wonder.”

## Panel 11
Susanne shows her manager a handwritten summary: shorter response times, fewer escalated cases, a small star in the margin. The manager smiles.  
**Text:** “The same manager, the same week as the previous complaint — now with a completely different figure to show.”

## Panel 12
Susanne and Torbjörn at the coffee machine with discreet Navichain logos on their mugs. Through the window, a modern European COE truck leaves the loading bay. Note: **“Next: when the vehicles themselves tell us where they are.”**  
**Text:** “The delivery is still someone else’s to drive. But the meeting with the customer — that is theirs again.”
