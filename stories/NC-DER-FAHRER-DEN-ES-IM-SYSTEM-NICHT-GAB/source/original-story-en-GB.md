# A Navichain story

## “The driver who wasn’t in the system — How Fredrik tamed the Christmas rush”

**Character:** Fredrik Alm, 39 — Resource Planner, Ådalens Frakt & Bud AB (18 of its own vehicles, brings in hired drivers from staffing agencies during seasonal peaks) **Supporting character:** Zoran Petrović — hired driver from the staffing agency Nordpool Bemanning, ADR-qualified **Supporting character:** Robert “Robban” Ek — driver, recurring series character (continuity, brief appearance in panels 9–10) **Source:** blog.navichain.se/resurshantering — Pillar 4: Resource Management (“Support for hired and temporary drivers” and “Plan proactively for holidays and peaks”) **Chosen angle:** A new angle in the series. The Susanne Wikström story (“90 days’ head start”) dealt with the permanent competence register and a tender. This story takes a different conflict from the same source article: mobile, temporary staffing during seasonal peaks — hired drivers who rotate in and out, and the risk of sending out a driver whose qualification no one has had time to verify. It ends with proactive planning ahead of the next seasonal peak. **Next story thread:** Ends with a teaser toward the vehicle module’s ADR equipment check (signage, fire extinguishers, eyewash) — an unused angle from the vehicle & hardware pillar.

## Visual style (standard for the whole series)

Warm 1950s Hergé/Tintin *ligne claire* aesthetic. Clean, evenly weighted black outlines. Flat, even colour areas — no halftone shading, no heavy raster shading. Parchment/cream-coloured base. Palette: rust red, mustard yellow, petroleum green, warm gold. Captions rendered as handwritten/hand-lettered text — never digital sans-serif. Physical attributes ahead of screen mock-ups where possible (binders, stamps, coffee mugs, clothes pegs on the noticeboard) — retro CRT screens where a screen is unavoidable. The surface should feel like a well-thumbed 1950s magazine found in a drawer after many years: subtle paper grain, soft edge wear, foxing, muted/slightly faded colour tones. The characters are warm and sympathetic throughout, genuinely happy and smiling in the resolution panels.

**Colour dramaturgy:**

- Panels 1–5: Red → problem/crisis phase
- Panel 6: Orange/mustard yellow → turning point
- Panels 7–12: Petroleum green → resolution

**Logo rules (apply to all panels):**

- Always called “Navichain’s official logo” — never “the N mark”, “N logo”, “N symbol”, “N monogram” or similar
- Completely absent in panels 1–5
- Introduced discreetly from panel 6/7 onward as environmental props (app header, coffee mug, binder spine, clothing, document watermark)
- Maximum about 5% of the panel area, never centred or advertising-like
- The illustrator must use Navichain’s official logo file, uploaded as a source file in the project — never an invented or stylised substitute
- No IT support appears anywhere in the story — Fredrik navigates Navichain entirely on his own

### Panel 1

**Image:** Fredrik’s office, mid-December, dark outside, snow against the window. The noticeboard behind him is full of notes: “EXTRA VEHICLES — W.51”, “STAFFING CALLED BACK”, several crossed-out rows. The phone is ringing, a stack of consignment notes for Christmas deliveries is growing on the desk. **Caption:** Christmas week. Volume has tripled. Fredrik has four of his own vehicles available — and needs nine. **Status:** 🔴 Red — staffing shortage

### Panel 2

**Image:** A hired driver, Zoran, comes in through the garage door, stepping out of an unfamiliar van. At loading he hands Fredrik a folded, worn sheet of paper. The date on the paper is hard to read, partly stained with ink. **Caption:** “Here — my ADR certificate. The staffing agency said everything was sorted.” Fredrik squints at the blurred date. **Status:** 🔴 Red — unverified qualification

### Panel 3

**Image:** Fredrik at the old Bakelite telephone, calling the staffing agency. A small time is visible on the wall clock: 16:47. At the other end of the line, an answering-machine symbol. **Caption:** The staffing agency has closed for the day. No one can confirm Zoran’s qualification before the vehicle has to leave. **Status:** 🔴 Red — no verification possible

### Panel 4

**Image:** Fredrik frantically flips through a binder labelled “HIRED — HISTORY”, full of loose notes from previous seasons, several with the same name but different crossed-out dates. **Caption:** Zoran has driven here before — in the summer. But is the same certificate still valid? No note says for certain. **Status:** 🔴 Red — no continuity

### Panel 5

**Image:** Fredrik stands with the clock ticking behind him, the load (marked with an ADR hazard diamond, class 3) waiting on the platform, Zoran waiting impatiently by the driver’s door. Fredrik must choose: send the vehicle out unverified, or miss the entire Christmas delivery. **Caption:** Twenty minutes until the time window closes. Send the vehicle — or let the customer down. No good option. **Status:** 🔴 Red — impossible choice

### Panel 6

**Image:** Fredrik sits down heavily at the desk after the vehicle has finally left, rubbing his forehead. He pushes aside the “HIRED — HISTORY” binder and opens his laptop. **Caption:** “Never again.” Fredrik decides: every hired driver must have their own digital profile — from the first minute, not afterwards. **Status:** 🟠 Orange — turning point

### Panel 7

**Image:** Fredrik at the computer, Navichain’s interface visible — he creates a time-limited driver profile for Zoran, photographs the ADR certificate with his phone and uploads it directly. A field “Assignment period: 12–24 Dec” is filled in. Navichain’s official logo discreetly in the corner of the screen. **Caption:** One profile, time-limited to the exact assignment. The system reads the validity date itself — no guessing left. **Status:** 🟢 Green — digital profile created

### Panel 8

**Image:** The screen shows Zoran’s profile with a clear green tick: “ADR — VALID UNTIL 2027-03”. Fredrik leans back, relieved. **Caption:** The truth in three seconds, not three phone calls. Zoran’s certificate is valid — in black and white. **Status:** 🟢 Green — qualification confirmed

### Panel 9

**Image:** The next day. Fredrik plans tomorrow’s runs in Navichain. The list of available drivers shows permanent and hired drivers side by side, colour-coded in the same way — only those with valid ADR qualification are shown for the dangerous-goods classified run. Robban appears at the bottom of the list, green tick. **Caption:** Permanent or hired — it no longer matters. The system shows only those who are actually allowed to drive that particular load. **Status:** 🟢 Green — automatic filtering

### Panel 10

**Image:** A few weeks later Fredrik clicks into a calendar view, a simple run overview with green, yellow and red fields extending into the future. Three rows glow yellow: “R. Ek — ADR — expires June”, “Four regular ADR certificates expire within 90 days”. **Caption:** In February, Fredrik can already see what is coming in June — right in the middle of summer traffic. Plenty of time to reschedule. **Status:** 🟢 Green — proactive planning

### Panel 11

**Image:** Fredrik books renewal courses directly from the calendar view, a couple of clicks, a confirmation email visible in a small notification box. Outside the window, trucks are leaving the yard in the early winter morning, calmly and under control. **Caption:** Four courses booked in advance. No last-second panic booking when summer finally arrives. **Status:** 🟢 Green — season secured

### Panel 12

**Image:** Fredrik in his office, an ordinary afternoon in January, leaning back with a coffee mug on which Navichain’s official logo is discreetly visible. Behind him, the noticeboard is now empty — only a photo of the team by the final vehicle of the Christmas delivery. A small note in the corner: “Next: vehicle ADR equipment — signs, extinguishers, eyewash.” **Caption:** The Christmas rush came and went. Nine vehicles, six of them hired — and not a single unclear qualification. **Status:** 🟢 Green — season completed

## Production notes for the illustrator

- **Continuity:** Robban Ek is an established series character (driver). Brief, natural appearance in panel 9 as one of the permanent drivers in the planning view — no leading role here.
- **New role and conflict:** Fredrik Alm is a resource planner, and unlike the Susanne Wikström story (permanent competence register, tender), this story is about mobile, temporary staffing under acute time pressure — another kind of risk from the same source article.
- **Panels 1–5 props:** Noticeboard with notes, a Bakelite telephone, a worn binder with loose notes, a folded and ink-stained paper certificate — contrasted with the time-limited digital profile in panel 7.
- **Panel 5 detail:** The ethical pressure (send an unclear driver or let the customer down) should feel real but not alarmist — the tone is stressful everyday work, not catastrophe.
- **Panels 9–10:** Show clearly that the system does not distinguish between permanent and hired staff in the interface — that is the point.
- **Logo:** Use Navichain’s official logo file, uploaded as a source file in the project, for all placements in panels 6/7–12. No invented or stylised version.
