# — A navichain story —

# THE FULL AMOUNT, IN THREE STEPS
### Åsa and the customer who wanted to pay a little at a time

**Format:** 12 panels + cover concept. Illustrator's brief.

**Angle:** The twenty-seventh episode in the series about connected workflows at Björklunda Åkeri AB follows Åsa Renberg for the third time, now with a problem that is simple to describe but easy to lose track of in practice: a major customer wants to pay for one large delivery in three separate instalments, over several weeks, instead of all at once. The risk is not that the money will fail to arrive — it is that, in the confusion, someone loses track of exactly how much has already come in, and the invoice is either chased unnecessarily or, worse, forgotten entirely when the final part is delayed.

**Source:** Navichain — Invoices/Invoice Cycle (verified: invoice statuses are Draft, Issued, Partially Paid, Paid, Overdue, Cancelled. Users issue, email/download, record payments and can check a configured payment provider. Paid invoices cannot be cancelled. Payment recording is user-initiated, not automatic.) The story is deliberately restrained: navichain never records a payment automatically without Åsa entering it herself — the "Partially Paid" status is updated from what she actually records, not from any external guess.

**Important narrative instruction:** No IT department or finance consultant appears. Åsa records each part-payment herself, at the moment the money actually arrives — the system keeps track of the total, but she is always the one who confirms that it is correct.

## Cover concept

Åsa sits at her desk with three yellow Post-it notes attached to the edge of the screen, each with a handwritten date and amount — a makeshift way of keeping track of three part-payments. Beside them, on the tablet, a single clear invoice row instead shows the status "Partially Paid" and a bar that fills as each recorded payment is entered. Her gaze moves between the notes and the screen, as if she has just decided which one she trusts more. Title lettering in the series' established rust-red/petroleum-green combination.

---

## VISUAL STYLE (applies to all panels)

Warm 1950s aesthetic in Hergé/Tintin *ligne claire*: clean outlines of even thickness, flat colour fields without halftones or heavy hatching. Parchment/cream-coloured base with rust red, mustard yellow, petroleum green and warm gold. Captions hand-lettered, never digital sans-serif. Physical props (Post-it notes, an old cash book, a coffee mug) take priority over sharp UI mock-ups. Lightly grainy offset-print texture with patina, soft edge wear and foxing.

**Recurring motif:** the three yellow Post-it notes on the edge of the screen dominate the early panels — a makeshift, fragile way to remember something important. From the turning point onwards, they are taken down one by one as each part-payment is recorded properly instead.

**Logo rule:** navichain's official logo is completely absent from panels 1–6. It is introduced discreetly from panel 7 — as a small icon in the corner of the invoice view, a logo in the header of the payment confirmation, and finally on Åsa's coffee mug in the last panel. Never centred, never more than about 5% of the panel area, never advertisement-like. *The official logo is uploaded as a source file in the project — the illustrator must use that file at every specified placement, not recreate it by hand.*

**No IT support:** No support person or IT department appears anywhere. Åsa solves everything herself.

## PANEL 1 — An unusual request
**Visual:** Åsa reads an email from a major customer: "Can we pay the invoice in three parts over the coming weeks? Big project, tight budget right now." She frowns, thoughtful rather than worried.
**Text:** "Three part-payments. A reasonable request — but how would she keep track?"
**Status:** 🔴 Red (uncertainty about something new)

## PANEL 2 — The Post-it solution
**Visual:** Åsa writes three yellow notes by hand — "Part 1: SEK 40,000", "Part 2: SEK 40,000", "Part 3: SEK 45,000" — and sticks them to the edge of the screen.
**Text:** "Three notes. A makeshift memory that could just as easily blow away."
**Status:** 🔴 Red (fragile solution)

## PANEL 3 — The first payment arrives
**Visual:** A bank statement shows an incoming payment. Åsa compares the amount with her first Post-it note, unsure whether it matches exactly what was agreed.
**Text:** "Did the amount match the note? Probably. But 'probably' was not really good enough."
**Status:** 🔴 Red (uncertainty remains)

## PANEL 4 — A colleague asks
**Visual:** A colleague asks Åsa in passing: "That big invoice is paid now, by the way, isn't it?" Åsa hesitates, looks at the notes, unsure of an immediate answer.
**Text:** "She did not know exactly. Only the notes knew — and they were easy to misunderstand."
**Status:** 🟠 Orange (turning point — insight into the weakness)

## PANEL 5 — She opens the invoice instead
**Visual:** Åsa puts the notes aside and instead opens the invoice itself in navichain on her tablet, clearly seeing the status marked "Issued", not yet paid.
**Text:** "The invoice already knew exactly what was true. She only needed to ask it."
**Status:** 🟠 Orange (clarity)

## PANEL 6 — The first entry
**Visual:** Åsa records the first incoming payment manually in the system. The status immediately changes to "Partially Paid", with a clear bar showing how much of the total has come in.
**Text:** "One amount entered. The status changed immediately — Partially Paid, exactly that much."
**Status:** 🟢 Green (control, clarity)

## PANEL 7 — One note comes down
**Visual:** Åsa pulls the first Post-it note from the edge of the screen, smiling a little to herself. navichain's official logo is visible discreetly in the corner of the invoice view behind her.
**Text:** "One less note to rely on memory for."
**Status:** 🟢 Green (symbolic relief)

## PANEL 8 — The second payment, two weeks later
**Visual:** A new bank statement, a new incoming payment. Åsa records it just as easily as the first one; the status and the bar update again.
**Text:** "The second part arrived two weeks later. The same simple entry, the same clear answer."
**Status:** 🟢 Green (repeated simplicity)

## PANEL 9 — The colleague asks again
**Visual:** The same colleague as in panel 4 asks again. This time Åsa answers immediately, without hesitation, looking at the screen: "Two-thirds paid, final part expected next week."
**Text:** "This time the answer came straight away. No more 'probably'."
**Status:** 🟢 Green (confidence)

## PANEL 10 — The last note
**Visual:** The last Post-it note sits alone on the edge of the screen, almost looking out of place now beside the clear invoice view.
**Text:** "One note left. Mostly out of old habit."
**Status:** 🟢 Green (transition, nearly done)

## PANEL 11 — The invoice becomes fully paid
**Visual:** The third and final payment is recorded. The status changes from "Partially Paid" to "Paid", the bar fills completely, with a clear green indication.
**Text:** "The final part came in. The status changed colour by itself, not because of a guess."
**Status:** 🟢 Green (the matter is concluded)

## PANEL 12 — A clear desk
**Visual:** Åsa leans back with her coffee mug, where navichain's official logo is visible discreetly. The edge of the screen is completely clear — no Post-it notes left.
**Text:** "Three part-payments. One truth, all the way through."
**Status:** 🟢 Green (ending, clear point)

## Production notes for the illustrator

- **The Post-it notes** are the clearest recurring visual motif in this episode: three in panel 2, two in panels 7–8, one in panel 10, zero in panel 12. The number of notes must always be readable in the image without text.
- **Colour dramaturgy:** Red dominates panels 1–4 (uncertainty, makeshift solution), orange is the turning point (panels 4–5), petroleum green dominates panels 6–12.
- **The status colour "Partially Paid" → "Paid"** should be visualised as a clear, growing bar or gauge rather than text alone, to make the progression visually clear across the panels.
- **The customer is never shown** — only as email text and bank statements. The focus stays on Åsa and how she handles it.
- **Physical props to prioritise:** the yellow Post-it notes, the bank statements, the coffee mug with a discreet logo in the final panel.
