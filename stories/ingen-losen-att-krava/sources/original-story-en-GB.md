# — A navichain story —

# NO RANSOM TO DEMAND
### Anette and the night the neighbouring haulier was held hostage — but she wasn't

**Format:** 12 panels + cover concept. Illustrator brief.

**Angle:** A completely new angle for the series: cybersecurity and operational resilience during an actual attack — not government surveillance (as in Sofia's and Birgitta's stories about the CLOUD Act/FISA 702), but *criminal* extortion attacks (ransomware) against hauliers' own local IT systems. The subject is highly topical: the transport and logistics industry is one of the most attractive ransomware targets in Europe, because every hour of downtime costs money and businesses are therefore more likely to pay. The story does not dramatise "what hackers do" in detail, but the consequence — an operation that becomes completely locked — and the contrast with an architecture that cannot be locked in that way, because there is no local server to encrypt.

**Source/grounding (verified in the product documentation):** navichain is a cloud-based, browser-based platform — no local server, no local installation. Sign-in is handled via Identity Server with account security that includes 2FA/recovery. Data is EU-hosted (established in earlier stories about data sovereignty). The platform can be accessed from any device with a browser and an internet connection — computer, tablet, mobile. This is deliberately phrased cautiously: the story claims *architectural* resilience (nothing local to encrypt, access from any device, account protection with 2FA) — **not** a specific uptime figure, SLA or guarantee that is not verified in the source material.

**Important narrative instruction:** navichain never "rescues" the neighbouring haulier in the story — that company remains affected, which is the point (contrast, not a miracle). navichain also never presents any "IT support" or "support department" — Anette handles everything herself, because the platform is self-explanatory. No frightening image of hooded hackers appears in the foreground; the threat is portrayed through consequences (dark screens, locked server-room doors, paper notes) rather than through a visual "villain" figure.

## Cover concept

Anette is in her office at night, with a single lamp switched on. Through the window, the neighbouring haulier's building can be seen across the road — *all* its windows are lit, silhouettes are running back and forth, and a police car with its blue lights on is parked outside. Anette's own office, by contrast, is calm: she sits relaxed with a tablet in her hand, a cup of coffee beside her, and the screen glows warmly and steadily. Title typography in the series' established rust-red/petroleum-green combination. The contrast between the chaos outside the window and the calm inside the room *is* the cover.

## VISUAL STYLE (applies to all panels)

Warm 1950s aesthetic in Hergé/Tintin *ligne claire*: clean contour lines of even thickness, flat colour areas without halftones or heavy cross-hatching. Parchment/cream-coloured base with rust red, mustard yellow, petroleum green and warm gold. Captions hand-lettered, never digital sans-serif. Physical props (binders, coffee mugs, tablets with warm rounded design, a physical server-cabinet door) are prioritised over sharp modern UI mock-ups. Light grainy offset-print texture with patina, soft edge wear and foxing — like a well-thumbed 1950s magazine. The characters are warm and expressive, genuinely relieved and happy in the resolution panels.

**The story takes place in the present day** (laptops, mobiles, tablets, cloud services) but **is drawn throughout in the classic style above** — that is the recurring tension throughout the series: contemporary tools, timeless visual storytelling.

**Recurring motif:** as Solberg Åkeri uses navichain, the amount of paper in the office decreases dramatically. Early panels show overflowing binders, yellow notes, and a whiteboard covered with marker notes. Later panels show the same surfaces empty or used for other things — the office can breathe, because everything important is now safely in the cloud instead of in a locked binder or on a local hard drive.

**Logo rule:** navichain's official logo is completely absent from panels 1–6. It is introduced discreetly from panel 7 onwards — as an app icon on the tablet, a small logo in the corner of the sign-in view, a sticker on the spine of a binder that now stands unused, and finally on Anette's coffee mug in the last panel. Never centred, never more than approximately 5% of the panel area, never advertising-like. *The official logo is uploaded as a source file in the project — the illustrator must use that file at every specified placement, not recreate it by hand.*

**No IT support:** No support person, IT department or "call IT" appears anywhere. Anette handles everything herself — sign-in, access, restoring the workflow — which is a deliberate point: the platform is self-explanatory.

## PANEL 1 — The alarm across the road
**Visual:** Evening. Anette is about to go home, car keys in hand, when the phone rings. Through the office window she sees the lights in the neighbouring haulier's building flicker strangely — as if the power is stuttering.
**Caption:** "The call came at 22:14. 'We can't get into anything. Everything is locked.'"
**Status:** 🔴 Red (concern, distant threat)

## PANEL 2 — The screens that went dark
**Visual:** Anette walks across the road. Through the neighbouring haulier's windows, employees can be seen staring at black screens. A message is visible reversed in the glass: a ransom amount, a deadline, a cryptocurrency address.
**Caption:** "Their entire system — bookings, invoices, driving logs — locked behind a code none of them has."
**Status:** 🔴 Red (crisis at the neighbour)

## PANEL 3 — The one local server
**Visual:** Through a partly open door, the neighbouring haulier's server cabinet can be seen: a single physical box with blinking red lights, now silent and dark. An IT consultant (contracted, not employed — the point is that they *have to* call someone from outside) leans over it with a torch.
**Caption:** "Everything they had built sat in one box, in one room, at one address."
**Status:** 🔴 Red (vulnerability made visible)

## PANEL 4 — Anette goes back
**Visual:** Anette walks back across the road towards her own dark office. She stops, hesitates, takes out her phone and looks at it — not in panic, but thoughtfully.
**Caption:** "She walked back across the road and wondered: what would have happened if it had been us?"
**Status:** 🟠 Orange (turning point approaching — reflection, not action yet)

## PANEL 5 — Remembering how it used to be
**Visual:** Brief flashback (softly framed, like a memory): Anette's own office a year earlier — a physical server in a cabinet behind her desk, just like the neighbour's, and a binder marked "BACKUP — DONE EVERY FRIDAY" with dust on it.
**Caption:** "A year earlier, she had had exactly the same box. And exactly the same binder."
**Status:** 🔴 Red (retrospective problem)

## PANEL 6 — The move to the cloud
**Visual:** The same memory continues: Anette a year earlier, signing in to navichain for the first time on an ordinary laptop. No server is visible in the background any longer — just a desk, a coffee cup, a calm screen.
**Caption:** "Then everything moved to navichain. No box left to steal. Nothing left to encrypt."
**Status:** 🟠 Orange (the turning point is explained)

## PANEL 7 — Back in the present: she simply signs in
**Visual:** Present day again. Anette sits in her own calm office and opens navichain on her tablet. A discreet sign-in window asks for a password and a code from her mobile — a clear but undramatic 2FA box. navichain's official logo is visible discreetly in the corner of the sign-in view.
**Caption:** "She simply signed in. Password, a code from the mobile — as usual."
**Status:** 🟢 Green (control, self-service)

## PANEL 8 — No door to break through
**Visual:** Split panel: on the left, the neighbour's empty, dark server room with pry marks on the door; on the right, Anette's screen showing her fleet, bookings and invoices — completely normal, completely accessible, as though nothing had happened.
**Caption:** "No local box to break into. Nothing to lock."
**Status:** 🟢 Green (architectural security)

## PANEL 9 — Any device
**Visual:** Anette accidentally leaves her laptop at home the following morning, realises it in the car, shrugs and signs in on her mobile instead while stopped at a red light (stationary, safe). The same interface, the same data, no difference.
**Caption:** "Left the computer at home. It didn't matter — everything was still there on the mobile too."
**Status:** 🟢 Green (flexibility, no single point of failure)

## PANEL 10 — Robban in the cab, unaware of the drama
**Visual:** Robban Ek calmly drives his usual route, checking the next stop in the driver app. He does not even know that the neighbouring haulier has had a crisis — for him, it is a completely ordinary day.
**Caption:** "Robban didn't even know anything had happened. His day looked like every other day."
**Status:** 🟢 Green (continuity, uninterrupted operation)

## PANEL 11 — Coffee and a binder that has become redundant
**Visual:** Anette's office, the same angle as in the memory image (panel 5) — but now the server cabinet is gone, replaced by a bookcase. The binder marked "BACKUP" is still on a shelf, but is now used to support a potted geranium. navichain's official logo is visible discreetly on Anette's coffee mug.
**Caption:** "The binder is still there. It now holds up a geranium."
**Status:** 🟢 Green (warm, relaxed resolution)

## PANEL 12 — The neighbour asks
**Visual:** A few days later. The owner of the neighbouring haulier — tired, with dark circles under his eyes after days of recovery work — stands in Anette's doorway and asks directly: "How did you manage?" Anette smiles and turns her tablet towards him, simply showing him the sign-in view.
**Caption:** "'How did you manage?' Anette simply turned the tablet around and showed him the answer."
**Status:** 🟢 Green (ending, clear point)

## Production notes for the illustrator

- **Colour dramaturgy:** Red dominates panels 1–5 (the neighbour's crisis + the memory of the old system), orange is the turning point (panels 4/6), petroleum green dominates panels 7–12.
- **Contrast principle:** The neighbouring haulier's crisis must never feel "solved" by navichain in the image — they remain in chaos throughout the story. That is the point: the difference is structural, not a miraculous intervention.
- **No frightening image of hackers:** The threat is portrayed through consequences (dark screen, pry mark, tired owner) — never through a hooded figure at a keyboard. Keep the tone measured, not thriller-like.
- **The 2FA box in panel 7** should be drawn simply and undramatically — one password field and a short code box, no technical icons or loud warning triangles.
- **Continuity:** Robban Ek (panel 10) is the same recurring driver as in earlier stories in the series — the same physical attributes must be maintained.
- **Physical props to prioritise:** the abandoned binder marked "BACKUP", the empty server-cabinet space (now a bookcase), the coffee mug with the discreet logo in the final panel.
