# — A Navichain story —

# THE NOTE THAT FINALLY HAS AN OWNER

### Erik and the board that holds the work together across departments

**Angle:** The eighth episode in the series about connected workflows ties together several earlier threads in the series — workshop, warehouse, traffic management — by showing what happens *after* a deviation has already been detected. A single event (for example, a failed inspection) often triggers several follow-up tasks for several different people. This story is distinct because it is not about solving the deviation itself, but about making sure none of the small follow-up tasks fall between the cracks when responsibility passes from one department to another.

**Source:** Navichain — Tasks Board (verified: a full editable and filterable kanban board plus a simpler quick-triage view. Fields: title/description/tags/status/priority/type/one assignee/due date, checklist and comments. Columns are configurable per organisation. Users drag status or edit it explicitly. A failed inspection or compliance deviation can create tasks automatically.)

## Cover concept

Erik stands by a noticeboard full of loose notes in different colours, some already yellowing, some with smudged notes no one remembers the meaning of anymore. Beside him, on a screen, a clear digital board with named columns begins to take shape — the same kind of information, but organised. Title lettering in the same rust-red/petroleum-green combination as the other covers in the series.

## PANEL 1 — The deviation is fixed. But is it finished?

**Visual:** Erik reads a message saying that a vehicle recently failed an inspection — the actual fault has already been repaired in the workshop, but he knows several things remain: a part to rebook, a customer to inform, a paper to archive. **Text:** "The fault is fixed. But 'fixed' is rarely the whole truth." **Status:** 🔴 Red — several invisible follow-up tasks remain

## PANEL 2 — Three departments, three memories

**Visual:** Erik moves between Kenneth in the workshop, Frida in the warehouse and Sara in traffic management, reminding each of them verbally what they need to do — there is no written, shared list anywhere. **Text:** "Three reminders, spoken, in three different places. None of them written down in the same place." **Status:** 🔴 Red — responsibility without a shared surface

## PANEL 3 — The note that gets forgotten

**Visual:** A yellow note saying "Call customer re delay" falls behind Sara's screen and is not discovered until days later. No one knew that particular task was still unresolved. **Text:** "A note behind a screen. No one knew it was there — until it was already too late." **Status:** 🔴 Red — a forgotten task, invisible to everyone else

## PANEL 4 — The customer's question no one could answer

**Visual:** The customer calls and asks why they never heard anything about the delay. Erik stands with the phone, not quite sure what to answer — there is no complete picture of what has actually been done. **Text:** "'You didn't say anything.' And Erik cannot even say for certain whether that is true." **Status:** 🔴 Red — no shared truth about what is complete

## PANEL 5 — The turning point: one board, not three memories

**Visual:** Erik opens Navichain's task board. The failed inspection has already created a task automatically — with a title, description and an empty checklist ready to be filled in. **Text:** "The task was already there. No one needed to remember to create it." **Status:** 🟠 Orange — follow-up starts on its own

## PANEL 6 — One owner per task

**Visual:** Erik breaks the case down into three clear subtasks, each with exactly one assignee and a due date: Kenneth (part order), Frida (receipt), Sara (customer contact) — no shared or unclear responsibility remains. **Text:** "Three tasks, three names. No more 'someone should do it'." **Status:** 🟠 Orange — responsibility named, not assumed

## PANEL 7 — Kenneth sees his part straight away

**Visual:** Kenneth opens his own view of the board — only what concerns him is visible, clearly placed in the "To do" column. A discreet sticker with Navichain's official logo appears in the corner of the board. **Text:** "He does not need to look for his task. It is already where he looks first." **Status:** 🟢 Green — the right task, visible to the right person

## PANEL 8 — Frida ticks it off

**Visual:** Frida receives the part, ticks off her item on the checklist and drags the card to the "Done" column — a small, concrete conclusion visible to everyone else on the board immediately. **Text:** "A tick in a box. But now everyone else knows it too, straight away." **Status:** 🟢 Green — progress visible to the whole team, in real time

## PANEL 9 — Sara calls the customer — and writes it down

**Visual:** Sara calls the customer with news about the delay, then leaves a short comment directly on the task card: "Customer informed 09:14." No note that can fall away behind a screen. **Text:** "The call is made. And this time the proof stays, in the right place." **Status:** 🟢 Green — follow-up documented where it belongs

## PANEL 10 — Erik's board, at a glance

**Visual:** Erik sees his whole area of responsibility in one view — several cases, each with its subtasks, clearly distributed across columns. No individual note to keep track of in his head anymore. **Text:** "No more deviations to remember. Just one board to look at." **Status:** 🟢 Green — overview instead of memory training

## PANEL 11 — The next time the customer calls

**Visual:** A new customer calls about another case. Erik answers immediately from the board: exactly what has been done, what remains, and who is doing it. No hesitant answer this time. **Text:** "'You didn't say anything' is no longer heard. Now the answer is already there, before the question is even asked." **Status:** 🟢 Green — trust built on visibility, not luck

## PANEL 12 — The noticeboard, finally empty

**Visual:** Erik leans against the old noticeboard from the cover — now completely empty, the notes gone. He holds a coffee mug with Navichain's official logo, with the digital board visible on the screen beside him. **Text:** "No task disappears behind a screen anymore. Each one finally has an owner." **Status:** 🟢 Green — the chain complete, from loose notes to shared responsibility

**Post-it on Erik's desk (teaser):** *"Next: the document everyone had to read — and the proof that they actually did."*

## Continuity note

The eighth episode in the series about connected workflows at Björklunda Åkeri AB, and the episode that deliberately weaves together threads from several earlier stories: Kenneth's workshop, Frida's warehouse and Sara's traffic management now meet around one and the same task board. Erik Malm is introduced as a new lead character specifically chosen to represent the coordinating role that earlier episodes only hinted at through individual departments' perspectives — the person who makes sure the whole thing actually gets finished, not just each individual part.
