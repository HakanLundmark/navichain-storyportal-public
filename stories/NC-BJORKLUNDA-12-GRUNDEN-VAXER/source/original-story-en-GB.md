# — A Navichain story —

# THE FOUNDATION THAT GROWS WITH THE COMPANY

### Oskar and the structure that inherits the right permissions by itself

**Angle:** The twelfth episode in the series about connected workflows links back to the previous episode’s theme of controlled permissions, but turns its attention to growth rather than exceptions: what happens when a company like Björklunda opens in a new location? The task is unique because it requires the right permissions to be in place from day one — neither too much nor too little, and without rebuilding them from scratch every time.

**Source:** Navichain — Settings/Departments (verified: departments inherit permission unions from their parent departments in the organisational structure.)

---

## Cast

| Character | Role | Company |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Oskar Lind** *(lead, second time)* | Administrator | Björklunda Åkeri AB |

Oskar has previously created Viktor’s API key in the series and arranged the team’s notifications. This time his task is bigger: to give an entirely new office the right conditions from its first day, without rebuilding what already works at head office.

## Cover concept

Oskar at his desk, with a simple organisational structure drawn on the screen: a main node labelled “Björklunda Åkeri AB”, with a branch growing down towards a new, still empty node labelled “Örebro terminal”. The line between them glows faintly, as though the permissions are already beginning to flow downwards. Title typography in the same rust-red/petroleum-green combination as the other covers in the series.

## PANEL 1 — The announcement

**Visual:** Oskar reads a message from management: Björklunda is opening a new terminal in Örebro in one week. He looks at the date, then at his own already full to-do list. **Text:** “A new location. One week to go. And everything has to be right from the very first day.” **Status:** 🔴 Red — little time, a lot to set up

## PANEL 2 — Building it all again from scratch

**Visual:** Oskar starts writing a list of roles and permissions for the Örebro office, role by role, just as he once built up head office — as though none of the work already done could be reused. **Text:** “The same work, one more time, from zero. As though head office had never existed.” **Status:** 🔴 Red — duplicate work for the same thing

## PANEL 3 — The shortcut that almost became a mistake

**Visual:** Under time pressure, Oskar considers giving the first Örebro account broad administrator permissions “just to get started” — then stops, unsure whether it is a good idea. **Text:** “Broad access solves the problem quickly. It also solves the wrong problem.” **Status:** 🔴 Red — a shortcut that risks becoming a permanent gap

## PANEL 4 — The clock is ticking towards opening day

**Visual:** A calendar on the wall counts down the days until the Örebro terminal opens. Oskar still has no finished solution, only an incomplete list and a broad permission he does not feel comfortable with. **Text:** “Four days left. Still no good solution — only a bad shortcut.” **Status:** 🔴 Red — time pressure without a sustainable way forward

## PANEL 5 — The turning point: the structure that already exists

**Visual:** Oskar opens the department structure in Settings and creates a new department, “Örebro terminal”, directly below the main organisation — instead of building something separate from scratch. **Text:** “Not a new system. Just a new branch on the same tree.” **Status:** 🟠 Orange — structure instead of starting over

## PANEL 6 — The permissions flow downwards

**Visual:** Oskar sees how the new department automatically inherits the permission unions from the main organisation above it — the same basic rights that already apply at head office, without him entering them all over again. **Text:** “The right permissions were already higher up. Now Örebro inherits them — without him having to ask for them twice.” **Status:** 🟠 Orange — inheritance, not a restart

## PANEL 7 — The first accounts are in place

**Visual:** Oskar adds the new employees in Örebro under the new department. Each one immediately gets the right baseline of permissions — nothing broader than necessary, and nothing missing either. A discreet sticker with Navichain’s official logo is visible in the corner of the structure view. **Text:** “Five new accounts. Five correct starting points — in minutes, not days.” **Status:** 🟢 Green — the right access from the first login

## PANEL 8 — The shortcut that never happened

**Visual:** Oskar removes the broad, temporary administrator permission he considered in panel 3 — it is no longer needed, because the structure solved the same problem the right way. **Text:** “The bad shortcut never remained. The good solution arrived in time instead.” **Status:** 🟢 Green — the risk that never took hold

## PANEL 9 — One change, once

**Visual:** A policy update is made at main-organisation level — a new rule concerning invoicing permissions. Oskar sees it automatically appear further down in the Örebro department structure too, without having to repeat the change there. **Text:** “One change at the top. The same change, already in place at the bottom.” **Status:** 🟢 Green — one update, not one per location

## PANEL 10 — One exception, exactly where it is needed

**Visual:** Örebro’s local manager asks for one extra permission for a specific employee. Oskar adds it precisely at that department — without touching the rest of the organisation’s permissions at all. **Text:** “One person’s need. One department’s adjustment. Nothing else affected.” **Status:** 🟢 Green — precision instead of broad exceptions

## PANEL 11 — The structure, in one overview

**Visual:** Oskar looks at the entire organisation tree on screen — head office and the Örebro terminal, clearly branched and easy to understand and review at a glance. **Text:** “The whole company, in a single picture. No doubt about who has what, or why.” **Status:** 🟢 Green — overview instead of scattered lists

## PANEL 12 — Opening day

**Visual:** Oskar looks at a photo that has just arrived from the Örebro terminal’s first day — colleagues at their new screens, already up and running. A coffee mug with Navichain’s official logo in his hand; the calendar behind him shows that the opening happened exactly on time. **Text:** “A new location, ready from day one. The same foundation, one branch further.” **Status:** 🟢 Green — the chain complete, from restart to inheritance

**Post-it on Oskar’s desk (teaser):** *“Next: the booking that came in by itself from the customer — and stayed for one extra look before moving on.”*

## Continuity note

The twelfth episode in the series about connected workflows at Björklunda Åkeri AB, and Oskar Lind’s second lead role in the series — a deliberate deepening of an already established administrator character rather than yet another new persona. The episode builds on the previous episode’s theme of controlled permissions (Görel Ask’s locked bookings), but turns it towards growth: the same principle of never giving away more access than necessary, applied to a company that is expanding rather than to a single exception.

**Next in the series:** The booking that came in by itself, and still paused for an extra look — how a customer-portal booking can be flagged for staff review before it moves on, so that self-service never has to mean that nobody is keeping an eye on things any more. An angle not yet used in the series.

## Accepted preflight corrections recorded in this chat

The following corrections were made before visual production and are part of the canonical validation record:

1. Panel 6 wording was tightened to describe the inherited permission baseline as a union inherited from ancestor nodes, not a copy of all head-office user permissions.
2. Panel 7 removed the unsupported time-effect claim “på minuter, inte dagar” and retained the concept of a shared inherited baseline without implying all effective permissions come only from the department.
3. Panel 8 was corrected so the broad administrator permission was never actually saved; the shortcut was only considered, then discarded.
4. Panel 10 was corrected from a department-wide exception to an individual complementary role/permission for the specific user, leaving the department baseline unchanged.
5. Panel 11 was corrected from a claim of complete per-user permission certainty to the safer claim that the structure and inheritance path can be followed in one overview.

Product Truth and Reality Validation were reported PASS before production. The visual production later encountered release-blocking asset drift in panel_09 and panel_10, and the CTA master became unavailable as a directly exportable source file in the current session.
