# — A Navichain story —

# THE CHAIN NO ONE SEES — UNTIL IT BREAKS

### Kenneth and the link between purchasing, stock and the workshop

**Angle:** Unlike earlier episodes in the series, each of which showed a single function (tracking, e-CMR, HaaS, cabotage, customer portal...), this episode shows what happens when several modules work as **one single chain**: a deviation in the workshop automatically triggers the correct stock balance, the correct purchase order and the correct delivery — without any person having to hold it all together manually. The task itself is unique: it exists only at the intersection of maintenance, stock and purchasing, and can therefore only be solved by a platform that unites all three.

**Source:** Navichain — Purchase Orders, Stock Levels, Stock Movements, Warehouses, Maintenance/Work Orders (verified platform flows: draft PO → send/confirm → receive → automatically updated stock balance → linked to work order).

## PANEL 1 — The alert during the morning check

**Visual:** Robban Ek carries out his morning inspection of the truck in the yard. He discovers a worn brake pad and reports the deviation on his mobile. Björklunda Åkeri's sign can be seen in the background in the dawn light. **Text:** "A deviation during the morning inspection. The truck must not go out until it has been fixed." **Status:** 🔴 Red — critical deviation, vehicle blocked

## PANEL 2 — The work order that is not enough on its own

**Visual:** Kenneth in the workshop, a sheet of paper with a handwritten work order in front of him. He scratches his head and looks towards a shelf of spare parts where an empty space can be seen — exactly the type of brake pad that is missing. **Text:** "A work order says what is wrong. It says nothing about what is on the shelf." **Status:** 🔴 Red — stock balance unknown

## PANEL 3 — The old round of phone calls

**Visual:** Kenneth with a telephone receiver in his hand, a list of three suppliers' numbers on a notepad, two already crossed out. Behind him, a binder labelled "PURCHASING" lies open and untouched. **Text:** "Three calls, three guesses about what they actually have in stock — and what we have already ordered." **Status:** 🔴 Red — purchasing and stock do not talk to each other

## PANEL 4 — The risk no one sees

**Visual:** A calendar on the wall with today's date circled in red. Next to it, a note: "Robban → Gothenburg 14:00". Kenneth looks anxiously between the clock and the empty shelf space. **Text:** "If the part is not here before 14:00, both the truck and the delivery come to a standstill." **Status:** 🔴 Red — delivery threatened by a single component

## PANEL 5 — The turning point: one screen, the whole picture

**Visual:** Kenneth opens Navichain on his tablet. The work order for Robban's truck is already open — and directly beneath it the item's stock balance is shown: "In stock: 0 · On the way: 0 · Reserved: 0". No guessing left. **Text:** "The same work order. But now with the exact balance — not an estimate." **Status:** 🟠 Orange — real balance, not a guess

## PANEL 6 — The purchase order is born from the deviation

**Visual:** Kenneth presses a button directly in the item view: "Create purchase order". A draft is filled in automatically — the correct supplier, the correct item number, the correct warehouse — linked to Robban's work order with the same case number. **Text:** "The purchase order is created where the shortage was discovered — not in a separate binder, much later." **Status:** 🟠 Orange — draft ready in under a minute

## PANEL 7 — The confirmation, without a phone call

**Visual:** Kenneth sends the purchase order with one button press. A confirmation appears on the screen: quantity confirmed, promised delivery date. A discreet sticker with Navichain's official logo is visible in the corner of the screen, as an app icon. **Text:** "Confirmed quantity. Promised date. No more 'I'll get back to you'." **Status:** 🟢 Green — order confirmed

## PANEL 8 — Arrival at the warehouse

**Visual:** Ingela receives the parcel at the warehouse, scanning the barcode with a handheld device. A receipt is generated directly on the screen. Navichain's official logo is shown discreetly on the casing of the handheld device. **Text:** "One scan. The stock balance updates in the same second — automatically." **Status:** 🟢 Green — receipt registered

## PANEL 9 — The part finds its place by itself

**Visual:** Split image: on the left, the item is booked into the correct shelf/bin in the warehouse structure; on the right, the work order updates automatically — the status changes from "Waiting for part" to "Ready to repair". **Text:** "The same system that created the order also sees that it has been resolved." **Status:** 🟢 Green — warehouse and workshop synchronised

## PANEL 10 — Ingela installs, Kenneth signs off

**Visual:** Ingela installs the new brake pad beneath Robban's truck. Kenneth stands beside her with the tablet and gives the final approval on the work order — the truck is marked as cleared for traffic. **Text:** "One name, one approval. No truck leaves the yard on a guess." **Status:** 🟢 Green — vehicle cleared

## PANEL 11 — The trail that never lies

**Visual:** Kenneth at his computer, a simple log open: deviation → work order → purchase order → receipt → installation, all with timestamps in a single line. A coffee mug with Navichain's official logo in the foreground. **Text:** "From broken part to rolling truck — the whole chain can be followed afterwards, without asking anyone." **Status:** 🟢 Green — full traceability, automatically

## PANEL 12 — 13:20

**Visual:** Robban Ek reverses the truck out of the workshop, giving Kenneth a wave through the window. The clock on the wall shows 13:20 — 40 minutes before departure. Kenneth leans back with his coffee mug, the tablet resting on the table with three green ticks visible. **Text:** "No round of phone calls. No guessing. Just a chain that held itself together." **Status:** 🟢 Green — delivery saved

## Continuity note

This is the first episode in the series that deliberately shows **three modules in a single chain** rather than an isolated function — a clear escalation from earlier single-function stories (tracking, cabotage, HaaS, customer portal). Kenneth Broman is a new main character chosen specifically to represent the real, composite role between workshop, warehouse and purchasing that often lacks a distinct platform function of its own to represent it — making him a natural bearer of precisely this holistic perspective. Robban Ek continues his role as the series' connecting figure, this time in a supporting role rather than the lead.

**Next in the series:** The customer's own goods in the warehouse — how Navichain keeps customer-owned goods physically and digitally separate from its own stock balance, without them ever being mixed up or incorrectly valued as the company's own.
