## Faithful en-GB translation of the canonical Narrative Master

**Format:** 12 panels + cover concept. Illustrator brief.

**Angle:** A new angle for the series: the driver shortage — not as an abstract industry problem, but as concrete risk behaviour around one newly hired driver. In an industry where drivers are in demand and change employers in days, not months, the most dangerous moment is not the job interview — it is the time *between* the yes and the first drive. Every day spent on paper forms, waiting for handwritten certificates and manual registration is a day when a competitor can call and offer the same thing faster. This clearly differs from earlier stories about the driver app (Karin/driving-time alert, Gunilla/communication, Johan/distraction protection) — those were about the driver *during* the drive. This one is about the time *before* the driver has even sat in the driver's seat, and about the company's ability to retain a candidate it has already won.

**Source/grounding:** Driver profiles in Navichain collect contact/language, vehicle/customer links, licence/employment/tachograph details, expiry dates (driving licence, tachograph, DGR, GDP, YKB, ADR authorisation with type/expiry), shift information and documents. A separate driver account is created through "Send account invite on save" — which sends either a password email or a phone-based SMS code. The story does not claim that the entire employment process (background check, contract, physical equipment) happens digitally in minutes; it is specifically the account activation and basic data that can be handled quickly.

**Narrative requirement:** Navichain never creates the driver automatically or makes the hiring decision — Mia always makes the active choice to add the driver and send the invitation. No IT department or support is shown in the story; Mia handles the entire flow herself. The competitor's offer is portrayed indirectly and anonymously — never as a named rival operator.
