# — A navichain story —

# TEN SECONDS, NOT TEN FIELDS
### Petra, Görel and the Monday order that stopped repeating itself

**Format:** 12 panels + cover concept. Illustrator brief.

**Angle:** The twenty-sixth episode in the series about connected workflows at Björklunda Åkeri AB leaves the internal office for the first time in a while to show the customer side of the same story — a recurring customer sending essentially the same kind of goods, to the same consignee, every Monday. The story connects two roles on opposite sides of the same booking: the customer who no longer has to fill in the same details every week, and Björklunda’s own staff who still deliberately review what comes in.

**Source:** Navichain — Bookings/Customer Portal (verified: bookings support create, edit, clone, delete. The Customer Portal is a separate self-service app where customers can create, clone, track and view CMR/invoice — but can only edit records with status "New". Portal-created orders are automatically flagged "Requires Review" for Björklunda’s staff.) The story is deliberately restrained: a cloned booking does **not** automatically create a confirmed delivery — it creates a new draft that still has to be reviewed and approved by Björklunda, just like all portal orders.

**Important narrative rule:** navichain never confirms the customer’s order automatically — Petra clones and submits it, but it is always Görel on Björklunda’s side who actually reviews and approves it before anything is planned. No IT department appears on either side. Petra never needs to call Björklunda for help with the portal.

## Cover concept

Split image: on the left, Petra one week ago, bent over the keyboard and an old booking form, in the middle of entering exactly the same consignee address, the same number of pallets and the same type of goods as the week before — visibly frustrated. On the right, in the present, Petra leaning back with a coffee cup in her hand while, with a single touch on the tablet, she has just cloned the same order in a few seconds. Title typography in the series’ established rust-red/petroleum-green combination.

## VISUAL STYLE (applies to all panels)

Warm 1950s aesthetic in Hergé/Tintin *ligne claire*: clean contour lines of even thickness, flat colour fields without halftones or heavy hatching. Parchment/cream background with rust red, mustard yellow, petroleum green and warm gold. Captions hand-lettered, never digital sans-serif. Physical props (a calendar with Mondays circled, a coffee cup, a clipboard) are prioritised over sharp UI mock-ups. Lightly grainy offset-print texture with patina, soft edge wear and foxing.

**Recurring motif:** a wall calendar with every Monday circled in red is visible at Petra’s in the early panels — a sign of a recurring, time-consuming ritual. From the turning point onwards, the calendar remains, but the circles are symbolically replaced by small, satisfied tick marks.

**Logo rule:** navichain’s official logo is completely absent from panels 1–6. It is introduced discreetly from panel 7 — as a small icon in the corner of the portal view, a logo in the header of the confirmation email, and finally on Görel’s coffee cup in the last panel. Never centred, never more than about 5% of the panel area, never advertisement-like. *The official logo is uploaded as a source file in the project — the illustrator must use that file at every specified placement, not recreate it by hand.*

**No IT support:** No support person or IT department appears anywhere, at either Petra’s or Görel’s.

## PANEL 1 — Monday, again
**Visual:** Petra is sitting at her computer, the wall calendar behind her with Mondays circled in red. She gives a small sigh and opens a booking form.
**Text:** "Monday. Same consignee, same number of pallets, same form to fill in from the beginning."
**Status:** 🔴 Red (repetitive frustration)

## PANEL 2 — Field by field
**Visual:** Close-up of the screen: Petra enters the address, contact person, type of goods, number of pallets, weight — one field at a time, exactly the same details as last week.
**Text:** "Ten fields. The same ten fields as last Monday. And the Monday before that."
**Status:** 🔴 Red (wasted time)

## PANEL 3 — A typo
**Visual:** Petra accidentally enters the wrong postcode, does not notice until she is almost about to save, sighs and corrects it.
**Text:** "Sometimes a typo slipped in — simply because everything was entered manually every time."
**Status:** 🔴 Red (error-prone)

## PANEL 4 — A colleague mentions clone
**Visual:** One of Petra’s colleagues looks over her shoulder and says: "Why are you entering it all again? Just clone last week’s." Petra looks up, amused but sceptical.
**Text:** "'Just clone it.' She had seen the button but never pressed it."
**Status:** 🟠 Orange (turning point)

## PANEL 5 — Petra finds the previous order
**Visual:** Petra logs in to the Customer Portal on her tablet and finds last Monday’s order in the list of previous orders.
**Text:** "There it was. Exactly the order she would need again."
**Status:** 🟠 Orange (recognition)

## PANEL 6 — One tap
**Visual:** Petra taps "Clone" next to the previous order. A new draft opens immediately, already filled in with the same consignee, goods and number of pallets.
**Text:** "One tap. Everything already there — she only had to confirm that it was correct."
**Status:** 🟢 Green (efficiency)

## PANEL 7 — She adjusts one detail
**Visual:** Petra changes only the number of pallets from four to six, because this week’s delivery is a little larger. navichain’s official logo is visible discreetly in the corner of the portal view.
**Text:** "Only what was actually different this week."
**Status:** 🟢 Green (precision, not blind copying)

## PANEL 8 — Submitted, not confirmed
**Visual:** Petra submits the order. A message shows "Submitted — awaiting review", not an immediate confirmation.
**Text:** "Submitted. Not finished yet — and she knew that."
**Status:** 🟢 Green (the right expectation, no overpromise)

## PANEL 9 — Görel sees it
**Visual:** At Björklunda, Görel Ask sees the incoming portal order flagged "Requires Review" in her own interface.
**Text:** "At Björklunda it appeared, clearly marked: waiting for a pair of eyes."
**Status:** 🟢 Green (human control retained)

## PANEL 10 — Görel reviews it
**Visual:** Görel quickly goes through the details — everything matches the usual pattern, only the number of pallets is different — and approves the order.
**Text:** "Thirty seconds of checking. Then it was a real booking."
**Status:** 🟢 Green (fast but deliberate review)

## PANEL 11 — The calendar with ticks
**Visual:** Back at Petra’s: the wall calendar is visible again, but the earlier red circles around Mondays have been replaced by small, satisfied tick marks.
**Text:** "Mondays stopped feeling like a task and became just a tick on the calendar."
**Status:** 🟢 Green (symbolic conclusion)

## PANEL 12 — Next Monday, already done
**Visual:** One week later. Petra sits with a coffee cup, cloning the next order in ten seconds while Görel, at Björklunda, drinks her own coffee from a cup with navichain’s official logo discreetly visible, already finished with the review.
**Text:** "Ten seconds to order. Thirty to review. Monday was already over before it had even had time to feel like a Monday."
**Status:** 🟢 Green (conclusion, clear point)

## Continuity note

The twenty-sixth episode in the series about connected workflows at Björklunda Åkeri AB is the first in a while to deliberately show both sides of the same booking at the same time — the customer’s and Björklunda’s. Görel Ask returns in a short but important role that shows that customer self-service never means Björklunda loses control: every portal order is still reviewed by a person before it becomes real.

**Next in the series:** An angle not yet used — how Björklunda handles a customer who wants to pay in several instalments for one and the same large delivery, without the invoicing flow becoming messy or anyone losing track of what has already been paid.

## Production notes for the illustrator

- **The wall calendar** is a clear recurring motif: red circles in panel 1, green ticks in panels 11–12 — the same wall, the same calendar, different markings.
- **The split composition on the cover** should clearly contrast "one week ago" (frustrated, manual) with "now" (relaxed, one click).
- **Petra and Görel never appear together in the same image** — they exist in parallel scenes cut between one another, reinforcing that each does her part without needing to contact the other directly.
- **Panels 8–9 are the story’s quiet core:** "Submitted, not confirmed" and "Requires Review" must be drawn clearly to avoid giving the impression that navichain would approve the order automatically.
- **Physical props to prioritise:** the wall calendar at Petra’s, Görel’s clipboard, the coffee cups with the discreet logo in the last panel.
