# THE RIGHT KEY, NOT ALL THE KEYS
### Oskar and the dispatcher who got exactly what she needed — and nothing else

**Angle:** The twenty-first episode in the series about connected workflows at Björklunda Åkeri AB links back to Oskar's two previous leading roles — the API key for Viktor (episode 3) and the department structure for the Örebro office (episode 12) — but turns its attention to the most common admin task of all: giving a single new employee access to the system. The task looks deceptively simple and is dangerous in practice, because the usual shortcut — copying a colleague's permissions "just to be safe" — almost always gives either too little or far too much access.

**Source:** Navichain — Users & Roles (verified: users have a fixed login email, active status, one or more roles and an optional driver profile. Roles group permissions/claims — documented claims include Transport, Bookings, Runs, Inspections, BookingLockOverride, DriverContract, Invoice and Settings. The Invoice claim hides financial values in web/mobile for users who do not have it. Settings grants administration rights/Documents. BookingLockOverride requires a stated reason and is logged. Roles cannot be removed while they are assigned or if they are the last remaining role.)

**Important narrative instruction:** Navichain never creates a role or assigns permissions automatically without Oskar actively choosing it. The system clearly shows what each role actually contains — Oskar chooses an existing, already defined role and links it to the new account; he does not build permissions from scratch, but neither does he "guess" by copying another person's account. No IT department appears. No SA role (the reserved system-administrator role) is handed out unnecessarily.

Oskar has previously given Viktor an API key and built the permission structure for the new Örebro office. This time the task is smaller and more personal: one new colleague, one first day, and the risk of either locking her out of what she needs or — worse — giving her access to things she should never see, such as other people's salary information or invoice values.

## Cover concept

Oskar sits at his desk with two screen images side by side in his mind: on the left, an old imagined shortcut — an account created simply by ticking the same boxes as an experienced colleague, with a warning triangle over some of them. On the right, a clean, clear role list where he instead selects a ready-made role labelled "Dispatcher" and links it to Elin's new account with a single click. Title typography in the series' established rust-red/petroleum-green combination.

## VISUAL STYLE (applies to all panels)

Warm 1950s aesthetic in Hergé/Tintin *ligne claire*: clean contour lines of even thickness, flat colour fields without halftones or heavy hatching. Parchment/cream-coloured base with rust red, mustard yellow, petroleum green and warm gold. Captions hand-lettered, never digital sans-serif. Physical props (a welcome card on Elin's desk, a coffee cup, a clipboard) are prioritised over sharp UI mock-ups. Lightly grainy offset-print texture with patina, soft edge wear and foxing.

**Recurring motif:** in the early panels, an old printed checklist sits on Oskar's desk — "NEW ACCOUNT: TICK THE SAME AS THE COLLEAGUE" — full of handwritten ticks and uncertain question marks. From the turning point onwards, the list is put away, and the role choice instead happens through a few clear, ready-made options on screen.

**Logo rule:** Navichain's official logo is completely absent in panels 1–6. It is introduced discreetly from panel 7 — as a small icon in the corner of the role list, a logo on Oskar's tablet, and finally on his coffee mug in the last panel. Never centred, never more than about 5% of the panel area, never advertisement-like. *The official logo is uploaded as a source file in the project — the illustrator must use that file at every specified placement, not recreate it by hand.*

**No IT support:** No support person or IT department appears anywhere. Oskar solves everything himself, in ten minutes, before Elin's first coffee has gone cold.

## Continuity note

The twenty-first episode in the series about connected workflows at Björklunda Åkeri AB, and Oskar Lind's third leading role — a natural continuation of his earlier stories about Viktor's API key (episode 3) and the Örebro office's department structure (episode 12). Where episode 12 was about an entire new location automatically inheriting the right permissions, this episode is about the much more common situation: one individual new employee, one single day, and the importance of neither locking someone out nor letting in too much. Sara Lund makes a brief, limited cameo that shows the human introduction is still needed — just not to explain the system.

**Next in the series:** A still-unused angle — how a haulage-company owner, in the middle of a period with several simultaneous customer enquiries, can see the entire pricing history for a particular route at a glance instead of digging through old invoices to remember what was quoted last time.

## Production notes for the illustrator

- **The checklist "TICK THE SAME AS THE COLLEAGUE"** should look clearly worn and handwritten in panel 2, and be put away (not thrown away) in panel 11 — the same symbolic movement as the binders and lists in earlier episodes.
- **The role list in panels 5–6** should be drawn simply and readably: four or five clearly named roles with one short description line each, no cryptic codes or technical terms.
- **Elin Sjöberg** is a new, recurring supporting role — note her appearance clearly for future cameos, like Robban Ek.
- **Sara Lund's cameo (panel 10)** should be brief and everyday — coffee, not support.
- **Physical props to prioritise:** the handwritten checklist, a small welcome card on Elin's new desk, the coffee mug with a discreet logo in the last panel.
