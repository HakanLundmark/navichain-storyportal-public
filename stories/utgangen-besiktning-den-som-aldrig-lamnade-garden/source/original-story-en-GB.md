# NAVICHAIN STORY
## "Expired inspection — the one that never left the yard"

**Series:** the navichain story
**Episode:** Third part in the same persona thread (Fors Maskinuthyrning AB) — after "Word against word — the damage that proved itself" and "Who bears responsibility? — The answer that existed before the question was asked"
**Target audience:** Owners/CEOs of rental companies with equipment subject to inspection requirements (lifts, pressure vessels, construction hoists, scaffolding), traffic planners/dispatch staff responsible for handover
**Core angle (not previously covered in the series):** Statutory inspection/calibration of rented equipment — ensuring that equipment with an expired inspection never leaves the yard, instead of discovering it at the customer
**Visual style:** Warm 1950s Hergé/Tintin *ligne claire* aesthetic — even black contour lines, flat colour fields, parchment/cream base, rust-red/mustard-yellow/petroleum-green/warm-gold palette, hand-lettered captions, physical attributes (inspection stickers, stamps, clipboards, records) rather than screenshots where possible, light offset-print grain with patina and soft edge wear — like a well-thumbed 1950s magazine. The story takes place in modern times: the characters work with computers and mobile phones, not paper.

---

## Main characters

- **Mikael Fors**, 44 — owner and day-to-day manager of **Fors Maskinuthyrning AB** (recurring). He has already solved damage documentation and ADR responsibility — now he discovers that the next risk has been hidden in the third layer: the inspection date.
- **Lena Bark**, 29 — handover manager/customer service at Fors Maskinuthyrning.
- **Robban Ek** — the series' recurring driver/haulier (continuity character), delivers and collects equipment for Fors.
- **Kenneth Dahl**, 51 — building contractor and regular customer (returning from "Word against word"), this time affected by a stoppage at his own worksite.
- **Construction work-environment inspector Owe Ljung** — the Swedish Work Environment Authority's representative during an unannounced site visit at Kenneth's construction site. Matter-of-fact, not hostile — he represents the rules, not a person to persuade.

---

## Story background
Fors Maskinuthyrning rents out a mobile construction hoist (person lift) which, under Swedish Work Environment Authority regulations, must undergo periodic inspection in order to be used legally. The inspection date is one detail among hundreds of other pieces of information about the equipment — and that detail is easily lost in a business focused on booking, delivery and invoicing.

---

## Colour dramaturgy
- **Panels 1–6 (red):** The inspection date is invisible until an authority asks for it — in the wrong place, at the wrong time.
- **Panels 6/7 (orange):** The turning point — Mikael realises the problem is not lack of knowledge, but that the date is never visible until someone actively looks for it.
- **Panels 8–12 (petroleum green):** Resolution — inspection and calibration status is visible for every piece of equipment before it leaves the yard, not afterwards.

**Logo placement:** navichain's official logo is completely absent from panels 1–6. It is introduced discreetly from panel 7 onwards as environmental set dressing (app header on the phone/screen, a decal on the yard information board, a binder spine in the office) — never centred, never advert-like, never more than about 5% of the panel area. The illustrator uses navichain's official logo from the source file uploaded to the project at every specified placement.

**No IT-support characters appear.** The platform should come across as self-explanatory in all resolution panels — Mikael and Lena configure and use navichain entirely by themselves.

**Terminology used (grounded in Navichain product documentation):** Equipment (equipment tracked with type/status/condition/location/assignee/value/warranty/maintenance/**calibration**), status Active/Maintenance/Retired/Out of Service (user-controlled). Calibration and warranty information is displayed and reported in the equipment register — the platform displays and organises the information, but does not replace the statutory inspection or the human decision to carry it out.
