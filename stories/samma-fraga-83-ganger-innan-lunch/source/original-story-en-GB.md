# THE SAME QUESTION. 83 TIMES. BEFORE LUNCH.

### Peter and the phone that finally went quiet

**Format:** 12-panel storyboard, editorial flat illustration style (warm 1950s Hergé/Tintin *ligne claire*, offset grain and patina) **Colour coding:** 🔴 = problem/crisis, 🟠 = transition/uncertainty, 🟢 = solved/secure **New angle:** Automatic customer notifications — an angle previously only hinted at (never done). The core is not “tracking” itself, but **the interruption**: the constant phone calls that eat into the dispatcher’s actual working time. The difference between “we can track vehicles” and “the customer never needs to call and ask” is the emotional hook.

---

## Cover — what should make the target audience unable to flip past

**Headline (large, dominant):** THE SAME QUESTION. 83 TIMES. BEFORE LUNCH. **Subheading:** Peter answered “Where is my delivery?” almost every five minutes — until the phone went quiet by itself. **Small body copy (lower corner):** A Navichain story about the interruption nobody counts as part of the working day.

**Cover image:** Peter sits in the middle of a runaway pile of old black Bakelite telephones — not one, but six or seven, stacked up and all ringing at once (an exaggerated, almost absurd comic image in the series’ style, à la Hergé when chaos is meant to feel physical). He holds a receiver in each hand, with a third wedged between his ear and shoulder. The desk is covered in yellow notes, each with the same handwritten question: “Where is the truck?” “Is it coming today?” “Is it delayed?” A wall clock behind him shows 11:47 — it is not even lunchtime yet. His tie is crooked, his hair tousled, his expression somewhere between panic and complete resignation — comically exaggerated, not depressing. Through the window behind him, a fully loaded truck can be glimpsed, having driven off long ago. No Navichain logo is visible in the cover image — fully in line with the logo being absent during the problem phase.

**Why it works as a hook:** Every dispatcher, transport planner and haulage-company owner in the target audience recognises exactly this pain immediately and personally — not as an abstract efficiency problem, but as a physical, daily, exhausting reality. The number “83” and the timestamp “before lunch” make it concrete enough to feel true rather than exaggerated, while the image is funny enough to stay in the memory.

---

**Main character:** Peter Lund, 45 — dispatcher at Ådalens Transport AB (22 vehicles, general cargo and distribution). Used to being the one who “has everything under control”, but having control has started to mean never getting to put the phone down.

**Supporting characters:**

- Anette, Peter’s wife — a brief appearance in panel 4 (phone call from home).
- Robban Ek — a continuity appearance as one of the drivers whose delivery triggers the first automatic notification in panel 8.

## Illustrator notes

- **The logo’s role and timing:** Completely absent from the cover and panels 1–6 (problem/transition phase). Introduced discreetly from panel 7 onwards (SMS sender field, signature screen, coffee mug). Max about 5% of the panel area, never centred or advertising-like. Use Navichain’s official logo (uploaded as a source file in the project) at every specified placement.
- **No IT support in any panel:** The activation in panel 6 should appear self-explanatory — a single toggle, no consultant, no training session, no support person in the image.
- **Comic exaggeration on the cover and in panels 1–2:** Unlike the series’ more restrained problem scenes, this story may be slightly funnier/more visually exaggerated specifically during the problem phase (the runaway pile of phones) — that is part of what makes the hook irresistible. The solution phase (panels 7–12), on the other hand, should feel as warm, authentic and calm as the rest of the series.
- **Emotional arc:** Peter should go from physically exhausted/comically overwhelmed (panels 1–4) to genuinely, warmly relieved (panels 9, 11) — not merely “problem solved” neutral.
- **Robban Ek cameo:** Appears in panel 8 and triggers the first concrete notification in the story — a recognition detail for returning readers, without taking focus away from Peter.
- **Differentiation from previous episodes:** This angle — automatic customer notifications as interruption reduction — has previously only been flagged as an upcoming teaser in other episodes, never made the main story. It deliberately differs from the earlier “quote-speed” angle (Kicki) by being about *interruptions throughout the whole day*, not speed in a single business event, and from the customer-portal angle by focusing on the customer never needing to log in or take any active step at all — the notification comes to the customer, not the other way round.
