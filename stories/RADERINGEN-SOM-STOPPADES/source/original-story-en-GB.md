# — A navichain story —

# THE DELETION THAT WAS STOPPED
### Frida and the mistake that never happened

**Angle:** The twentieth episode in the series about connected workflows at Björklunda Åkeri AB turns around a theme the series has previously shown from the positive side — colour-coded warnings (Kenneth, episode 19), transparent matching (Sara), clear reasons instead of black boxes. This time it is about *preventing* something, not spotting it in time: an ordinary, human mistake — deleting the wrong record — that must never become a disaster afterwards, because the system explains exactly why it cannot be done, before it happens.

**Source:** Navichain — Deletion Rules (verified: deletion performs a reference/status check and explains blockers. Protected entities include fleet resources, telematics, carriers, locations, warehouses, workshops, maintenance types/parts/categories, suppliers, organisations, users, bookings and pricing services. Runs/work orders/maintenance have status gates. Deletion is generally soft delete according to the glossary.)

**Important narrative rule:** navichain never prevents Frida from deleting something she is actually allowed to delete — the system only explains *why* a specific record is currently protected, with a clear reference to what still points to it. It is always Frida who understands and acts on the explanation; no automatic “undo” function, no IT ticket, nobody who rescues her afterwards. The message itself is the rescue.

## Cover concept

Frida is sitting at her computer, her finger about to press a “Delete” button, her expression road-road-road but starting to become worried. The screen shows a clear, calm message that has stopped her: a small red icon, and beneath it a short explanatory text with three bullet points listing what is still linked to the record. Behind her, blurred in the background, an old binder marked “DELETED BY MISTAKE — 2019” as a reminder of how it used to end. Title typography in the series’ established rust-red/petroleum-green combination.

---

## VISUAL STYLE (applies to all panels)

Warm 1950s aesthetic in Hergé/Tintin *ligne claire*: clean contour lines of even thickness, flat colour fields without halftones or heavy hatching. Parchment/cream base with rust red, mustard yellow, petroleum green and warm gold. Captions hand-lettered, never digital sans-serif. Physical props (the old “DELETED BY MISTAKE” binder, a coffee mug, a clipboard) are prioritised over sharp UI mock-ups. Lightly grainy offset-print texture with patina, soft edge wear and foxing.

**Recurring motif:** the binder marked “DELETED BY MISTAKE — 2019” remains visible in the early panels as a historical scar — proof of how things used to go, before navichain. In later panels it is moved away, dusty and unused, because that kind of mistake simply does not happen anymore.

**Logo rule:** navichain’s official logo is completely absent from panels 1–6. It is introduced discreetly from panel 7 — as a small icon in the corner of the deletion dialogue, a logo on Frida’s tablet, and finally on her coffee mug in the final panel. Never centred, never more than about 5% of the panel area, never advertising-like. *The official logo is uploaded as a source file in the project — the illustrator must use that file at every specified placement, not recreate it by hand.*

**No IT support:** No support person or IT department appears anywhere. Frida understands the message and resolves the situation entirely herself.

## PANEL 1 — The Friday clean-up
**Visual:** Frida goes through a list of old, apparently inactive records in the system — an old carrier that has not been used for a long time, a location that seems unnecessary. She marks several for deletion at once, as part of an ordinary routine.
**Text:** “An ordinary Friday. Clear away what is no longer needed.”
**Status:** 🔴 Red (routine action, hidden risk)

## PANEL 2 — The memory on the wall
**Visual:** In the background of the office, the binder “DELETED BY MISTAKE — 2019” can be seen, a little dusty but still on the shelf — a silent omen of what can happen.
**Text:** “That binder was still there for a reason. Nobody wanted to remember why.”
**Status:** 🔴 Red (historical warning)

## PANEL 3 — One click too many
**Visual:** Frida clicks “Delete” on one of the records — a carrier that looks inactive, but is actually linked to a booking further in the future, invisible in her current view.
**Text:** “She did not know the record was still connected to something.”
**Status:** 🔴 Red (invisible link)

## PANEL 4 — The message stops her
**Visual:** Instead of the record disappearing, a clear, calm dialogue box appears: a small red icon, and a short text explaining exactly what is blocking it — “Linked to 1 upcoming booking.”
**Text:** “Nothing disappeared. The system stopped her — and explained why.”
**Status:** 🟠 Orange (turning point — prevented in time)

## PANEL 5 — She understands immediately
**Visual:** Frida reads the message, her eyebrows rising in recognition rather than confusion. She opens the mentioned booking in a new tab to see the context.
**Text:** “One more click, and she understood exactly what had happened.”
**Status:** 🟠 Orange (clarity)

## PANEL 6 — Kenneth’s cameo
**Visual:** Kenneth Broman walks past with a coffee mug, sees the screen and nods in recognition: “The same thing happened to me last week with a truck linked to a work order. Nice that it tells you.” Brief, contained cameo.
**Text:** “‘Nice that it tells you’ — Kenneth had been there himself.”
**Status:** 🟢 Green (recognition, shared reassurance)

## PANEL 7 — She solves it the right way
**Visual:** Frida opens the carrier record and ends the booking link properly instead — not by forcing a deletion through. navichain’s official logo is visible discreetly in the corner of the view.
**Text:** “She resolved it properly instead of forcing a shortcut.”
**Status:** 🟢 Green (the right way forward)

## PANEL 8 — A new attempt
**Visual:** Frida tries to delete the record again, now that the link is gone. This time it goes through without obstruction, with a simple confirmation.
**Text:** “The second attempt went through immediately — because there was nothing left to protect.”
**Status:** 🟢 Green (confirmed action)

## PANEL 9 — The other records
**Visual:** Frida continues through the rest of her list. Another record shows the same kind of warning — linked to maintenance history this time — and she now recognises the pattern immediately without becoming worried.
**Text:** “The next warning no longer felt like a problem. Just information.”
**Status:** 🟢 Green (trust built)

## PANEL 10 — The binder is moved away
**Visual:** Frida takes the “DELETED BY MISTAKE — 2019” binder down from the shelf and puts it in an archive cabinet, not thrown away, but no longer visible in the office workspace.
**Text:** “The binder got to move. It did not need to stay out anymore.”
**Status:** 🟢 Green (symbolic conclusion)

## PANEL 11 — Friday afternoon, calm
**Visual:** Frida leans back with a coffee mug where navichain’s official logo is visible discreetly. The workspace is tidy, the list has been reviewed, nothing worrying remains.
**Text:** “The whole list reviewed. Nothing lost, nothing forgotten.”
**Status:** 🟢 Green (warm resolution)

## PANEL 12 — The Monday that never became a crisis
**Visual:** Time jump to Monday morning. The booking that would have vanished without a trace if the deletion had gone through now rolls out as planned, with the correct carrier linked, without anyone in the office even thinking about how close it came.
**Text:** “Monday’s delivery went exactly as it should. Nobody knew how close it had come to never happening.”
**Status:** 🟢 Green (ending, clear point)

## Production notes for the illustrator

- **Colour dramaturgy:** Red dominates panels 1–3 (routine, hidden risk), orange is the turning point (panels 4–5), petroleum green dominates panels 6–12.
- **The “DELETED BY MISTAKE — 2019” binder** must be clearly visible in panel 2 and then moved away in panel 10 — a silent visual parenthesis around the whole story.
- **The dialogue box in panel 4** should be drawn simply and calmly, not as an alarming error message — a small icon and short, factual text, in line with the series’ self-explanatory tone.
- **Kenneth’s cameo (panel 6)** should be brief and contained, like earlier cameos in the series — recognition, not a new plotline.
- **Physical props to prioritise:** the dusty binder, the clipboard on Frida’s desk in the early panels, the coffee mug with a discreet logo in panel 11.
