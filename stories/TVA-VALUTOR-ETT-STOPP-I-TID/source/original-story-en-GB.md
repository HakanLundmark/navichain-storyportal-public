# — A Navichain story —

# TWO CURRENCIES, ONE STOP IN TIME
### Åsa and the invoice that was never allowed to go out wrong

**Format:** 12 panels + cover concept. Illustrator brief.

**Angle:** The twenty-fourth episode in the series about connected workflows at Björklunda Åkeri AB follows Åsa Renberg for the second time. After Torbjörn's growth journey in episode 22, the company has taken on more international customers. The problem is specific and easy to miss: a booking can carry charges in several different currencies at the same time — for example, a freight charge in SEK and a surcharge that was accidentally entered in EUR — and it is entirely possible to continue working with such a booking for weeks without noticing anything is wrong, right up until the invoice is to be created.

**Source:** Navichain — Bookings/Pricing (verified: charges can retain different currencies on the same booking, but a booking with multiple currencies cannot be invoiced until the currencies have been aligned. Currency conversion uses the latest stored rate. An imported exchange-rate preview requires the user to confirm and save it.)

**Important narrative instruction:** Navichain never fixes the currency conflict automatically for Åsa. The system stops invoicing and clearly shows what is inconsistent, but she is always the one who chooses how the charge is to be adjusted and confirms the exchange rate. No IT department or finance consultant appears. Åsa resolves the entire situation herself, well before anything incorrect can be sent to the customer.

## Cover concept

Åsa sits at her desk with two calculating machines lined up — an old mechanical one and, beside it, a modern tablet. The screen shows a clear warning: “The booking contains SEK and EUR — align the currencies before invoicing.” Her expression is not panic but calm attention, as if she has just caught something before it could become a problem. Title lettering uses the series' established rust-red/petroleum-green combination.

---

## VISUAL STYLE (all panels)

Warm 1950s-style European *ligne claire*: clean outlines of even thickness, flat colour fields without halftones or heavy hatching. Parchment/cream base with rust red, mustard yellow, petroleum green and warm gold. Captions should feel hand-lettered, never like digital sans-serif. Physical props — an old paper exchange-rate table, a calculator, a coffee mug — take priority over sharp UI mock-ups. Light offset-print grain with patina, gentle edge wear and foxing.

**Recurring motif:** a yellowed handwritten exchange-rate table (SEK/EUR/USD) is pinned to the wall behind Åsa's desk in the early panels — a sign of how currency conversion used to be done from memory and from a table that quickly became outdated. The table is taken down in a later panel.

**Logo rule:** the official Navichain logo is completely absent from panels 1–6. It is introduced discreetly from panel 7 — on an appropriate digital carrier and, finally, on Åsa's coffee mug in the last panel. It is never centred, never more than about 5% of the panel area and never advertisement-like. The official uploaded logo source must be used; it must not be recreated by hand.

**No IT support:** No support person or IT department appears anywhere. Åsa resolves everything herself.

## PANEL 1 — An ordinary international booking
**Visual:** Åsa reviews a list of bookings ahead of the week's invoicing. One of them is for a German customer — apparently completely ordinary.
**Text:** “One booking among many. Nothing that stood out at a quick glance.”
**Status:** 🔴 Red (hidden risk, not yet visible)

## PANEL 2 — The old rate table
**Visual:** Behind Åsa hangs a yellowed handwritten exchange-rate table — SEK, EUR, USD — with a date at the top that is several months old.
**Text:** “The table on the wall did not know what today's exchange rate actually was.”
**Status:** 🔴 Red (outdated reference)

## PANEL 3 — Two charges, two currencies
**Visual:** Åsa zooms in on the booking's charge lines: the freight charge is in SEK, but a surcharge for an extra stop was accidentally entered in EUR by a colleague earlier in the week.
**Text:** “The freight charge in kronor. The surcharge in euros. No one had noticed the difference.”
**Status:** 🔴 Red (the error is discovered)

## PANEL 4 — Almost too late
**Visual:** Åsa moves the pointer towards “Create invoice”, ready to proceed as usual, before she has stopped to consider the currency difference.
**Text:** “One more click — and only then would the currency conflict reveal itself.”
**Status:** 🔴 Red (near miss)

## PANEL 5 — The system stops her
**Visual:** Instead of an invoice being created, a clear, calm warning appears: “The booking contains SEK and EUR — align the currencies before invoicing.”
**Text:** “Nothing went out. The system stopped her exactly where the inconsistency was.”
**Status:** 🟠 Orange (turning point — prevented in time)

## PANEL 6 — She understands immediately
**Visual:** Åsa reads the message, nods to herself and opens the booking's charge lines so she can see both side by side.
**Text:** “She did not have to guess what was wrong. The message said it plainly.”
**Status:** 🟠 Orange (clarity)

## PANEL 7 — A current rate, not the old table
**Visual:** Åsa opens the currency settings in Navichain on her tablet and sees an imported current exchange-rate preview waiting for her confirmation. The official Navichain logo is discreetly integrated on the planned digital carrier.
**Text:** “A current rate, imported and ready — she only had to confirm and save it.”
**Status:** 🟢 Green (current information)

## PANEL 8 — She confirms it deliberately
**Visual:** Åsa selects “Confirm and save” for the exchange rate. She then deliberately chooses how the EUR surcharge should be adjusted/converted to SEK; the system does not alter the existing charge until she makes that choice.
**Text:** “She saved the rate, then chose the adjustment herself. Nothing changed without her say-so.”
**Status:** 🟢 Green (control retained)

## PANEL 9 — The booking is aligned
**Visual:** The booking's charge lines are now consistently shown in SEK, the currency warning is gone, and “Create invoice” is active rather than blocked.
**Text:** “Same booking. Same charges. Now in one currency.”
**Status:** 🟢 Green (resolved)

## PANEL 10 — Torbjörn's brief cameo
**Visual:** Torbjörn Björklund walks past Åsa's desk, sees that the warning has been resolved and gives a short, amused nod: “Good thing someone catches that before it becomes my problem.” Brief, contained cameo.
**Text:** “Good thing someone catches that before it becomes my problem.”
**Status:** 🟢 Green (confirmation, shared reassurance)

## PANEL 11 — The table comes down
**Visual:** Åsa takes the old yellowed exchange-rate table off the wall and puts it in a drawer — not thrown away, but no longer relevant.
**Text:** “The table was allowed to retire. The rate was already where she needed it.”
**Status:** 🟢 Green (symbolic closure)

## PANEL 12 — The invoice is sent, correctly
**Visual:** Åsa leans back with her coffee mug, carrying the discreet official Navichain logo, while the invoice is sent from the screen behind her — one correct total in a single currency.
**Text:** “The invoice went out exactly as it should. The customer never knew how close it had come to being wrong.”
**Status:** 🟢 Green (resolution, clear point)

## Production notes for the illustrator

- **Colour dramaturgy:** Red dominates panels 1–4 (hidden risk, near miss); orange is the turning point (panels 5–6); petroleum green dominates panels 7–12.
- **The yellowed exchange-rate table** should look visibly outdated in panel 2 (with a clearly old date) and be taken down in panel 11 — the same symbolic movement used for binders and lists in earlier episodes.
- **The warning in panel 5** should be calm and factual, not an alarming error message — short text, clear colour, in keeping with the series' self-explanatory tone.
- **Torbjörn's cameo (panel 10)** should be brief and contained, as in earlier cameos in the series.
- **Physical props to prioritise:** the old mechanical calculating machine from the cover concept, the rate table on the wall and the coffee mug with the discreet official logo in the final panel.
