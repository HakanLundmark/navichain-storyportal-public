# NAVICHAIN STORY
## "Word against word — the damage that proved itself"

**Export status:** Directly sourced from the user's complete story brief in this chat. This is the original story source. A later preflight correction was discussed in chat, but no complete corrected Narrative Master was separately materialised as an exportable file; therefore this file does not silently reconstruct that later revision.

**Series:** the Navichain story  
**Episode:** Standalone — the rental vertical (Equipment / Asset management)  
**Target audience:** Owners/CEOs of rental companies (machines, tools, construction aids), as well as dispatchers/hand-over staff  
**Core angle (not previously covered in the series):** Burden of proof when rented equipment is damaged — documented condition at hand-over and return, instead of "word against word"  
**Visual style:** Warm 1950s Hergé/Tintin *ligne claire* aesthetic — even black contour lines, flat colour areas, parchment/cream-white base, rust-red/mustard-yellow/petroleum-green/warm-gold palette, hand-lettered captions, physical attributes (receipts, stamps, checklists, forms) rather than screenshots wherever possible, light offset-print grain with patina and soft edge wear — like a well-thumbed 1950s magazine. The story takes place in modern times: the characters work with computers and mobile phones, not paper.

## Main characters

- **Mikael Fors**, 44 — owner and day-to-day manager of **Fors Maskinuthyrning AB**, a rental company for building and civil-engineering equipment (excavators, plate compactors, construction hoists, generators) outside Södertälje. Worn but proud, used to solving problems with his head rather than money.
- **Lena Bark**, 29 — hand-over manager/customer service at Fors Maskinuthyrning. The person caught in the middle when customers are angry.
- **Kenneth Dahl**, 51 — building contractor and regular customer, frequently rents larger machines. Honest but uncompromising when he feels he has been billed unfairly.
- **Robban Ek** — the series' recurring driver/haulier (continuity character). Here he rents a plate compactor for his own company and makes a cameo in the resolution.

## Colour dramaturgy
- **Panels 1–6 (red):** Invisible burden of proof — damage is discovered after the equipment has already left the yard, no documentation exists, "word against word" between Mikael and the customer.
- **Panel 7 (orange):** Turning point — Mikael realises the problem is not the customers, but the absence of evidence.
- **Panels 8–12 (petroleum green):** Resolution — every hand-over and return is documented digitally, disputes disappear, trust is restored.

**Logo placement:** Navichain's official logo is completely absent from panels 1–6. It is introduced discreetly from panel 7 onwards as environmental props (app header on the phone/screen, a sticker on the yard's information notice, a coffee mug) — never centred, never advertisement-like, never more than about 5% of the panel area. ## Summary table for the illustrator

| Before Navichain | With Navichain |
|---|---|
| Handwritten, often blank hand-over receipts | Digital checklist per equipment type, completed on the phone |
| Damage discovered on return — no one can prove when it happened | Timestamped condition at hand-over *and* return, with photo |
| "Word against word" — dispute, unpaid invoice, lost trust | Evidence decides immediately — the customer pays without argument |
| Customer waits for staff to carry out the check | Customer performs their own check-in/check-out in the app (see Robban Ek) |
| Someone has to keep track of condition history manually | Automatic history per item of equipment, searchable and shareable |
| No IT department needed — Mikael and Lena build the template themselves | Still no IT dependency — entirely self-explanatory |

**Terminology used (grounded in Navichain's product documentation):** Equipment (equipment tracked with type/status/condition/location/responsible person/value/maintenance), Equipment inspection (template-based checklist OK/Issue/Fixed/N/A, with notes and optional hour-meter reading), status Clean/Issue Reported/Resolved. Damage cases discovered during inspection can automatically generate a follow-up task, giving Mikael and Lena a traceable case to close — not just an oral conflict.
