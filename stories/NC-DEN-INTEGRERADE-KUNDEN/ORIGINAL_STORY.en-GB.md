# Navichain story: "The Integrated Customer"

### Persona: Nina Ahlström, Key Account Manager

**Source:** Article "The Integrated Customer: Become a Seamless Part of Their Value Chain" (theme series "Integrations That Change Everything")

**Format:** 12 panels, Swedish dialogue/captions in the Narrative Master, 1950s Hergé/Tintin style (ligne claire), colour dramaturgy red → mustard/orange → petrol green. Navichain's official logo completely absent from panels 1–6, introduced discreetly from panel 7.

---

## Panel 1 — The meeting Nina dreaded

**Image:** Nina is sitting in a glass-walled meeting room at a major customer (industrial company, warehouse halls visible through the window). Opposite her: Andreas, the customer's logistics manager, with his arms crossed and a binder full of printed emails in front of him. **Caption:** "Fourth time this year we've had to track down a discrepancy after the fact, Nina." **Status:** 🔴 Red — customer relationship under pressure

## Panel 2 — The binder as evidence

**Image:** Close-up of the binder. Printed order confirmations, handwritten changes in the margins, a POD receipt with the wrong date circled in red ink. **Caption:** "Everything goes by email. And emails get forgotten." **Status:** 🔴 Red — manual double entry

## Panel 3 — The threat

**Image:** Andreas leans forward, pointing at a competitor's quotation lying upside down on the table. Nina recognises the logo and turns pale. **Caption:** "Our new ERP provider promises that everything will be connected automatically. Maybe it's time for us to look around." **Status:** 🔴 Red — customer about to leave

## Panel 4 — The drive back

**Image:** Nina in the driver's seat on the way back to the office, rain against the windscreen, her gaze blank. On the passenger seat is the binder she has been allowed to borrow. **Caption:** "We deliver perfectly. But it never shows up in their system." **Status:** 🔴 Red — value invisible to the customer

## Panel 5 — The whiteboard

**Image:** Nina draws the flow on a whiteboard at the office: one box labelled "Our TMS", one box labelled "Customer's ERP", and a large red cross between them. **Caption:** "Two systems that never talk to each other. That's the whole problem." **Status:** 🔴 Red — broken chain

## Panel 6 — The colleague with the idea

**Image:** Johan (finance) sticks his head through the door and points at the whiteboard. "That cross — it doesn't have to be there." **Caption:** "Talk to IT. They already connected the authority reporting last week." **Status:** 🟠 Mustard — the insight takes shape

## Panel 7 — The connection is built

**Image:** Nina and Viktor (IT) sit together at a screen. In the corner of the screen is a small interface mark: Navichain's official logo, discreetly placed. They connect icons for "TMS" and "Customer's ERP" with a simple line. **Caption:** "One connection. Then everything flows by itself." **Status:** 🟢 Green — integration begins

## Panel 8 — The booking that makes itself

**Image:** Split-screen: at the customer, an order is created in their ERP system; the same second it appears in Nina's booking list on the screen. A small Navichain icon appears in the corner of the booking flow. **Caption:** "No one emails anymore. The order is already here." **Status:** 🟢 Green — two-way synchronisation in real time

## Panel 9 — POD without asking

**Image:** A driver signs a delivery digitally at a loading dock. The receipt is sent directly into the customer's system — a small notification appears on Andreas's screen in the background without him doing anything. **Caption:** "The proof of delivery is in their system before he has even asked for it." **Status:** 🟢 Green — automatic POD flow

## Panel 10 — The proposal before the question

**Image:** Nina looks at a screen where a system suggests an optimised pickup based on the customer's stock levels — a Navichain watermark is discreetly visible in the bottom corner of the interface. **Caption:** "We suggested a pickup before they knew they needed one." **Status:** 🟢 Green — predictive collaboration

## Panel 11 — New meeting, new mood

**Image:** The same glass-walled meeting room as in panel 1. Andreas is smiling now, leaning back, no binder in sight — only a tablet. A Navichain-branded mug stands on the table between them. **Caption:** "We haven't had to call you for three weeks. That says it all." **Status:** 🟢 Green — trust restored

## Panel 12 — The contract is extended

**Image:** Nina walks out of the meeting holding a signed contract extension, with a broad smile. In the background, through the glass wall, a central screen at the customer shows several connected systems pulsing green around a node marked with Navichain's official logo. A note on her notepad: "Next: the vehicle as a data source." **Caption:** "One connection, not a threshold. Next: when the truck starts telling its own story." **Status:** 🟢 Green — the customer becomes part of the flow

## Note to the illustrator

- **Style:** Warm 1950s Hergé/Tintin ligne claire — evenly weighted black outlines, flat colour fields, no heavy shading. Parchment/cream base palette with rust red, mustard yellow, petrol green, warm gold. Hand-lettered captions. Slightly yellowed, grain-dense offset feel like a well-thumbed 1950s magazine.
- **Navichain's official logo:** The official logo file is uploaded as a source file in the project — use that file at every specified placement; do not invent a replacement. Always call it "Navichain's official logo", never "the N mark", "N logotype", "N symbol", "N monogram" or similar.
  - Panels 1–6: the logo is completely absent.
  - Panels 7–12: introduced gradually and discreetly — interface corner (panel 7) → icon in booking flow (panel 8) → watermark in interface (panel 10) → mug on the table (panel 11) → central node in the final image (panel 12).
  - Never exceeds about 5% of the panel area, never centred or advertising-like.
- **Characters:** Nina is warm and engaged throughout, genuinely happy in the resolution panels. Andreas goes from stiff/suspicious to relaxed and smiling.
- **Continuity:** Builds on Viktor's story ("A Backbone, No Longer an Island") — Nina takes over where the customer relationship is put to the test. The teaser in panel 12 points to the next article in the theme week: "The Connected Fleet — When the Vehicle Becomes a Data Source."
