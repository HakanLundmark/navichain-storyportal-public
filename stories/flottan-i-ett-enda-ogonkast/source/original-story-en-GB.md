# THE FLEET, AT A SINGLE GLANCE

### Nina and the map that shows not only where — but why

**Angle:** The seventh episode in the series about connected workflows returns to the first, most fundamental question of the morning for anyone responsible for an entire fleet: which vehicles are moving, which are not — and why. What makes the task distinctive is that the answer requires two things at the same time: where the vehicle actually is right now, and why it may be standing still. One without the other is not enough.

**Source:** Navichain — Fleet Map and the inspection/deviation workflow (verified: Fleet Map lists trucks/trailers whose linked device has reported a position, clearly distinguishes moving/stopped/stale status and displays vehicle/run/ETA data where available, with automatic updates. Driver inspections where every deviation creates a defect and notifies back office; a non-critical deviation leaves the vehicle available, a critical deviation automatically blocks the vehicle and it can only be returned to service through a separate, named approval — never by the person who reported the fault.)

## Cover concept

Nina stands in front of a large screen with a live map full of small vehicle icons — some moving, some stopped with a clear marker. One of the icons is highlighted in red with a small symbol suggesting "waiting". She points directly at it, already in the middle of an explanation. Title lettering in the same rust-red/petroleum-green combination as the other covers in the series.

## PANEL 1 — The morning's first question

**Visual:** Nina comes into the office at seven o'clock and glances at a whiteboard full of magnets — one for each vehicle — some moved by different colleagues late the night before, with no one completely sure whether they are still correct. **Text:** "Twenty vehicles. One whiteboard. No one really knows whether it is still right." **Status:** 🔴 Red — status without a reliable source

## PANEL 2 — The round around the office

**Visual:** Nina goes from desk to desk asking colleagues: "Is number 14 out?", "Do you know what happened to number 22?". The answers vary, no one fully certain. **Text:** "The same question, five times, five half-answers." **Status:** 🔴 Red — the status lives in different people's heads

## PANEL 3 — The vehicle that was supposed to be out

**Visual:** Sara comes over with a booking she has just assigned to vehicle 22 — but the vehicle turns out to have been standing in the yard since yesterday, out of service for a reason neither of them knows. **Text:** "A booking on a vehicle that was already out of service. No one knew until now." **Status:** 🔴 Red — resource planned without a real basis

## PANEL 4 — The hour spent just finding out

**Visual:** The clock on the wall has reached eight. Nina is still sitting with a half-finished list of which vehicles can actually be counted on today. **Text:** "A whole hour, just to know what should already have been known." **Status:** 🔴 Red — time lost before the day has even begun

## PANEL 5 — The turning point: the map that stays current

**Visual:** Nina opens Navichain's fleet map on the large screen. Every vehicle appears as an icon — clearly marked moving, stopped or stale — updated in real time instead of yesterday's magnets. **Text:** "No more whiteboard. Just what is actually true right now." **Status:** 🟠 Orange — actual status, not yesterday's guess

## PANEL 6 — Not only where — why

**Visual:** Nina clicks vehicle 22's icon. A detail box opens: "Stopped — critical deviation, blocked since 06:42, awaiting approved return to service." Nothing mysterious remains. **Text:** "Not just that it is standing still. Exactly why — and what is required before it can run again." **Status:** 🟠 Orange — the cause is visible, not only the symptom

## PANEL 7 — The day is replanned, immediately

**Visual:** Nina and Sara look at the map together and move today's booking from vehicle 22 to a vehicle that is actually available — the decision takes seconds instead of another round of calls. A discreet sticker with Navichain's official logo is visible in the corner of the map view. **Text:** "One decision, directly from the map — not another round of questions." **Status:** 🟢 Green — replanning without delay

## PANEL 8 — A deviation that does not stop the day

**Visual:** A driver's morning inspection later that day flags a minor deviation on another vehicle — it remains available, no block, only a note. Nina does not even raise an eyebrow. **Text:** "Not every deviation stops a vehicle. The map knows the difference — and now Nina does without asking." **Status:** 🟢 Green — the right level of severity, visible immediately

## PANEL 9 — Return to service, with a name behind it

**Visual:** Vehicle 22 is marked green again — a named approval has returned the vehicle to service after the critical deviation was resolved, visible in the history rather than as a silent status change. **Text:** "Someone responsible put their name to the fact that it was ready again. **Visual:** Nina and Sara review the day's situation together in front of the large screen — the whole fleet in a single view instead of a round between desks. ## Illustrator reference

- **Style:** Warm 1950s tone, Hergé/Tintin *ligne claire*: even contour line, flat colour fields, no heavy halftone. Subtle offset grain, patina, soft edge wear.
- **Colour dramaturgy:** Panels 1–4: rust-red, early morning and uncertain artificial light. Panels 5–6: rich orange, the large screen lights up and gives the room a new focus. Panels 7–12: petroleum green, clear morning light, calm composition.
- **Nina Palm:** Practical operations-manager clothing, preferably a light jacket suggesting that she is often out in the yard as well as at a desk. Restless, moving around a lot in the red panels — still and focused in front of the screen in the green ones.
- **The map as a visual motif:** Vehicle icons should clearly distinguish moving/stopped/stale state through shape or icon colour — avoid making the map purely decorative; the detail box in panel 6 should be readable and concrete.
- **Important narrative instruction:** No panel may imply that Navichain automatically replans bookings or decides on its own which vehicle should drive what — Nina and Sara make that decision together, with better information. No panel may imply that the person who reports a critical deviation can personally approve the vehicle's return to service — it must always be a clearly separate, named person.
- **Sara Lund / Robban Ek:** Same returning visual design as in earlier episodes. Short, clearly bounded supporting-character moments.
- **Navichain's official logo:** Use the uploaded source file exactly as it is — never redraw it by hand. Absent in panels 1–4. Introduced discreetly from panel 7 (the corner of the map view) and then appears on the coffee mug in the final panel — never centred, never advertising-like, maximum about 5% of the panel area.
- **No IT support:** No external technician, no support case. Nina resolves everything herself through the interface — Navichain is self-explanatory throughout the story.
- **Paper vs screen:** The whiteboard and magnets dominate panels 1–4 physically. From panel 5 onwards the large screen takes over as the room's natural focal point — let the whiteboard appear faded/inactive in the background from panel 7 onwards, with its clear ending in panel 11.

## Preflight amendments used in production

These amendments were explicitly established in this chat before image production:

- Fleet Map and operational/inspection status are treated as separate surfaces: the map shows reported position/moving/stopped/stale information; vehicle/inspection status supplies the reason a vehicle may not be allowed to run.
- Panel 3 production logic was revised so Vehicle 22 was planned earlier and then became unavailable after the 06:42 critical inspection, rather than being newly assigned after already being grounded.
- Fleet Map wording was revised from “real-time” to automatic updates / what the vehicles report now.
- Nina and Sara make the replanning decision; Navichain does not autonomously reassign the booking.
- A non-critical inspection issue does not itself ground the truck.
- Returning a truck to service requires a separate named sign-off after the critical defect is resolved; readiness to run is distinct from already moving.
