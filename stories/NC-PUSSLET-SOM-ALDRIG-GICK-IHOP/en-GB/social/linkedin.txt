THE PUZZLE THAT NEVER FIT

Two haulage companies had become one.

62 vehicles now had to share the same way of working, the same data and the same future.

For Rasmus Boström, the new IT and procurement lead at Nordvästra Fraktkedjan AB, the work began in the most rational way possible: with a requirements list.

Telematics.
Tariffs.
e-CMR.
Sustainability data.
Data sovereignty.

Then came the vendor demos.

The problem was not that the alternatives were bad. Quite the opposite.

Several systems were strong in different areas. But the more boxes Rasmus filled in on his comparison matrix, the clearer the real question became:

Should a merged transport business really have to build its future from several separate systems just to get the whole picture?

That was where Navichain became interesting.

Not as just another system in the line-up, but as a way to bring transport planning, telematics, tariffs, e-CMR, sustainability data and daily operations together in one platform.

Suddenly, the procurement was no longer about which vendor had the flashiest demo.

It was about which solution best fitted the business Rasmus would actually be responsible for after the merger.

Two haulage companies.
One system.
One shared view of the business.

That may be the most important question when choosing a TMS:

Are you evaluating features one by one - or evaluating how the entire business needs to work together?
