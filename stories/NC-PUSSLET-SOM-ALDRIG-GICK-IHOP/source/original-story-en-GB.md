# Storyboard: "The Puzzle That Never Fit" — Rasmus and the search for a complete TMS

**Part of:** a Navichain story (a standalone episode in the same universe as Maria, Johan, Elin, Patrik, Susanne, Sofia Bergqvist, Björn and others — Robban Ek makes a continuity appearance in panel 11)

**Character:** Rasmus Boström, 41 — newly appointed Head of IT and Procurement after two haulage companies merge to form Nordvästra Fraktkedjan AB (62 vehicles, a mix of long-haul and distribution traffic)

**Supporting characters:**

- Three salespeople/demonstrators — generic, clearly named by system (AddSecure, MobiOne, Opter) but intentionally indistinct/interchangeable in their character design, to keep the focus on the systems rather than the individuals
- Robban Ek — driver, brief continuity scene in panel 11

**Source material:** Navichain's comparison guide "TMS-guiden 2026: Navichain vs AddSecure, MobiOne och marknadens giganter" (blog.navichain.se)

**Angle (new, not previously used in the series):** The structured vendor-comparison process itself — as the new system owner for a merged haulage company, having to evaluate several named competing systems side by side in a decision matrix, and discovering that each system is strong in its own area but weak in the rest (the telematics specialist lacks tariff and multi-leg logic, the tariff specialist lacks telematics and requires local client installation, the distribution specialist lacks CSRD and enterprise logic). This is clearly distinct from earlier episodes about hidden costs in a single quotation (Björn, 10–50 vehicles), hardware CAPEX specifically (Camilla, Mange), data sovereignty specifically (Sofia Bergqvist, Calle), and internal system shortcomings (Viktor, Gustav) — here the focus is the comparative procurement process across several named market players and the realisation that an all-in-one solution beats a patchwork of best-in-class point solutions.

**Format:** 12 panels + cover concept **Series label in the header of the finished image:** "A Navichain story"

---

## Visual style (applies to all panels)

Warm 1950s *ligne claire* aesthetic in the Hergé/Tintin tradition: clean contour lines of even weight, flat/even colour fills (no halftone shading, no heavy cross-hatching). A parchment/cream base with rust red, mustard yellow, petroleum green and warm gold. Captions are rendered as handwritten/hand-drawn lettering — never digital sans-serif. Physical props (documents, clipboards, whiteboards, archive binders, stamps) are prioritised over screenshots/mock-ups where possible; retro CRT screens where screens are unavoidable. The surface should feel lightly grainy in an offset-print style with age and patina: subtle paper texture, soft edge wear/foxing, muted/slightly faded colours — like a well-thumbed 1950s magazine. Characters are warm, expressive and genuinely happy/smiling in the solution panels.

**Modern context, classic visual language:** The story takes place in the present day (2026) — Rasmus works with a laptop, tablet and mobile phone, not paper and a telephone handset, as Navichain comes into use. Manual paperwork and analogue equipment (typewriter, binders, fax) belong in the problem phase's depiction of the old, fragmented way of working — not in Navichain's solution.

## Colour dramaturgy

- Panels 1–6: **Red** → merger, system chaos, the comparison work starts going in circles
- Panels 6–7: **Orange/mustard yellow** → turning point: the insight that no individual competing system is sufficient
- Panels 8–12: **Petroleum green** → Navichain covers the whole matrix and the merged business gets one common system

## Logo handling

- The logo is referred to throughout as **"Navichain's official logo"** in this brief and in all instructions to the illustrator.
- The illustrator must use the uploaded source file containing the official logo at every specified placement — never an interpretation, simplification or stylised replacement.
- **Completely absent in panels 1–6** (deliberately — reinforcing the contrast between the fragmented comparison work and the later, unified solution).
- Introduced discreetly from panel 7 onwards, solely as an environmental prop: the corner of a tablet screen, a coffee mug, a clipboard, a document watermark.
- Never more than about 5% of the panel area, never centred, never advertising-like.
- No IT support person appears in any panel — Rasmus navigates Navichain himself without assistance, reinforcing that the platform is self-explanatory.

## Cover concept

**Visual:** Rasmus at a conference-room table, surrounded by four open binders/quotation folders labelled with different system names, with a whiteboard behind him full of comparison columns and question marks. Expression: analytically tired, squeezed between the alternatives. **Title:** "The Puzzle That Never Fit" **Subtitle:** "Rasmus and the search for a complete TMS" **Header:** "A Navichain story"

---

### Panel 1 — The merger

**Visual:** Rasmus in a new, half-empty office with boxes still waiting to be unpacked. Two signs on the wall — the old haulage companies' names — are being replaced by one common sign: "Nordvästra Fraktkedjan AB". On the desk: two laptops, each open to one of the old systems. **Text:** "Two haulage companies became one. Now 62 vehicles have to go into the same system — and nobody knows which one." **Status:** 🔴 Red — two systems, one decision left

### Panel 2 — The requirements list

**Visual:** Close-up of Rasmus's tablet as he writes a list in a notes app: "Telematics. Tariffs. e-CMR. CSRD. Data sovereignty." The list grows longer than the screen can accommodate. **Text:** "Five requirements on paper. No idea which system can handle more than two of them." **Status:** 🔴 Red — the requirements keep growing

### Panel 3 — The AddSecure demo

**Visual:** Video call on Rasmus's laptop with a salesperson showing advanced driving-and-rest-time graphics and GPS tracking on a shared screen. Rasmus takes notes, but looks doubtful as he switches to the next tab in his own comparison template: "Multi-leg handling: ?" **Text:** "Best in class at telematics. But the tariff and multi-leg logic feels like an afterthought." **Status:** 🔴 Red — strong telematics, weak TMS core

### Panel 4 — The Opter demo

**Visual:** A new video call; another salesperson shows a complex tariff matrix in a heavy, older interface. A chat line says: "Requires local client installation." Rasmus's tablet beside it shows a note: "Start-up: SEK 30,000–60,000. Telematics: purchased separately." **Text:** "Great at Nordic tariffs. But no telematics of its own — and installation is required on every computer." **Status:** 🔴 Red — strong tariff logic, hardware-light at the customer but software-locked

### Panel 5 — The MobiOne demo

**Visual:** Third call; a salesperson shows a simple, polished driver interface on a mobile phone. Rasmus nods in recognition — it looks easy — but his comparison template shows empty boxes for "CSRD reporting" and "Consolidation/terminals". **Text:** "The drivers would love it. But it is built for simple distribution, not our complexity." **Status:** 🔴 Red — simple interface, too thin for the operation

### Panel 6 — The whiteboard

**Visual:** Rasmus stands in front of a whiteboard in the conference room with three columns — one name per system — filled with crosses and ticks in different patterns. No column is entirely green. He sits down and rubs his forehead. **Text:** "Three systems. Three half-solutions. Do we really need to buy three contracts to cover one?" **Status:** 🔴 Red — no single winner

### Panel 7 — The fourth option

**Visual:** A colleague places a tablet in front of Rasmus, open to Navichain's web platform. The same requirements list from panel 2 is visible beside it, with ticks appearing one after another as Rasmus scrolls. A discreet corner mark with Navichain's official logo is visible at the top of the screen. **Text:** "A fourth name appears on the list. This time the boxes tick off, one by one." **Status:** 🟠 Orange — all-in-one appears on the radar

### Panel 8 — The live demo

**Visual:** Rasmus in a video call, but this time he points and clicks through Navichain's interface himself in real time: consolidation, e-CMR signing and a Scope 3 graph in the same view. A coffee mug with Navichain's official logo is on the table. **Text:** "No question he asks gets a 'we solve that with an add-on'. It is already there." **Status:** 🟠 Orange — one platform, the whole requirements list

### Panel 9 — The matrix fills in

**Visual:** Rasmus back at the whiteboard, now adding a fourth column — "Navichain" — and filling it with green ticks row by row while the other three columns remain mixed. A sticker in the corner of the whiteboard carries Navichain's official logo. **Text:** "Telematics. Tariffs. e-CMR. CSRD. Swedish cloud. Everything in the same column, for the first time." **Status:** 🟢 Green — the comparison is decided

### Panel 10 — The decision

**Visual:** Rasmus signs a digital agreement on his laptop while a colleague beside him nods with satisfaction. The screen shows a confirmation box: "SEK 0 start-up fee. SEK 199/user/month." No binders or paper piles remain in view. **Text:** "No start-up cost to justify to the board. Just a clear yes." **Status:** 🟢 Green — agreement signed, without CAPEX

### Panel 11 — The rollout

**Visual:** One month later: Robban Ek, the driver, stands by his truck and logs into Navichain's driver app on his own mobile phone without an introduction or a manual in his hand. He smiles at the camera, thumbs up. A small sticker with Navichain's official logo is discreetly visible on the dashboard. **Text:** "No training day. No IT department to call. Robban was up and running before the coffee went cold." **Status:** 🟢 Green — self-explanatory, day one

### Panel 12 — One common system

**Visual:** Rasmus in the now fully furnished office, with a single screen on his desk showing the status of the entire merged fleet in Navichain's portal — 62 vehicles, one system. Through the window, trucks from both former haulage companies can be seen, now carrying the same decal. He leans back, holding a mug with Navichain's official logo. **Text:** "Two haulage companies. One system. And for the first time — one single truth to rely on." **Teaser for the next episode:** A small note in the corner of the image: "Next: How do you calculate the real return on 40 vehicles?" **Status:** 🟢 Green — the merger reaches the finish line
