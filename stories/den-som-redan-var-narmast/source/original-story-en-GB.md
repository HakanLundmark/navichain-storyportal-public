# THE ONE WHO WAS ALREADY CLOSEST
### A Navichain story — Yvonne, Rasmus and the guess that cost forty-five minutes

**Persona:** Yvonne Kron, 44, coordinator/dispatcher, Kron Elteknik AB  
**Co-lead:** Rasmus Öberg, 28, field technician, Kron Elteknik AB  
**New supporting character:** Torgny Blad, owner of Norrtulls Bageri, the affected emergency customer  
**Persona thread:** Episode 6 in the Kron Elteknik thread — after “The Job No One Could Dispute”, “The Return Trip That Never Needed to Happen”, “The Note That Was Never Written”, “The Man Who Was Supposed to Be in Two Places” and “The Clock That Did Not Lie”  
**Theme:** Emergency jobs — finding the right technician for an urgent job by guessing where someone is “roughly” located instead of actually knowing it  
**Navichain’s role in the story:** Fleet Map showing the service vehicles’ actual reported positions, so the person who is genuinely closest can be sent directly — not the person who happens to answer the phone or is assumed to be nearby

**Note about the previous teaser:** The previous episode teased a customer’s complete history with Kron Elteknik over several years. That angle is too close to an already-used story in the series (a customer overview covering all jobs, in an earlier episode about the customer portal and trust), so this episode instead takes a genuinely unused angle: actual vehicle positioning for emergency jobs.

**Format:** 12 panels + cover concept, Swedish dialogue/captions in the visual master, warm 1950s Hergé/Tintin-style ligne claire, parchment/cream base palette with rust red, mustard yellow, petroleum green and warm gold, hand-lettered captions, subtle print grain and patina — modern context, classic visual language  
**Colour dramaturgy:** 🔴 Red (problem, panels 1–6) → 🟠 Orange (turning point, panel 7) → 🟢 Petroleum green (solution, panels 8–12)

**Logo rule (mandatory):** Navichain’s official logo is completely absent in panels 1–6. It is introduced discreetly from panel 7 onwards — as an environmental detail (the app header on a mobile screen, a service-van decal), never centred or advertising-like, max. about 5% of the panel area. ## PANEL 1 — The alarm from the bakery
**Image:** Yvonne answers the phone and hears a stressed voice. In the background, her screen shows a schedule with the technicians’ approximate routes for the day, but no exact position.  
**Caption:** “The power is out in the entire bakery — the dough is rising, the chillers are stopping. We need someone NOW.”  
**Status:** 🔴 Red — urgent job, nobody knows where anyone actually is

## PANEL 2 — The first guess
**Image:** Yvonne calls Rasmus, whom she remembers having had a job “in that area” in the morning. Rasmus answers from the driver’s seat and looks confused out through the window.  
**Caption:** “I’m actually still in Nyköping — that’s quite a way from there.”  
**Status:** 🔴 Red — wrong person, wrong place

## PANEL 3 — The second guess
**Image:** Yvonne calls Daniel instead. He is not completely sure where he is in relation to the bakery’s address and checks his own mobile map while speaking.  
**Caption:** “Wait, let me check... I think I’m about twenty minutes away?”  
**Status:** 🔴 Red — even the technician himself is guessing

## PANEL 4 — The clock is ticking at Torgny’s
**Image:** Torgny Blad in the bakery looks anxiously at a thermometer on one of the chillers, already creeping towards a warning threshold. Staff hurry trays of dough out.  
**Caption:** “Every five minutes without power was five minutes closer to losing a day’s production.”  
**Status:** 🔴 Red — real financial damage is accumulating while they wait

## PANEL 5 — The wrong technician is sent
**Image:** Yvonne makes a decision and sends Daniel, who turns out to be much farther away than she thought. The van is shown driving off on the inefficient route relative to the bakery, indicated with a dashed line on a simple map sketch in the panel.  
**Caption:** “Forty-five minutes, instead of the ten it could have been.”  
**Status:** 🔴 Red — the guess became expensive

## PANEL 6 — Torgny, angry and disappointed
**Image:** Torgny stands with his arms crossed in the doorway when Daniel finally arrives. A crate of discarded dough is visible on a bench behind him.  
**Caption:** “Forty-five minutes. You didn’t even know where your own vans were.”  
**Status:** 🔴 Red — trust badly damaged

## PANEL 7 — Micke remembers the map
**Image:** Micke passes Yvonne’s desk after the difficult call and casually mentions that he has seen a map view in the system where the vans are already shown with their positions — he simply never had a reason to look at it until now.  
**Caption:** “The vans are already visible on the map. Why did you call around instead of looking?”  
**Status:** 🟠 Orange — turning point

## PANEL 8 — The map Yvonne had never opened
**Image:** For the first time, Yvonne opens a map view on her computer where all service vans are shown as markers with clearly updated reported positions. A discreet Navichain logo appears in the app header.  
**Caption:** “There they were. All nine, with their actual reported locations — not a guess in sight.”  
**Status:** 🟢 Green — actual reported position, visible for the first time

## PANEL 9 — The next emergency call
**Image:** Another urgent call comes in, this time from a different customer. Yvonne looks straight at the map instead of calling around and sees one marker clearly closest to the address.  
**Caption:** “No round of calls this time. Just one glance at the map.”  
**Status:** 🟢 Green — the decision takes seconds, not calls

## PANEL 10 — Rasmus, actually closest this time
**Image:** Rasmus in his van; his phone pings with a newly assigned job. The map glimpsed on his phone shows his own position near the new address.  
**Caption:** “This time there was no guess — Rasmus really was closest.”  
**Status:** 🟢 Green — the right technician, for the first time

## PANEL 11 — Fast arrival, situation saved
**Image:** Rasmus arrives quickly at the new customer. The customer looks relieved and impressed. A small Navichain sticker is discreetly visible on the service van’s door.  
**Caption:** “Ten minutes, not forty-five. This time nothing had time to be lost.”  
**Status:** 🟢 Green — the emergency job is solved in time

## PANEL 12 — The map becomes a habit
**Image:** Yvonne in the office. Once the caller has given the address, her eyes routinely move to the map view. Through the window, one of the vans is seen driving away. On the desk lies a small note with a sketch of a clock and several stars — a teaser for the next story.  
**Caption:** “The map became the first thing she looked at — not the last thing she wished she had.”  
**Status:** 🟢 Green — ending + teaser

## Product terminology and grounding

Grounded in verified Navichain documentation (NAVICHAIN-PRODUCT-TRUTH.md):

- **Fleet Map:** Shows trucks/trailers whose linked reporting device has reported a position, with search/selection/location, marker status/type and run/ETA data where available. Auto refresh is documented as 30 seconds and can be disabled. A reported position is required for a vehicle to appear on the map.
- **Service vans as vehicles in the system:** In this story, Kron Elteknik’s service vans are represented by the same vehicle entity used for trucks in the core documentation, with linked reporting telematics. This is a story configuration assumption, not a claim that every Navichain tenant is configured this way.
- **Human decision boundary:** The story does not claim that the map automatically proposes or assigns the nearest technician. Yvonne makes the assessment and assignment herself based on the vehicle positions the map displays.

**Explicit limitation (what is NOT claimed):** The story does not claim zero-latency real-time updates — the documentation describes polling and therefore allows a short delay rather than immediate push. Nor does it claim that Navichain autonomously dispatches the nearest technician.
