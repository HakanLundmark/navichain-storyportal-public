# — A Navichain story —

# NOT BAD LUCK. A PATTERN.
### Sara and the carrier that was always late "just this once"

**Format:** 12 panels + cover concept. Illustrator brief.

**Angle:** The twenty-eighth episode in the series about connected workflows at Björklunda Åkeri AB returns to Sara Lund, who earlier in the series demonstrated transparent matching of external carriers when the company's own vehicles were fully booked (episode 4). This time, it is not about choosing the right carrier for a single assignment, but about discovering that one of the carriers she has repeatedly hired actually delivers late far more often than it has felt like "just bad luck" — a pattern that is invisible when each delay is considered on its own, but obvious as soon as the bookings are placed side by side.

**Source:** Navichain — Bookings (verified: the bookings list can be filtered by, among other things, carrier, workflow status and pickup/delivery date ranges, and the selection is preserved across pages. Find Best Match scores favourites, working day, contract/insurance, ADR, capacity, service and zones, as well as lead time — the user makes the selection.) The story is deliberately restrained: it does **not** claim that Navichain automatically detects or warns about patterns of late deliveries from a specific carrier — Sara is the one who filters the information, sees the picture herself and draws the conclusion. What she then does about it — adjusting the carrier's favourite status — is also an active human choice, not an automatic downgrade.

**Important narrative instruction:** Navichain never flags the carrier as "bad" for Sara. The system only shows the actual dates and statuses in a list — Sara is the one who sees the pattern, draws the conclusion and acts. No IT department appears.

## Cover concept

Sara is sitting at her desk with a pile of old handwritten notes about different delays — each note has its own apparently unrelated reason: "traffic jam", "illness", "wrong address". Beside them, on the screen, there is instead a filtered list in which the same carrier's name is repeated row after row, all with red "Overdue" markings in the same column. Sara's expression moves from surprise to insight. Title typography in the series' established rust-red/petroleum-green combination.

---

## VISUAL STYLE (applies to all panels)

Warm 1950s aesthetic in Hergé/Tintin *ligne claire*: clean contour lines of even thickness, flat colour fields without halftones or heavy hatching. Parchment/cream-coloured base with rust red, mustard yellow, petroleum green and warm gold. Captions hand-lettered, never digital sans-serif. Physical props (loose notes with excuses, a coffee mug, a clipboard) take priority over sharp UI mock-ups. Lightly grainy offset-print texture with patina, soft edge wear and foxing.

**Recurring motif:** the loose notes with different excuses for delays dominate the early panels, each one isolated and apparently unrelated. From the turning point onwards, they are visually brought together into one clear list — the same information, but finally seen together.

**Logo rule:** Navichain's official logo is completely absent in panels 1–6. It is introduced discreetly from panel 7 — as a small icon in the corner of the bookings-list filter view, a logo on Sara's tablet, and finally on her coffee mug in the last panel. Never centred, never more than about 5% of the panel area, never advertisement-like. *The official logo is uploaded as a source file in the project — the illustrator must use that file at every specified placement, not recreate it by hand.*

**No IT support:** No support person or IT department appears anywhere. Sara discovers and resolves everything herself.

## PANEL 1 — Another excuse
**Visual:** Sara takes a call from Nordtrans: "The delay was caused by a traffic jam, these things happen." She writes it on a note and places it on an already growing pile.
**Text:** "Traffic jam. It sounded reasonable. On its own."
**Status:** 🔴 Red (isolated event, no suspicion yet)

## PANEL 2 — The pile grows
**Visual:** Sara looks through her pile of loose notes — "illness", "wrong address", "traffic jam" again, "waiting time at loading" — each with Nordtrans's name in the corner, but never compared with one another.
**Text:** "Five notes. Five different reasons. None of them connected to the others — yet."
**Status:** 🔴 Red (pattern invisible)

## PANEL 3 — A customer gets in touch
**Visual:** The phone rings again — this time it is a customer, slightly irritated: "This is the third time this month your carrier has been late to us." Sara falls silent for a moment.
**Text:** "'The third time.' The customer had counted. Sara had not."
**Status:** 🔴 Red (external pressure, insight beginning to form)

## PANEL 4 — She decides to check
**Visual:** Sara puts down the phone, looks at her pile of notes with fresh eyes, then opens the bookings list in Navichain on her tablet.
**Text:** "If it was a pattern, it ought to be visible — if only she looked the right way."
**Status:** 🟠 Orange (turning point — decision to investigate)

## PANEL 5 — The filter is set
**Visual:** Sara sets a filter: carrier "Nordtrans", date range covering the last three months, status. The list starts to take shape.
**Text:** "One carrier. Three months. One single filter."
**Status:** 🟠 Orange (investigation)

## PANEL 6 — The list reveals itself
**Visual:** The screen fills with rows — the same carrier name again and again, several with clear "Overdue" or late-delivery markings in the same column. Navichain's official logo is visible discreetly in the corner of the view.
**Text:** "Seven delays in three months. No longer five notes — one clear row after another."
**Status:** 🟢 Green (the pattern becomes visible)

## PANEL 7 — The insight lands
**Visual:** Sara leans back, astonished but not deeply surprised — as though something she had sensed for a long time has finally acquired a face.
**Text:** "It was never bad luck. It was a pattern all along."
**Status:** 🟢 Green (clarity)

## PANEL 8 — The call with Nordtrans
**Visual:** Sara calls Nordtrans, calm but firm, with the list open on the screen in front of her as she speaks. "Seven delays in three months. We need to talk about what is happening."
**Text:** "She did not need to guess or accuse. The figures spoke for themselves."
**Status:** 🟢 Green (matter-of-fact confrontation)

## PANEL 9 — An active choice
**Visual:** Sara opens the carrier's profile and changes its favourite status — not removing it entirely, but lowering its priority in future matches.
**Text:** "She removed the favourite mark. Not as punishment — as an honest update."
**Status:** 🟢 Green (active decision, not automation)

## PANEL 10 — The next match
**Visual:** The next time an external carrier is needed, the Find Best Match list shows Nordtrans lower down, with another carrier higher up and a clear, transparent score explaining why.
**Text:** "Next time, the list suggested someone else at the top. For good, visible reasons."
**Status:** 🟢 Green (consequence, transparent)

## PANEL 11 — The pile of notes disappears
**Visual:** Sara gathers up her old pile of loose notes and puts them in the waste-paper basket — they are no longer needed; the list does the same job better.
**Text:** "The notes could go. The list remembered better than they ever had."
**Status:** 🟢 Green (symbolic conclusion)

## PANEL 12 — One month later
**Visual:** Sara leans back with her coffee mug, where Navichain's official logo is visible discreetly. The phone is quiet — no customer has called to complain about delays this month.
**Text:** "A whole month. No call about a delay that should have been visible earlier."
**Status:** 🟢 Green (conclusion, clear point)

## Production notes for the illustrator

- **The pile of notes** is the episode's clearest visual motif: scattered and unrelated in panels 1–3, brought together as one clear list in panel 6, removed in panel 11.
- **Colour dramaturgy:** Red dominates panels 1–3 (isolated events, pattern invisible), orange is the turning point (panels 4–5), petroleum green dominates panels 6–12.
- **Panel 8 is the story's matter-of-fact core:** Sara's tone towards Nordtrans should be drawn as calm and professional, not accusatory — she confronts with facts, not emotions.
- **Nordtrans never appears as a person in the image** — only as a voice on the phone or a name in the list, in line with the series' previous treatment of external counterparties.
- **Physical props to prioritise:** the handwritten excuse notes, the clipboard on Sara's desk, the coffee mug with a discreet logo in the final panel.
