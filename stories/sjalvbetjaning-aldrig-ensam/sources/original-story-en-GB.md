# — A Navichain story —

# SELF-SERVICE, NEVER ALONE

### Anette, Sara and the booking that stopped for an extra look

**Angle:** The thirteenth episode in the series about connected workflows meets a customer directly, for the first time since Viktor’s episode about system integration — but this time it is not about a business system, but about a person booking her own transports herself. The task is unique because it requires two things that can easily be seen as opposites: genuine self-service, and a guarantee that nothing still slips through unnoticed.

**Source:** Navichain — Customer Portal (verified: separate self-service app with order creation, editing only in New status, clone function, tracking, invoice preview and price estimate when enabled. Portal-created orders are automatically flagged “Requires Review” for staff. Public tracking requires no login and can show timeline, POD photo/signature and package detail.)

## Cast

| Character | Role | Company |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Anette Malm** *(new protagonist)* | Logistics coordinator, customer | Kustlogistik AB |
| **Sara Lund** *(returning)* | Dispatcher — reviews flagged portal bookings | Björklunda Åkeri AB |

Anette represents the customer side of the series for the first time in a long while: someone who wants to be able to book quickly, herself, without having to call — but who still wants to know that someone actually looks at what she submits.

## Cover concept

A divided spread: on the left, Anette at her computer, clicking through a simple booking form in the customer portal. On the right, Sara at Björklunda, where the same booking appears in a review queue with a clear, discreet flag beside it. A thin line connects the two sides without being a literal cable. Title typography in the same rust-red/petroleum-green combination as the other covers in the series.

---

## PANEL 1 — The phone, every time

**Visual:** Anette calls Björklunda to book yet another transport and waits in the phone queue. Sara on the other side is in the middle of another call and cannot answer immediately. **Text:** “One call, for every booking. Neither of them has time to wait for that any more.” **Status:** 🔴 Red — every booking requires a phone call

## PANEL 2 — Missed time window

**Visual:** Anette hangs up after finally reaching Sara — but the delivery window she needed is already tighter than it should have been, simply because the call was delayed by half an hour. **Text:** “Half an hour of waiting became a tighter time window than necessary.” **Status:** 🔴 Red — time lost in the booking process itself

## PANEL 3 — Wanting something faster

**Visual:** Anette sighs and wishes for a way to book directly, herself — but hesitates at the same time. Would a completely automated system really catch it if she happened to enter something incorrectly? **Text:** “Faster, yes. But who catches it if she happens to enter something wrong, if no one is looking any more?” **Status:** 🔴 Red — speed and reassurance feel like opposites

## PANEL 4 — The story she heard

**Visual:** A short, suggested flashback: Anette tells a colleague about another company where a fully automated booking went wrong without anyone noticing until the goods were already in the wrong place. **Text:** “‘No one saw it until it was already too late,’ the colleague said. Anette never quite forgot it.” **Status:** 🔴 Red — the concern about being completely without human oversight

## PANEL 5 — The turning point: the portal that still sees

**Visual:** Anette gets access to Björklunda’s customer portal and creates her next booking herself, directly on screen — no call, no waiting time. **Text:** “The same booking. No queue, no call — just a form she fills in herself.” **Status:** 🟠 Orange — self-service, direct

## PANEL 6 — The flag that appears

**Visual:** Anette enters unusually large dimensions for one of the packages. The moment she saves the booking, a small, clear flag appears: “Sent for review by Björklunda.” Nothing moves on silently. **Text:** “She entered it herself. But this time it stopped — for an extra look.” **Status:** 🟠 Orange — self-service, but never unobserved

## PANEL 7 — Sara sees it, straight away

**Visual:** Sara at Björklunda sees Anette’s booking appear in her review queue, clearly marked. She opens it and immediately notices the unusual dimensions. A discreet sticker with Navichain’s official logo is visible in the corner of the queue. **Text:** “One booking, one flag, one real person who actually sees it.” **Status:** 🟢 Green — a human eye, exactly where it is needed

## PANEL 8 — The correction, before it becomes a problem

**Visual:** Sara sees that the dimensions require a larger vehicle than the one that would otherwise have been sent. She adjusts the plan and sends a short comment back to Anette explaining why. **Text:** “Not an error discovered at loading. An error caught before it even becomes one.” **Status:** 🟢 Green — the problem solved before it arose

## PANEL 9 — Anette gets a quick answer

**Visual:** Anette sees the confirmation arrive in the portal, together with Sara’s short explanation about the adjusted vehicle. Faster than a phone call would have been — and with the same reassurance as before. **Text:** “Faster than a call. And still someone who actually cared enough to check.” **Status:** 🟢 Green — speed and reassurance, both at the same time

## PANEL 10 — The ordinary bookings, unobstructed

**Visual:** The same day, Anette creates two more completely ordinary bookings. They go through without getting stuck in any queue — review reserved for what actually deviates, not for everything. **Text:** “Most of it goes straight through. Only what stands out stops for a look.” **Status:** 🟢 Green — review where it is actually needed, not everywhere

## PANEL 11 — Tracking, without login

**Visual:** Anette shares a tracking link with her own customer without the customer having to log in anywhere — the timeline, POD photo and signature are clearly visible when the delivery is complete. **Text:** “Her own customer can see all the way through — no account, no password, just the link.” **Status:** 🟢 Green — openness without hassle for everyone involved

## PANEL 12 — Anette, finally at ease

**Visual:** Anette leans back at her computer, coffee cup in hand. Through a small circled inset, Sara can be seen at Björklunda, equally calm, the review queue almost empty for the day. **Text:** “She books by herself now. But she is still never completely alone with it.” **Status:** 🟢 Green — the chain complete, from waiting time to trusted self-service

**Post-it on the edge of Anette’s screen (teaser):** *“Next: the driver without coverage — and why the app still keeps track of everything.”*

## Illustrator reference

| Element | Instruction |
| --- | --- |
| **Style** | Warm 1950s tone, Hergé/Tintin *ligne claire*: even contour line, flat colour fields, no heavy screen tone. Discreet offset grain, patina, soft edge wear. |
| **Colour dramaturgy** | Panels 1–4: rust red, waiting and hesitation on both sides of the phone line. Panels 5–6: rich orange, the form opens a new route, the flag appears clearly. Panels 7–12: petroleum green, fast and reassuring on both sides. |
| **Anette Malm** | Practical office clothing at the customer, a different visual tone from Björklunda’s internal characters so she clearly reads as “outside” the company. Impatient/waiting in the red panels, relaxed in the green ones. |
| **Divided composition** | Recurring compositional motif: let Anette’s and Sara’s sides of the screen/room be visually distinguishable but clearly connected — especially in panels 6–9 where the same booking moves between them. |
| **The review flag** | It should be a clear, small and readable element — a flag or marker, not a large warning sign that looks like an error. It signals care, not a problem. |
| **Important narrative instruction** | No panel may suggest that all portal bookings are manually reviewed, or that review is an obstacle to self-service — only that what deviates is caught, while the rest goes through unobstructed (see especially panel 10). |
| **Sara Lund** | Same recurring visual design as in previous episodes. |
| **Navichain’s official logo** | Use the uploaded source file exactly as it is — never recreate it by hand. Absent in panels 1–4. Introduced discreetly from panel 7 (corner of the queue) and then visible on a coffee mug in the final panel — never centred, never advertising-like, maximum about 5% of the panel area. |
| **No IT support** | No external technician, no support case on either side. Both Anette and Sara solve everything themselves through the interface — Navichain is self-explanatory throughout the story. |

## Continuity note

The thirteenth episode in the series about connected workflows, and the first since Viktor’s episode to deliberately show Navichain from the customer side of the screen. Anette Malm is introduced as a new customer persona, clearly distinct from previous customer angles in the series (Sofia Wallin and Sofia Ahlgren’s focus on fewer incoming calls, Elin’s paperless customer journey): here the point is specifically the tension between self-service and oversight, and how both can exist at the same time. Sara Lund returns in a supporting role that links the customer’s side and the carrier’s side of the same booking.

**Next in the series:** The driver without coverage — how Navichain’s mobile app continues to work even when a driver temporarily lacks network coverage, with queued updates sent as soon as contact returns. An angle not yet used in the series.
