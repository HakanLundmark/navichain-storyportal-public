# — A Navichain story —

# THE ADDRESS THAT WASN'T ALLOWED TO MOVE
### Nils and the change that almost stirred everything else up

**Format:** 12 panels + cover concept. Illustrator brief.

**Angle:** The thirtieth episode in the series about connected workflows at Björklunda Åkeri AB covers a last-minute change from a customer — but *not* the same kind of safeguard as Görel Ask's earlier story about locked booking statuses (episode 11, BookingLockOverride). This time the protection is different and more deceptive to understand: once an address has been used on a booking, its coordinates are locked, specifically to prevent an apparently harmless correction to a single delivery from accidentally moving an entire hub that several *other*, already planned runs also rely on.

**Source:** Navichain — Organizations (verified: once an address has been used on a booking, its coordinates are locked to prevent rerouting existing work.) The story is deliberately restrained: it does **not** claim that Navichain automatically recognises or warns about *every* conceivable address conflict in advance — it is Nils who understands why the lock exists and solves the customer's actual need in the right way: by adding a new, correct address for the specific delivery instead of forcing a move of the shared address's coordinates.

**Important narrative instruction:** Navichain never moves a coordinate silently, nor does it let Nils accidentally reroute other people's planned runs. No IT department appears. Nils resolves the whole situation himself, by understanding *why* the safeguard exists rather than seeing it as an obstacle to get around.

## Cover concept

Nils stands in front of a large wall map with several coloured pins, one of them marked with a small padlock icon. A customer has asked him to move that particular pin a few hundred metres to "correct" an address — but beside the locked pin are thin threads connecting it to several other pins, as though he has just realised how much actually depends on it. Title typography in the series' established rust-red/petroleum-green combination.

---

## VISUAL STYLE (applies to all panels)

Warm 1950s aesthetic in Hergé/Tintin *ligne claire*: clean contour lines of even thickness, flat colour fields without halftones or heavy hatching. Parchment/cream base with rust red, mustard yellow, petroleum green and warm gold. Captions hand-lettered, never digital sans-serif. Physical props (the wall map with pins and threads, a coffee cup, a notebook) are prioritised over sharp UI mock-ups. Lightly grainy offset-print texture with patina, soft edge wear and foxing.

**Recurring motif:** the wall map with pins and connecting threads is the episode's central visual motif — a physical representation of how a single address invisibly holds several different runs together. The threads become more clearly visible as Nils understands the connection.

**Logo rule:** Navichain's official logo is completely absent from panels 1–6. It is introduced discreetly from panel 7 — as a small icon in the corner of the address view, a logo on Nils's tablet, and finally on his coffee cup in the last panel. Never centred, never more than about 5% of the panel area, never advertisement-like. *The official logo is uploaded as a source file in the project — the illustrator must use that file at every specified placement, not recreate it by hand.*

**No IT support:** No support person or IT department appears anywhere. Nils solves everything himself.

**Status:** 🔴 Red (unexpected obstacle)

**Visual:** Nils leans back, slightly irritated, as though the system is getting in the way of something that ought to be simple.

## PANEL 5 — The wall map
**Visual:** Nils gets up, walks to the large wall map with pins, finds the same address marked there — and for the first time sees the thin threads connecting it to several other planned runs.
**Text:** "Then he saw the map. The address wasn't a single pin. It was a hub."
**Status:** 🟠 Orange (turning point — understanding)

## PANEL 6 — He understands why
**Visual:** Nils quietly counts the other pins connected to the same address — three other customers' deliveries, all planned from that very coordinate.
**Text:** "If he had moved it, three other deliveries would have moved with it — without anyone asking for that."
**Status:** 🟠 Orange (insight into the purpose of the protection)

## PANEL 7 — The right way forward
**Visual:** Nils opens Navichain on his tablet again and instead adds a new, separate address line specifically for the customer's current delivery. Navichain's official logo appears discreetly in the corner of the view.
**Text:** "He created a new address instead of moving the old one."
**Status:** 🟢 Green (correct solution)

## PANEL 8 — The customer's delivery is adjusted
**Visual:** Nils links the new, correct address to that customer's current booking, while all other bookings remain completely untouched.
**Text:** "The customer's delivery got the right address. Nobody else's disappeared or moved."
**Status:** 🟢 Green (precision)

## PANEL 9 — He calls back
**Visual:** Nils calls the customer back and calmly explains: "Your new address has been added for future deliveries. The old one stayed where it was for a reason — other work depended on it too."
**Text:** "He explained it, instead of just solving it silently."
**Status:** 🟢 Green (transparency)

## PANEL 10 — The customer understands
**Visual:** Over the phone, the customer's voice sounds pleased rather than annoyed: "Ah, I didn't know that. Good that you added a new one instead."
**Text:** "The customer hadn't asked to disrupt anyone else. She simply hadn't known."
**Status:** 🟢 Green (trust)

## PANEL 11 — The threads, untouched
**Visual:** Nils looks back at the wall map — all the threads remain exactly where they were, untouched, while a new pin has been placed beside them for the customer's new address.
**Text:** "Everything else stayed exactly where it was supposed to."
**Status:** 🟢 Green (symbolic conclusion)

## PANEL 12 — Coffee and calm
**Visual:** Nils leans against the desk with his coffee cup, where Navichain's official logo appears discreetly, and looks contentedly at the map with all the threads intact.
**Text:** "An address that wasn't allowed to move. And everything else that stayed exactly as it should."
**Status:** 🟢 Green (ending, clear point)

## Production notes for the illustrator

- **The wall map with threads** should be drawn clearly in panels 5–6 and 11 — the threads invisible/indistinct earlier in the story, then noticeable once Nils "sees" the connection.
- **Colour dramaturgy:** Red dominates panels 1–4 (false simplicity, unexpected obstacle, brief frustration), orange is the turning point (panels 5–6), petroleum green dominates panels 7–12.
- **The padlock icon in panel 3** should be drawn calmly and matter-of-factly, not as an alarming error message — in keeping with the series' self-explanatory tone.
- **The difference from episode 11 should be clear in tone:** Görel's episode was about a strict, formal safeguard requiring a stated reason; this one should feel more like an explanatory "aha" — a protection that becomes obviously logical as soon as the whole picture is visible.
- **Physical props to prioritise:** the wall map with pins and threads, the notebook, the coffee cup with a discreet logo in the last panel.
