# — A Navichain story —

# THE LIST THAT SHOWS ITS REASONS

### Sara and the matching that explains itself

**Angle:** The fourth episode in the series about connected workflows leaves the internal warehouse and the customer's ERP and moves into traffic management: choosing which external carrier should handle a job that the haulage company's own vehicles cannot fit in. The task is unique because it requires several simultaneous criteria — availability, ADR qualification, insurance, zone, lead time, favourite status — to be weighed against one another in seconds, under time pressure. Important for this story: Navichain never chooses automatically for the traffic planner. The system shows a transparent, scored list with clear reasons and warnings — Sara still makes the decision.

**Source:** Navichain — Carriers / Find Best Match (verified: scores favourites, operating days, contract/insurance, ADR, capacity, service and zones, as well as lead time; the user chooses. Marketing-safe wording: "Compare carrier matches with transparent reasons and warnings" — **not** "automatically assigns jobs to the best carrier". Carrier cost is still entered manually on the run.)

Sara works at the same company as Kenneth, Frida and Oskar from earlier episodes, but in a role none of them has had: the person who must decide every day which external carrier gets a job when the company's own vehicles are fully booked — a decision that traditionally relied on memory and gut feeling rather than comparable facts.

## Cover concept

Sara at her desk, eyes fixed on a screen where several carrier names are listed, each with a clear score and a small row of icons beside it — not numbers in a black box, but visible reasons. Behind her, blurred in the background, an old whiteboard with crossed-out phone numbers remains as a memory of how things used to be done. Title lettering in the same rust-red/petroleum-green combination as the other covers in the series.

## PANEL 1 — The job that does not fit

**Visual:** Sara looks at the run list: all of the company's own vehicles are already booked for the day; a new urgent job — a drum of dangerous goods — needs an external carrier within two hours. **Text:** "Own vehicles: fully booked. The clock: ticking. The right carrier: still unknown." **Status:** 🔴 Red — no capacity, time pressure mounting

## PANEL 2 — The list in her head

**Visual:** Sara leafs through a notebook with twelve carrier names, some underlined, some with an old question mark beside them. No clear order, no common yardstick. **Text:** "Twelve names. None of them ranked by anything Sara can actually rely on right now." **Status:** 🔴 Red — no comparable basis for the choice

## PANEL 3 — The habitual choice

**Visual:** Sara calls the carrier she "always uses". The call ends in an awkward pause — the carrier is unsure whether their ADR qualification still applies to this particular type of goods. **Text:** "The habitual choice, not the right choice. And neither of them knows for certain." **Status:** 🔴 Red — the wrong carrier almost booked

## PANEL 4 — The clock moves, the phone goes quiet

**Visual:** Sara calls the next name on the list — no answer. The next one. Busy. The wall clock has crept closer to the deadline. She puts a hand to her forehead. **Text:** "Four calls. One hour left. Still nobody who definitely fits." **Status:** 🔴 Red — time pressure without reliable information

## PANEL 5 — The turning point: a list with reasons

**Visual:** Sara opens "Find Best Match" directly on the booking in Navichain. A short, scored list of carriers appears — every row with a clear icon line: ✅ ADR, ✅ Available today, ⚠️ Insurance expires in 6 days. **Text:** "No guessing. A list, and beside every name — exactly why." **Status:** 🟠 Orange — transparent reasons, not a black box

## PANEL 6 — The warning that appears in time

**Visual:** Sara moves the mouse/finger over the carrier she almost chose in panel 3 — the same warning icon about the ADR qualification expiring soon is clearly visible in the list, this time before she calls. **Text:** "The same carrier that almost became the wrong choice. Now the warning is visible — before the call, not after." **Status:** 🟠 Orange — the risk is visible before the decision, not after

## PANEL 7 — Sara's own decision

**Visual:** Sara reads through the three highest-scoring rows, compares the reasons with one another and clicks the one she judges best herself — the system has not chosen for her, it has only made the choice informed. A discreet sticker with Navichain's official logo is visible in the corner of the list view. **Text:** "The system suggests. Sara chooses. Still her decision — just no longer her guess." **Status:** 🟢 Green — informed decision, not automatic decision

## PANEL 8 — Confirmed in twenty minutes

**Visual:** Sara sends the booking request to the selected carrier directly from the list. A confirmation arrives almost immediately; the run is created with the right carrier linked. **Text:** "Twenty minutes, not two hours. And the right carrier from the start." **Status:** 🟢 Green — run confirmed and created

## PANEL 9 — The cost is still hers to set

**Visual:** Sara manually fills in the carrier cost on the run — one field, no automatic price. She nods to herself; it is one thing the system deliberately leaves with her. **Text:** "The matching is automatic. She still sets the cost herself, line by line." **Status:** 🟢 Green — the right things automated, the right things left with the human

## PANEL 10 — Dangerous goods, the right qualification

**Visual:** The dangerous-goods drum is loaded by the selected carrier's driver, with the ADR plate clearly visible on the vehicle. No uncertainty, no "we'll check it later". **Text:** "The right qualification, confirmed before the load even rolled out through the gate." **Status:** 🟢 Green — the right carrier, the right goods, no risk left

## PANEL 11 — Robban in the rear-view mirror

**Visual:** Robban Ek passes Sara in the corridor on his way out to his own regular run, nodding towards her screen where the list with reasons is still visible. A brief, silent gesture of recognition between colleagues. **Text:** "The same tool, different jobs — but never a guessing game anymore." **Status:** 🟢 Green — the same confidence regardless of who drives

## PANEL 12 — The calm of the afternoon

**Visual:** Sara leans back, holding a coffee mug with Navichain's official logo, while the whiteboard with the crossed-out phone numbers is now only a faded memory in the background. **Text:** "Twelve names in her head became a list with reasons. The decision is still hers — just never blind anymore." **Status:** 🟢 Green — the chain complete, from panic to a confident choice

**Post-it on the edge of Sara's screen (teaser):** *"Next: the weather that is already visible on the map — before it has time to become a problem on the road."*

| **Style** | Warm 1950s tone, Hergé/Tintin *ligne claire*: even contour line, flat colour fields, no heavy raster. Discreet offset grain, patina, soft edge wear. |
| **Colour dramaturgy** | Panels 1–4: rust-red, tight office light under time pressure. Panels 5–6: rich orange, the list opens up the image area. Panels 7–12: petroleum green, airy and reassuring light. |

| **The list as a visual motif** | Recurring compositional motif: the carrier list must always be shown with clear, readable icons (tick/warning triangle) beside every name — never only an anonymous score. The score is an aid, not a verdict. |
| **Important narrative rule** | No panel may imply that the system chooses or books automatically for Sara. She clicks, she chooses, she enters the cost. The matching explains — it does not decide. |

## Continuity note

The fourth episode in the series about connected workflows, and the first that deliberately avoids suggesting automated decision-making — an important nuance compared with the more seamless automation in earlier episodes (purchase orders, stock levels, API sync). Here the point is instead that the tool is transparent rather than autonomous: it explains, the user chooses. Sara Lund is introduced as a new traffic-planner persona at the same company as Kenneth, Frida and Oskar, reinforcing Björklunda Åkeri AB as the series' recurring home for the internal chain of episodes. Robban Ek continues his established function as the series' connecting figure.

**Next in the series:** Weather on the map — how Navichain's weather map (severity, temperature, wind, precipitation on a three-hour timeline about five days ahead) lets a traffic planner see the risk before it becomes a problem on the road. A pure display/reporting function, not automatic replanning — another unused angle in the series.
