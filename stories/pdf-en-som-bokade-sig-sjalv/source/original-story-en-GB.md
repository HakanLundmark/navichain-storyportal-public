# A navichain story

# THE PDF THAT BOOKED ITSELF

### Sandra & navichain's Magic Drop order intake

**Tagline (cover):** Every order email was a copy-and-paste task. Until one of them wasn't.

**Source:** demosite.navichain.se/plattformen/orderhantering/ — angle: **AI order intake (Magic Drop)** — "navichain-exclusive": the AI reads PDF/email, interprets sender, recipient, addresses, package count, weight and special instructions, and automatically creates a booking proposal. The user reviews and approves — the system never guesses silently; uncertain fields are flagged for manual review.

**Angle note for Håkan:** The order management page has several functions that have already touched the series in passing (Magic Drop appeared briefly as a secondary panel moment in Helen's story about four disconnected systems, and package handling/barcodes as well as the customer portal's delivery status have their own stories). This story is the first to make **Magic Drop the entire main conflict** — not planning, not invoicing, not package tracking, but the very moment when an incoming order document becomes a booking. New protagonist, new work role (order administrator, not dispatcher or finance manager), new feeling: the exhaustion of being a human copying machine between inbox and TMS.

**Main character:** Sandra Lindkvist, 34 — order administrator, Holmqvist Fjärrtransport AB (22 vehicles, freight and groupage transport in central Sweden). Dark-blonde hair in a ponytail, glasses, knitted cardigan over a blouse. Sits at an inbox that never stops.

**Supporting character:** Robban Ek — driver (established continuity character in the series), appears in the final panels when a Magic Drop booking lands directly in his driver app.

**Supporting characters (minor roles):** Torbjörn Holm, operations manager (Sandra's manager, seen on the phone/in the background), a customer who calls and wonders where the goods have gone.

**Format:** 12-panel illustrator brief + cover concept.

---

## Visual style (for the illustrator — series standard)

- Warm 1950s style in Hergé/Tintin "ligne claire": evenly thick black contour lines, flat colour areas without halftone shading or heavy crosshatching.
- Parchment/cream-white base, palette in rust red, mustard yellow, petroleum green and warm gold.
- Hand-lettered captions — never digital sans-serif.
- Light grainy offset-print texture with patina: subtle raster grain, paper texture, soft edge wear/foxing, muted and slightly faded tones — like a well-thumbed 1950s magazine from a chest of drawers.
- Physical props (stamps, coffee mugs, stapler, binders, cork noticeboard) are preferred to UI mockups. The story takes place in the present day, but the visual idiom is always that of the 1950s — retro CRT screens/period-shaped screens where screens are unavoidable.
- The characters are warm, expressive and sympathetic throughout, genuinely happy and smiling in the solution panels.
- **No IT support appears in any panel.** Sandra navigates navichain entirely on her own — no consultant, no support line, no training day. The system is self-explanatory, just as in the other stories in the series.
- **From paper to screen:** In the problem panels (1–6), printed order emails, Post-it notes, binders and a pen that is constantly moving dominate. From panel 7 onward, this is progressively replaced by computer and mobile phone — Sandra no longer copies things by hand; she drags, clicks and approves digitally.

### Logo rules (series standard)

- It is always called **"navichain's official logo"** — never "the navichain logo", "the N mark", "N logotype", "N symbol" or "N monogram".
- **Completely absent in panels 1–6** — deliberately, to reinforce the contrast between before and after.
- Introduced discreetly from panel 7 onward, as environmental props: the corner of a screen, a coffee mug, a bag, a vehicle decal, a receipt.
- Maximum approximately 5% of the panel area. Never centred, never advertising-like.
- The official logo file is uploaded as a source file in the project. The illustrator must use that file exactly as it is, at every specified placement — never an invented, interpreted or stylised substitute.

## Panel 1

**Visual description:** Sandra at her desk early in the morning, surrounded by a pile of printed order emails and PDF printouts. The screen behind her shows an overflowing email inbox window. She sighs and picks up the first sheet. **Caption:** Monday, 07:52. Forty-three new order requests. None of them has entered itself into the system yet. **Status:** 🔴 Red — the workload is waiting

## Panel 2

**Visual description:** Close-up of Sandra's hands copying information from a PDF order email, line by line, into an Excel-like booking form: sender, recipient, address, package count, weight. **Caption:** Address, weight, package count — the same information, copied by hand, order after order. **Status:** 🔴 Red — manual duplication

## Panel 3

**Visual description:** Sandra scratches her head, looking between two windows/papers — an address looks strange, the numbers are hard to read in a blurry PDF fax. The wall clock shows that hours have passed. **Caption:** "Is it 1,200 kg or 1,700 kg? The writing is terrible in this scan." **Status:** 🔴 Red — uncertain information, no one to ask

## Panel 4

**Visual description:** The phone rings. Sandra answers with a tense expression. A speech bubble: a customer wonders why the goods that were supposed to be collected this morning are still there — the booking never made it into the system. **Caption:** "You were supposed to collect at 09:00. Where is the truck?" — an order disappeared in the pile. **Status:** 🔴 Red — the consequence of manual entry

## Panel 5

**Visual description:** Sandra hangs up, leans back with her hands over her face. The clock shows 17:40. The pile of untreated PDF orders is still high despite a full working day. **Caption:** A whole day. Still fourteen orders left in the pile. **Status:** 🔴 Red — exhaustion, no end in sight

## Panel 6

**Visual description:** Torbjörn (the operations manager) comes by Sandra's desk, sees the pile, sees Sandra's exhausted look. He sits on the edge of the desk with his phone in his hand and shows her something on the screen. **Caption:** "Sandra. We don't need to copy orders by hand anymore. Look at this." **Status:** 🔴 Red — the final straw, but a solution is glimpsed

## Panel 7

**Visual description:** Transition panel in mustard-yellow light. Sandra is now sitting at the computer (not the paper pile) and drags a PDF order email straight into navichain's interface with the mouse pointer. An AI icon lights up in the corner. navichain's official logo is visible discreetly in the browser tab header — the first time in the story. **Caption:** She drags in the document. Magic Drop starts reading. **Status:** 🟠 Orange — turning point, Magic Drop is tested

## Panel 8

**Visual description:** The screen shows how the fields fill themselves in, one by one: sender ✓, recipient ✓, address ✓, package count ✓, weight ✓ — with a small loading animation that takes a few seconds. One field (an unclear note about the loading bay's opening hours) is highlighted in yellow for manual review. Sandra reads in surprise. **Caption:** Sender, recipient, address, weight — all filled in within seconds. The only thing flagged is what the AI is actually uncertain about. **Status:** 🟢 Green — automatic interpretation, never silent guessing

## Panel 9

**Visual description:** Sandra clicks "Review and approve" with one finger, with a coffee mug bearing navichain's official logo visible discreetly beside the keyboard. A green confirmation mark: "Booking #3044 created." **Caption:** One click — and an entire order email becomes a finished booking. **Status:** 🟢 Green — booking complete, no retyping

## Panel 10

**Visual description:** Split panel. On the left: a dispatcher drags the booking onto an available truck on a digital map. On the right: Robban Ek in his cab, his mobile pings with the new assignment directly in the driver app — he never needs to call in and ask what applies. navichain's official logo is visible discreetly in his app header and on the truck door. **Caption:** The same booking moves on by itself — to planning, to Robban, without a single extra call. **Status:** 🟢 Green — seamless flow from order to driver

## Panel 11

**Visual description:** Evening scene. Sandra's mobile lights up with a notification: the customer portal has automatically sent a delivery notice to the customer — the same customer who called angrily in panel 4. Sandra smiles and puts down the phone without needing to do anything herself. **Caption:** The customer who called this morning gets their delivery confirmation — without Sandra picking up the phone. **Status:** 🟢 Green — customer informed automatically

## Panel 12

**Visual description:** Sandra leaves the office in good time, coat on, bag over her shoulder with navichain's official logo discreetly visible as a small metal badge. The desk behind her is tidy — no pile of paper, just a closed laptop. A small note on the edge of the screen can be glimpsed: "Next: the notification that arrives before the customer even has time to ask." **Caption:** Forty-three orders. One working day. No pile left on the desk. **Status:** 🟢 Green — result & teaser for the next story

## Cover concept

**Motif:** Sandra in the foreground, the PDF sheet in one hand swelling almost over the edge in a chaotic pile of scribbles, figures and typos — the image of the sheet "dissolves" toward the right and flows into a clean, orderly booking box on a screen. Behind her, blurred: a truck already on its way. navichain's official logo discreetly visible in the corner of the screen. **Title:** THE PDF THAT BOOKED ITSELF **Subtitle:** Sandra & navichain's Magic Drop order intake **Kicker (as on the series' other covers):** "Forty-three order emails. Zero copied by hand."

No IT support appears — navichain is self-explanatory throughout.
