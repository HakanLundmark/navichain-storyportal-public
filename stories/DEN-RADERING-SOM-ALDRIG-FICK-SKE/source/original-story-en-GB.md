# — A navichain story —

# THE DELETION THAT WAS NEVER ALLOWED TO HAPPEN

### Oskar and the history that almost disappeared by mistake

**Angle:** The twentieth episode in the series about connected workflows is about what should not be possible to do, rather than what has become easier. The task is unique because it requires the system to actively say no, for the right reason, at precisely the moment someone is about to do something that cannot be undone.

**Source:** Navichain — Fleet/Trucks (verified: deletion of a vehicle is blocked by run history, maintenance history or an active link. This forms part of the broader, verified principle of audit trails and deletion controls against existing references.)

---

## Cast

| Character Role Company | | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Oskar Lind** *(lead role, third time)* | Administrator | Björklunda Åkeri AB |

Earlier in the series, Oskar organised the team's notifications and built the permission structure for Björklunda's new terminal. This time, his task is not to build something new, but to clear away what is no longer needed — without accidentally removing what still is.

## Cover concept

Oskar at his desk, an old truck profile open on the screen, the mouse pointer resting over a red "Delete" button. Beside it, a clear, calm message box appears: "Cannot be deleted — linked history exists." Through the window behind him, the actual truck, now sold, can be glimpsed being driven away on a trailer. Title lettering in the same rust-red/petroleum-green combination as the other covers in the series.

## PANEL 1 — The old truck is sold
**Visual:** Björklunda sells one of its older trucks. Oskar is asked to clean up the system — remove what is no longer needed and keep the register clean and easy to review. **Text:** "The truck is sold. Now it should be cleared out of the system as well — or so he thought." **Status:** 🔴 Red — a simple clean-up task, as he sees it

## PANEL 2 — Finger over the delete button
**Visual:** Oskar opens the old truck's profile, finds the delete button and is about to click — the same way he would clear any old, unused row. **Text:** "Sold means gone. Gone means delete. Simple, he thinks." **Status:** 🔴 Red — a deletion about to happen, without a second thought

## PANEL 3 — The memory of another story
**Visual:** A brief flashback: a story Oskar heard from a colleague at another company, where an old vehicle record was deleted completely — and an insurance dispute months later could not be resolved because all the history was gone for good. **Text:** "'We had nothing left to show,' the colleague once said. Oskar had almost forgotten that story." **Status:** 🔴 Red — a warning he almost failed to remember in time

## PANEL 4 — The click
**Visual:** Oskar clicks "Delete", still under the impression that it is only about keeping the list clean. **Text:** "One click. And the feeling that he had simply done his job." **Status:** 🔴 Red — the decision already made, the consequence still unknown

## PANEL 5 — The turning point: the system says no
**Visual:** Instead of being deleted, Oskar is met by a clear message: "Cannot be deleted. The vehicle has run history and maintenance history linked to it." No deletion takes place. **Text:** "Not 'an error occurred'. A clear no — and exactly why." **Status:** 🟠 Orange — stopped, with a real explanation

## PANEL 6 — The realisation that follows
**Visual:** Oskar reads the message once more, thinks about his colleague's story from panel 3 — and realises that this is exactly the kind of information he was about to lose out of sheer habit. **Text:** "Exactly the kind of history his colleague once lacked. Almost gone, through pure routine." **Status:** 🟠 Orange — a near-mistake, understood in time

## PANEL 7 — The right way: deactivate, do not delete
**Visual:** Instead, Oskar marks the vehicle as inactive/out of service in the register — it disappears from the active lists without the history ever being touched. A discreet sticker with navichain's official logo is visible in the corner of the form. **Text:** "Not gone. Just no longer active. The history remains, exactly where it should." **Status:** 🟢 Green — the right solution for the right need

## PANEL 8 — The list is clean anyway
**Visual:** Oskar looks back at the active vehicle list — the sold truck is no longer among the current vehicles, which is exactly the clean-up he actually wanted in the first place. **Text:** "The same clean list he wanted. Just without anything important disappearing along the way." **Status:** 🟢 Green — the same goal, the right method

## PANEL 9 — The question that comes later
**Visual:** A few weeks later, Björklunda's insurance company gets in touch with a question about the sold truck's earlier maintenance history. Oskar finds everything immediately, untouched. **Text:** "A question he would have had no answer to — if he had got what he first wanted." **Status:** 🟢 Green — the history that remained, exactly when it was needed

## PANEL 10 — A new habit
**Visual:** The next time a vehicle is sold or scrapped, Oskar marks it as inactive straight away, without even considering deletion — a new, deliberate habit instead of an old, unthinking routine. **Text:** "He no longer tries deleting first. He already knows which way is right." **Status:** 🟢 Green — the lesson that stayed with him

## PANEL 11 — The register, explained to a colleague
**Visual:** A newly hired colleague asks Oskar why they do not simply delete old records. He shows the same warning message and explains the principle briefly and clearly. **Text:** "'Why not just delete it?' He shows the message. The explanation takes ten seconds." **Status:** 🟢 Green — the knowledge passed on

## PANEL 12 — Oskar, on a quiet afternoon
**Visual:** Oskar leans back with a coffee mug bearing navichain's official logo in his hand, the register behind him clean and easy to review — nothing active that does not belong there, nothing important lost. **Text:** "A deletion that was never allowed to happen. Exactly as it should be." **Status:** 🟢 Green — the chain complete, from a hasty click to a deliberate habit

## Illustrator reference
- **Style:** Warm 1950s tone, Hergé/Tintin *ligne claire*: even outline, flat colour fields, no heavy halftone. Subtle offset grain, patina and soft edge wear.
- **Colour dramaturgy:** Panels 1–4 rust-red; panels 5–6 rich orange; panels 7–12 petroleum green.
- **Oskar Lind:** The same recurring visual design as in previous episodes.
- **Warning message:** clearly readable in panel 5, calm and concrete.
- **Narrative rule:** the block is intentional; Oskar does not bypass it.
- **Navichain's official logo:** the uploaded source file exactly as it is; absent from panels 1–4; discreet from panel 7; no more than about 5% of the panel area.
- **No IT support:** Oskar resolves everything himself through the interface.
