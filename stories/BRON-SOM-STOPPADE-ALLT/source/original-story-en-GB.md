# THE BRIDGE THAT STOPPED EVERYTHING

## Conny & Navichain’s truck-aware route optimisation

**Tagline (original brief):** A map for cars. A 42-tonne truck. What could go wrong?

**Main character:** Conny Sjövall, 52 — owner & traffic manager, Sjövall Åkeri AB (7 vehicles, international transport Sweden–Germany–the Netherlands)

**Supporting character:** Robban Ek — driver.

## Original story

1. Conny gets the call that Robban has become stuck under a bridge in Germany.
2. Robban’s truck is wedged beneath a low railway bridge outside Hamburg.
3. The car navigation shows the route but does not know the truck’s actual height.
4. Conny faces recovery, repair, fines/costs and an unhappy customer.
5. Seven vehicles, five countries — Conny cannot memorise every bridge and road restriction in Europe.
6. He reviews different rules and realises that an ordinary map does not know a truck’s weight, height or prohibited roads.
7. A colleague mentions a map built for trucks; Conny discovers Navichain.
8. Conny enters vehicle profiles for the fleet with the relevant vehicle data.
9. Navichain plans a route that takes the vehicle profile into account and routes around the height restriction.
10. Robban drives calmly on a route calculated for the actual vehicle combination.
11. The machine parts arrive intact and on time at the customer in the Netherlands and are digitally acknowledged on site.
12. Back at the office, Conny has control of the fleet’s routes and says: “We still drive the same roads. But never blindly again.”

**Next in the series:** ADR-classified dangerous goods and cross-border documentation for the same fleet.

## Preflight corrections used in the sv-SE master

- The cover weight was normalised from 42 tonnes to 40 tonnes.
- The vehicle combination height was normalised from 4.2 m to 4.0 m against a 3.8 m clearance.
- The incident was described as an attempted passage under a signed height restriction, not as the vehicle generally exceeding a legal height limit.
- The claim of “five minutes of data entry” was removed.
- EU 561/2006 was not presented as an invented real-time indicator in the turn-by-turn view.
