# — A navichain story —

# TWENTY CALLS BECAME ONE
### Kaj and the morning when nobody needed to ring around

**Format:** 12 panels + cover concept. Illustrator brief.

**Angle:** A new angle for the series: sudden absence and urgent reassignment of a run. Previous stories about the dispatcher role have dealt with choosing an *external* carrier when the company’s own vehicles are fully booked (Sara Lund, "The List That Shows Its Reasons") or with planning *in advance* (Jenny Corell’s cabotage forecast). This is the only story in the series that shows the acute, unvarnished morning panic that arises when an already staffed run suddenly has no driver — and how the dispatcher solves it by filtering out the right colleague from the company’s own staff, rather than guessing or calling in alphabetical order.

**Source/grounding (verified in the product documentation):** Driver profiles in navichain contain licence classes, ADR type/expiry date and shift information. Profiles can be filtered. A run supports multiple drivers and can be reassigned. The notification channel for run assignment is configurable per organisation (email/SMS/push); the driver is notified when a run becomes confirmed. The story is deliberately restrained: it does **not** claim that navichain automatically knows who is available right now or assigns the run itself — Kaj filters out a qualified shortlist and makes the active choice himself, just as with external carrier matching in earlier episodes.

**Important narrative requirement:** navichain never chooses the replacement automatically — it is always Kaj who sees the list and makes the decision. No IT department or support is shown. Kaj solves the entire situation himself, on his own screen, before the first customer has even had time to wonder where the truck is.

Kaj has been a dispatcher for eight years. The worst kind of morning is not a delayed delivery — it is an empty driver’s seat and a customer appointment that is already in the calendar.

## Cover concept

Kaj sits at his desk, the telephone receiver balanced against his shoulder, with an old, worn personnel list full of crossed-out names in front of him — but he is not looking at the list. Instead, his gaze is fixed on a tablet where a short, clear row of three names is lit up, one of them already marked green. The expression on his face is shifting from stress to relief. Title typography in the series’ established rust-red/petroleum-green combination.

---

## VISUAL STYLE (applies to all panels)

Warm 1950s aesthetic in Hergé/Tintin *ligne claire*: clean contour lines of even thickness, flat colour fields without halftones or heavy cross-hatching. Parchment/cream-coloured base with rust red, mustard yellow, petroleum green and warm gold. Captions hand-lettered, never digital sans-serif. Physical props (the worn personnel list, an old desk telephone, a coffee table) take priority over sharp UI mock-ups. Lightly grainy offset-print texture with patina, soft edge wear and foxing.

**The story takes place in the modern day** (tablets, mobile phones, push notifications) but **is drawn in the classic style above**.

**Recurring motif:** The physical personnel list — a yellowed sheet of paper with telephone numbers in ink, several crossed out over the years — dominates the early panels. From the turning point onward, the list ends up at the bottom of a paper tray, and the office surface in the final panel is almost empty except for a tablet and a coffee mug.

**Logo rule:** navichain’s official logo is completely absent from panels 1–6. It is introduced discreetly from panel 7 — as an app icon on Kaj’s tablet, a small logo in the corner of the filtering view, a sticker on the spine of a binder that now stands unused, and finally on Kaj’s coffee mug in the last panel. Never centred, never more than about 5% of the panel area, never advertising-like. *The official logo is uploaded as a source file in the project — the illustrator must use that file at each specified placement, not recreate it by hand.*

**No IT support:** No support person or IT department appears anywhere. Kaj solves everything himself.

## PANEL 1 — The call at 05:40
**Image:** Kaj’s phone rings at dawn. Half asleep, he answers: "Vidar, I can’t manage to drive today. Fever." The calendar on the wall behind him shows a customer delivery at 08:00 with Vidar’s name next to it.
**Caption:** "05:40. Vidar was ill. The delivery was due to go at eight o’clock."
**Status:** 🔴 Red (urgent problem)

## PANEL 2 — The worn list
**Image:** Kaj pulls a yellowed personnel list out of a binder, full of names and telephone numbers, several crossed out with marker strokes over the years.
**Caption:** "He no longer knew which numbers were even correct."
**Status:** 🔴 Red (uncertain information)

## PANEL 3 — The first call
**Image:** Kaj calls the first number on the list. A time is shown discreetly in the corner: 05:47. A speech bubble suggests a confused, newly awakened reply from the other end.
**Caption:** "Call one. Wrong licence class — he could not have driven it anyway."
**Status:** 🔴 Red (wasted time)

## PANEL 4 — More calls, same result
**Image:** A collage of three small frames: Kaj calls, calls, calls again. The times rise: 05:52, 06:04, 06:11. One reply suggests missing ADR authorisation, another that the person is already booked on another shift.
**Caption:** "Wrong qualification. Already busy. No answer at all."
**Status:** 🔴 Red (escalating pressure)

## PANEL 5 — The clock is closing in
**Image:** Kaj looks at the clock: 06:35. Outside the window it is starting to get light. The wall calendar with "08:00 — Delivery" suddenly feels much closer.
**Caption:** "Nearly an hour gone. Still no driver."
**Status:** 🔴 Red (time pressure culminates)

## PANEL 6 — A colleague mentions the filtering
**Image:** Kaj’s colleague comes in with coffee, sees the worn list and says: "Why are you ringing around? Just filter the driver list — right class, right ADR, right shift." Kaj looks up, sceptical but curious.
**Caption:** "'Just filter.' Kaj had never tried it."
**Status:** 🟠 Orange (turning point)

## PANEL 7 — The filtering
**Image:** Kaj opens the driver view in navichain on his tablet and sets filters: licence class C+E, ADR type, shift "morning/forenoon". navichain’s official logo is visible discreetly in the corner of the view.
**Caption:** "Three filters. One click. The list shrank to three names."
**Status:** 🟢 Green (clarity)

## PANEL 8 — The shortlist
**Image:** Close-up of the screen: three driver names with the correct qualifications clearly marked — including Robban Ek, whose ADR type and licence class match exactly.
**Caption:** "No more guessing game. Only the people who could actually drive it."
**Status:** 🟢 Green (precision)

## PANEL 9 — The reassignment
**Image:** Kaj taps to move the run to Robban Ek in the system. A confirmation message appears: "Run reassigned — Robban Ek."
**Caption:** "One tap. The run changed driver without a single extra call."
**Status:** 🟢 Green (action)

## PANEL 10 — The notification reaches Robban
**Image:** Robban Ek, at home at the breakfast table, feels his phone vibrate. A notification shows the new run and the time.
**Caption:** "Robban got the message before the coffee had even had time to go cold."
**Status:** 🟢 Green (immediacy)

## PANEL 11 — The delivery on time
**Image:** Robban loads the truck and drives off, the clock shows 07:52 — eight minutes to spare. Kaj’s office is calm in the background.
**Caption:** "07:52. The delivery went as planned."
**Status:** 🟢 Green (goal achieved)

## PANEL 12 — The list in the waste-paper tray
**Image:** Back at the office. Kaj puts the old, worn personnel list into a paper tray under the desk. On the desk there is only the tablet and a coffee mug with navichain’s official logo.
**Caption:** "Twenty possible calls became a single tap."
**Status:** 🟢 Green (ending, clear point)

## Production notes for the illustrator

- **Colour dramaturgy:** Red dominates panels 1–5 (acute crisis, wasted calls), orange is the turning point (panel 6), petroleum green dominates panels 7–12.
- **Collage panel (panel 4):** three small frames within the same panel to show repetition and rising frustration without extending the panel count.
- **Times as a recurring visual element:** visible in the corner of several early panels to build time pressure without text.
- **Vidar is never shown** — only as a voice on the phone in panel 1. This keeps the focus on Kaj and the solution, not on the absence itself.
- **Continuity:** Robban Ek (panels 8, 10, 11) is the same recurring driver as in previous stories — the same outward attributes should be maintained. This is the first time he himself becomes the one who "saves the day" by being the right match, rather than a pure cameo.
- **Physical props to prioritise:** the yellowed personnel list with crossed-out names, the old desk telephone, the coffee mug with discreet logo in the final panel.
