# — A navichain story —

# SAME ROUTE, SAME ANSWER
### Torbjörn and the three quotes that almost became three different prices

**Angle:** The twenty-second episode in the series about connected workflows at Björklunda Åkeri AB introduces, for the first time in the entire series, the man behind the name on the sign: Torbjörn Björklund himself, owner and CEO. The task is one of the most fundamental in the entire industry and at the same time one of the most underestimated risks: being able to quickly answer several customers asking for a price for the same or a similar route, without digging through old invoices to remember what was quoted last time — and without accidentally giving three customers three different prices for the same job.

**Source:** Navichain — Pricing/Offers (verified: pricing services own one or more price sheets with ranges by postcode/geo-zone, unit tiers and an optional minimum; sheets can be cloned. Offers reuse booking-style data — organisations, dates, packages, charges, route, carrier and requirements — and can be created, cloned and sent individually or in bulk. The story is deliberately restrained: it does **not** claim that navichain automatically finds the lowest or “optimal” price, or that a history view showing all previous offers for a particular route appears instantly if no such specific view exists. Instead, it shows what is actually verified: a current, configured price sheet for the route to work from, plus the ability to clone a previous offer as the template for a new one — which produces the same practical outcome: consistent pricing, no guessing game.)

**Important narrative instruction:** navichain never sets the price for Torbjörn — he always triggers the calculation himself and approves it, just as pricing elsewhere in the series is described as assisted, not automatic. No IT department appears. No finance department needs to be involved for him to find the right price sheet.

## Cover concept

Torbjörn sits at his desk with three telephone handsets balanced around him and an old notepad in front of him where he has quickly scribbled three different, mutually inconsistent prices for essentially the same route — Gothenburg–Malmö. Beside the pad, on a tablet, a single clear price sheet for the route is already open. His eyes move between the pad and the screen, as if he has just realised which of the two he should trust. Title lettering in the series’ established rust-red/petroleum-green combination.

---

## VISUAL STYLE (applies to all panels)

Warm 1950s aesthetic in Hergé/Tintin *ligne claire*: clean contour lines of even thickness, flat colour fields without halftones or heavy cross-hatching. Parchment/cream base with rust red, mustard yellow, petroleum green and warm gold. Captions are hand-lettered, never digital sans-serif. Physical props (old invoice binders, a notepad, a coffee mug with Torbjörn’s initials) are prioritised over sharp UI mock-ups. Lightly grainy offset-print texture with patina, soft edge wear and foxing.

**Recurring motif:** an entire wall of old invoice binders behind Torbjörn’s desk in the early panels — sorted by year rather than by route, making them almost impossible to search quickly. From the turning point onwards, the wall visually shrinks in importance (the camera/composition shifts focus away from it) in favour of the simple, clear screen on the desk.

**Logo rule:** navichain’s official logo is completely absent from panels 1–6. It is introduced discreetly from panel 7 — as a small icon in the corner of the price-sheet view, a logo in the offer PDF header, and finally on Torbjörn’s coffee mug in the final panel. Never centred, never more than around 5% of the panel area, never advertising-like. *The official logo is uploaded as a source file in the project — the illustrator must use that file for every specified placement, not recreate it by hand.*

**No IT support:** No support person or IT department appears anywhere. Torbjörn solves everything himself in the middle of an ordinary working day.

## PANEL 1 — Three calls, same day
**Visual:** Torbjörn sits with the telephone in his hand. On the notepad he has written three customer names, all with the same destination — Gothenburg–Malmö — and three different proposed prices beside them, none exactly the same.
**Text:** “Three customers, same route, same day. Three different figures on the pad.”
**Status:** 🔴 Red (inconsistency visible)

## PANEL 2 — The wall of binders
**Visual:** Behind Torbjörn is an entire wall of old invoice binders, sorted by year. He gets up, searches, pulls out a binder and flips through it frantically without finding the right route.
**Text:** “The invoices were there. Just not sorted by what he needed to know.”
**Status:** 🔴 Red (wasted time)

## PANEL 3 — The uncertainty
**Visual:** Torbjörn holds up two old invoices side by side — both for similar routes, but with different amounts and no clear explanation for the difference.
**Text:** “Two invoices, two prices, no explanation why.”
**Status:** 🔴 Red (trust risk)

## PANEL 4 — The clock is ticking
**Visual:** Torbjörn’s phone lights up again — another customer waiting for an answer. He looks between the binder on his lap and the flashing screen, pulled in two directions.
**Text:** “The fourth call was already waiting while he was still looking for the first answer.”
**Status:** 🔴 Red (escalating pressure)

## PANEL 5 — Åsa’s brief cameo
**Visual:** Åsa Renberg walks past with a coffee mug, sees the pile of binders and says calmly: “You know the price sheets are already in the system, Torbjörn? You don’t have to dig.” Brief, self-contained cameo.
**Text:** “‘You don’t have to dig.’ He had forgotten that he already had the answer.”
**Status:** 🟠 Orange (turning point)

## PANEL 6 — He opens the price sheet
**Visual:** Torbjörn puts the binder down and instead opens the pricing view on his tablet, searching for the Gothenburg–Malmö route.
**Text:** “One search field instead of an entire wall of binders.”
**Status:** 🟠 Orange (action)

## PANEL 7 — One price sheet, one truth
**Visual:** The screen shows a clear, current price sheet for the route — ranges, unit tiers, minimum, all at once. navichain’s official logo appears discreetly in the corner of the view.
**Text:** “One sheet. One current truth for the route, not three different memories.”
**Status:** 🟢 Green (clarity)

## PANEL 8 — He calculates deliberately
**Visual:** Torbjörn enters the customer’s details and presses “Calculate price” himself — no automatic guessing; he sees the result and approves it.
**Text:** “He pressed the button himself. The system calculated, but he decided.”
**Status:** 🟢 Green (control retained)

## PANEL 9 — Clone instead of starting over
**Visual:** For the next customer, with a similar enquiry, Torbjörn clones the offer he has just sent instead of starting again from scratch.
**Text:** “The next customer got the same foundation, not a new guess.”
**Status:** 🟢 Green (consistency)

## PANEL 10 — Three answers, same day
**Visual:** Split-panel: three screens/phones, three customers all receiving their answer during the same afternoon — prices consistent with one another where the routes are genuinely similar.
**Text:** “Three answers the same afternoon. This time without a single figure contradicting another.”
**Status:** 🟢 Green (efficiency + credibility)

## PANEL 11 — The binders are moved
**Visual:** Torbjörn takes a couple of the oldest invoice binders down from the wall and puts them in an archive cabinet — not thrown away, but no longer the first place he turns.
**Text:** “The binders could become history instead of reference books.”
**Status:** 🟢 Green (symbolic conclusion)

## PANEL 12 — Evening, calm
**Visual:** Torbjörn leans back with his coffee mug, where navichain’s official logo appears discreetly. Through the window, the yard can be seen, with the whole fleet from previous episodes — Kenneth’s workshop, Frida’s warehouse, Sara and Oskar’s office — quiet and in order.
**Text:** “Same route. Same answer. Every time.”
**Status:** 🟢 Green (conclusion, clear point — and a quiet tribute to the whole company)

## Continuity note

The twenty-second episode in the series about connected workflows at Björklunda Åkeri AB, and the first episode in which Torbjörn Björklund — the man behind the company name — takes centre stage. After twenty-one episodes about individual roles and departments, this story ties the series together at owner level: the same platform that has helped Kenneth, Frida, Sara, Oskar and all the others also helps him with the most fundamental thing of all — being able to trust his own price. Åsa Renberg returns in a brief cameo to remind him that the solution was already there all along.

**Next in the series:** An angle not yet used — how a customer who calls and asks “where is my delivery?” can get the answer directly from Torbjörn or anyone else in the office, without having to find the right dispatcher or wait for someone else to become available.

## Production notes for the illustrator

- **Colour dramaturgy:** Red dominates panels 1–4 (inconsistency, time pressure), orange is the turning point (panels 5–6), petroleum green dominates panels 7–12.
- **The wall of invoice binders** should feel tangibly overwhelming in panel 2, then deliberately shrink in the composition (less prominent, farther away in the depth of the image) from panel 7 onwards without disappearing entirely.
- **The split-panel in panel 10** should show three clearly separate but similar scenes to underline consistency without needing extra text.
- **Torbjörn Björklund** is a new, central persona — the first time the company’s eponymous owner appears in the series. Record his appearance clearly for future returns, just as with Robban Ek and Kenneth Broman.
- **Panel 12 functions as a subtle “series tribute”:** the yard/office is glimpsed with environments from previous episodes suggested in the background — no earlier characters need to be clearly visible, only implied.
- **Physical props to prioritise:** the notepad with three scribbled prices in panel 1, the wall of year-sorted binders, the coffee mug with the discreet logo in the final panel.
