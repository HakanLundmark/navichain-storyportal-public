# — A navichain story —

# THE SHELF THAT KEEPS THINGS APART

### Frida and the boundary between the customer's goods and the haulier's own inventory

**Angle:** A direct continuation of the previous episode's chain (deviation → warehouse → purchasing), but focused on a different type of goods: things that do not belong to the haulier at all. Customer-owned goods held in temporary storage must never be mixed up with the company's own spare-parts inventory — neither physically on the shelf nor in valuation. Once again, the task is unique to the intersection between warehousing and the customer relationship: an ordinary warehouse app keeps track of "what is there", but not "whose is it, and how much may be released without asking again".

**Source:** Navichain — Warehouses / Customer Goods custody module (verified flow: receipt linked to customer/warehouse/booking, customer-owned goods are never included in own inventory value, barcode is auto-assigned, scanner lookup honestly reports none/one/many, release up to the balance held, wrong customer/warehouse/quantity refuses release instead of blocking the driver after departure, full history for receipt/placement/release).

Frida works in the same building as Kenneth from the previous episode, but her part of the warehouse handles something completely different: seasonal goods that customers such as Solgläntan Möbler store temporarily at Björklunda between deliveries. Where Kenneth's story was about knowing what *the haulier owns*, Frida's is about never mistaking what *the haulier merely stores*.

## Cover concept

Frida in the middle of the warehouse aisle, one hand resting on a pallet marked with the customer's name, the other on a tablet. The aisle is clearly divided — on the left, Björklunda's own labelled shelves (the same environment as in Kenneth's episode), on the right, a section with Solgläntan's seasonal furniture beneath a clear, separate sign. ## PANEL 1 — The delivery that does not belong to the haulier

**Visual:** A truck reverses in with pallets of garden furniture from Solgläntan Möbler. Frida meets it with a clipboard, looking uncertainly between the pallets and a blank field on the paper where a barcode should be. **Text:** "Customer goods. Not ours. But they still have to come in through the same gate." **Status:** 🔴 Red — no barcode, no clear location

## PANEL 2 — Same shelf, different owners

**Visual:** A warehouse shelf where the customer's furniture boxes are mixed in with Björklunda's own spare-parts boxes, separated only by a handwritten note: "NOTE! Customer goods — do not touch". The note has come loose at one corner. **Text:** "A paper note is all that separates what is ours from what is not." **Status:** 🔴 Red — physical and accounting risk

## PANEL 3 — The old phone call

**Visual:** Frida on the phone with Marianne Holt (via a small circled inset/telephone-receiver symbol), both uncertain: how much furniture was collected last week, how much remains? Neither has an exact answer. **Text:** "'How much do we still have with you?' Neither of them knows for certain." **Status:** 🔴 Red — no running balance for the customer's goods

## PANEL 4 — Almost the wrong release

**Visual:** A driver from another company is ready to load furniture for a completely different customer. Frida holds up her hand, hesitating — the quantity does not quite match what she remembers, but she has nothing to check against. **Text:** "Without a reliable balance, every release becomes a guess — and this time the guess is almost wrong." **Status:** 🔴 Red — incorrect release almost becomes reality

## PANEL 5 — The turning point: a dedicated receipt

**Visual:** Frida opens navichain on her tablet and registers the receipt linked directly to Solgläntan Möbler and this warehouse. A barcode number is assigned automatically because the customer's boxes have no code of their own. **Text:** "No code of its own on the box? The system creates one — linked to exactly this customer, this warehouse." **Status:** 🟠 Orange — receipt registered, traceable from the start

## PANEL 6 — Two inventories, one floor

**Visual:** The screen clearly shows two separate balances side by side: "Björklunda's own inventory value" and "Customer-owned goods — Solgläntan Möbler" — the same physical floor space, but never the same figure. **Text:** "The customer's goods are never counted in our own inventory value. Never by mistake, never 'for convenience'." **Status:** 🟠 Orange — values are kept separate by principle, not by luck

## PANEL 7 — The receipt that only needs to be created once

**Visual:** A neat PDF goods-receipt note is generated on the screen, linked to customer, warehouse and date. Frida sends it to Marianne with one click. Navichain's official logo is visible discreetly in the corner of the screen. **Text:** "One receipt. Right from the start. No reason to create it again." **Status:** 🟢 Green — receipt generated and sent

## PANEL 8 — Robban collects the right quantity

**Visual:** Robban Ek connects an outbound booking linked to the customer's goods. He scans the pallet while loading — the system automatically releases exactly the linked entry, no more, no less. **Text:** "One scan at loading. The right quantity is released — automatically, not from memory." **Status:** 🟢 Green — automatic release on collection

## PANEL 9 — The almost-wrong release that never happens

**Visual:** The same situation as in panel 4 is repeated — but now the system clearly refuses on the screen: "Wrong customer/warehouse/quantity" before the driver has even loaded the truck. Frida calmly points at the screen instead of guessing. **Text:** "This time the screen speaks up — before the truck leaves the yard, not afterwards." **Status:** 🟢 Green — incorrect release stopped in time

## PANEL 10 — Frida answers immediately

**Visual:** The phone rings again — Marianne Holt asks how much remains. Frida answers directly from the screen without leaving her desk, pointing to an exact balance. **Text:** "The same question as last time. This time the answer is on the screen, not in her head." **Status:** 🟢 Green — balance available in real time

## PANEL 11 — The history that settles the dispute

**Visual:** A brief dispute arises about a delivery from three weeks ago. Frida opens the history: receipt, placement, every release — all with timestamps. Marianne (via phone) falls silent, satisfied. **Text:** "Received, placed, released — all still there, in order, without anyone having to remember correctly." **Status:** 🟢 Green — complete traceability resolves the dispute

## PANEL 12 — Two inventories, one calm afternoon

**Visual:** Frida leans against a pallet, a coffee mug with navichain's official logo in her hand. In the background Kenneth walks past with his own tablet, occupied with his own corner of the warehouse — the two parts of the building are clearly seen working side by side without crossing into each other. **Text:** "Two inventories under the same roof. Never mixed up — never a memory game." **Status:** 🟢 Green — the chain complete, from receipt to trust

## Continuity note

This is the second episode in a deliberate series about connected, multi-function workflows rather than isolated features — and the direct follow-up to the previous episode's teaser about the customer's own goods in the warehouse. Frida Ahl is introduced as a new protagonist to represent a role clearly distinct from Kenneth's: he owns his inventory, she manages someone else's. Kenneth returns in a brief, clearly delimited cameo to show that the two warehouse flows exist side by side in the same building without ever crossing — a visual reminder of the series' principle that customer-owned goods are never included in the company's own inventory value. Robban Ek continues in his established role as the series' connecting figure.

**Next in the series:** The integration story — how a customer's own business system can talk directly to navichain via API/webhook, without manual entry on either side. An angle not yet used in the series, well suited to introducing a new persona on the customer's IT or finance side.
