# NEVER EMPTY ON THE WAY HOME

### Sara and the load waiting for a road that was already planned

**Story ID:** `aldrig-tom-pa-vag-hem`  
**Master language:** `sv-SE`  
**Master version:** `1.0`  
**Protagonist:** Sara Lund, dispatcher, Björklunda Åkeri AB  
**Supporting character:** Robban Ek, driver, Björklunda Åkeri AB  
**Primary Navichain capability:** Runs — Find return loads

## Original story / Narrative Master translation

**Angle:** The seventeenth episode in the series about connected workflows returns to Sara Lund, this time not to choose an external carrier but to solve a problem that costs money and fuel without appearing as an urgent case: an empty truck on its way home. The task is unique because it requires a quick, safe match against a road that is already planned — while also checking rules (driving and rest times, cabotage) that are otherwise easily forgotten in the rush to fill an empty leg.

**Source:** Navichain — Runs, Find return loads (verified: ranks unplanned bookings near an empty leg and flags driving-hours/rest-time and cabotage risk before the user adds one. The run grid reports, among other things, the share of empty kilometres.)

Earlier in the series, Sara has chosen the best carrier and made sure alerts reach the right person. This time her task is about something quieter: a road home that has already been planned does not need to be driven empty just because no one had time to look for what could fill it.

## Cover concept

Sara at her screen, a map with a planned route stretching out and then back again — the homeward leg marked with a sad, empty-truck icon. Beside it, a short list appears: a few waiting bookings, ranked, with one already marked by a clear green tick. Title lettering in the same rust-red/petroleum-green combination as the other covers in the series.

## PANEL 1 — The road home, empty as usual

**Visual:** Sara plans a delivery to another city. The homeward journey after the delivery is clearly visible on the map — completely empty, with no load planned for the return leg.  
**Text:** "Out with a load. Home without one. That's how it has always been."  
**Status:** 🔴 Red — a planned but empty journey home

## PANEL 2 — Searching by hand

**Visual:** Sara scrolls through waiting, unplanned bookings in a long list, trying to guess which ones are near the planned return route — no clear order, just a time-consuming search.  
**Text:** "Twenty bookings to scroll through. None of them sorted by what actually fits."  
**Status:** 🔴 Red — matching without help, pure guesswork

## PANEL 3 — A choice that almost went wrong

**Visual:** Sara chooses a booking that seems to be roughly in the right place and adds it to the return route, without properly checking driving and rest times or cabotage limits — just by feel.  
**Text:** "It felt right. But 'feels right' isn't the same as knowing."  
**Status:** 🔴 Red — a decision based on instinct, not control

## PANEL 4 — The discovery at the last moment

**Visual:** Just before saving, Sara discovers that the booking she selected would push Robban close to the permitted driving-time limit and would also touch a cabotage rule she had not considered. She removes the choice at the last second.  
**Text:** "Almost wrong. This time she spotted it herself — at the last moment."  
**Status:** 🔴 Red — a risk that almost slipped through

## PANEL 5 — The turning point: the list that already knows where the road goes

**Visual:** Sara opens "Find return loads" on the planned run. A short, ranked list of unplanned bookings near the empty leg appears — not all bookings, only those that are actually in the right area.  
**Text:** "No long list to scroll through. Just what actually lies along the road home."  
**Status:** 🟠 Orange — matching against the right leg, not the entire list

## PANEL 6 — The risk is visible before the choice is made

**Visual:** Beside each suggestion is a clear flag: driving/rest-time risk or cabotage risk, shown before Sara has even added the booking. The same type of risk that almost slipped through in panel 3 is now visible immediately on another suggestion farther down the list.  
**Text:** "The same kind of risk that almost became a mistake. Now it's already visible in the list — before the choice, not after."  
**Status:** 🟠 Orange — risk visible in advance, not discovered at the last moment

## PANEL 7 — The right load, the right road

**Visual:** Sara chooses the highest-ranked booking without a warning flag and adds it to the return route. A discreet sticker with Navichain's official logo is visible in the corner of the list view.  
**Text:** "The right load, already heading the same way. No detour, no risk left to worry about."  
**Status:** 🟢 Green — an empty leg becomes a paid leg

## PANEL 8 — The empty kilometres decrease, visibly

**Visual:** The run summary updates — the share of empty kilometres for this run clearly falls, visible in the same view Sara is already looking at.  
**Text:** "The same run. A significantly smaller share driven completely unnecessarily."  
**Status:** 🟢 Green — a measurable difference, not just a feeling of doing the right thing

## PANEL 9 — Robban drives home, with a load

**Visual:** Robban Ek loads the new booking after his delivery and drives home with goods instead of an empty truck. He notices the difference — a paid leg instead of just driving hours.  
**Text:** "The same road home. This time with something in the load space."  
**Status:** 🟢 Green — a previously empty leg, now a real job

## PANEL 10 — The customer who waited less

**Visual:** The customer whose booking had been waiting receives the delivery earlier than it otherwise would have in a normal available slot — it simply fitted perfectly into a road that was already planned.  
**Text:** "A booking that had been waiting, and a road already going exactly there — no one had seen the match until now."  
**Status:** 🟢 Green — faster delivery, without extra driving

## PANEL 11 — The week's pattern

**Visual:** Sara looks back at the week's runs — several previously empty journeys home have now been filled, one after another, not as a one-off effort but as a new recurring habit.  
**Text:** "Not one run that got better. A habit that makes more of them better."  
**Status:** 🟢 Green — the pattern, not just the occasion

## PANEL 12 — Sara, a calm afternoon

**Visual:** Sara leans back holding a coffee mug with Navichain's official logo, while the map behind her shows several runs where both the outbound and homeward journeys now carry loads.  
**Text:** "Out with a load. Home with a load, this time. Just as it should be."  
**Status:** 🟢 Green — the chain complete, from empty road to paid leg

## Continuity note

The seventeenth episode in the series about connected workflows at Björklunda Åkeri AB, and Sara Lund's third lead role in the series — a natural development from her earlier episodes about carrier selection and notifications into a new, quieter problem: the empty journey home. Robban Ek returns in a short cameo that gives the driver's perspective on the same change. The episode shares a theme with earlier cabotage and risk-flagging episodes in the series, but focuses specifically on resource utilisation rather than individual regulatory compliance.

**Next in the series:** The delivery that failed the first time and still did not become chaos — how a failed delivery attempt is recorded with a clear reason and can be followed by a new attempt or a return with the address reversed, without anyone having to improvise on site. An angle not yet used in the series.
