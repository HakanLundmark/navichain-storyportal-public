# Nothing falls through the cracks

### A navichain story — Henrik & navichain's task engine

**Character:** Henrik Ström, operations coordinator at a haulage company. He is the person every loose end lands with — the customer waiting for a response, the POD that never came back, the certificate that is about to expire, the damage case that has gone quiet. None of them is one big thing; they are fifty small things, any one of which can cost a customer if it is forgotten.

**Style:** Warm 1950s Hergé/Tintin "ligne claire" — even-weight contour lines, flat colour areas, a parchment/cream base, rust-red/mustard-yellow/petroleum-green/warm-gold palette, hand-lettered captions, subtle offset grain and patina. The story takes place in the **present day** — Henrik works at a computer and on a mobile, not with paper — but is drawn in the classic style. Colour dramaturgy: red (problem phase, panels 1–6) → mustard yellow/orange (transition) → petroleum green (solution).

### Illustrator's note — navichain's official logo

By "navichain's official logo" we mean navichain's actual official brand logo — not a generic "N" monogram placeholder. The official logo file is uploaded as a source file in the project; the illustrator must use that file at every marked placement below, not create their own interpretation of the logo.

- **Panels 1–6 (problem phase):** The logo is never visible. Henrik works with loose notes, memory notes and an unstructured phone list — no navichain material exists in his world yet.
- **Panels 7–12 (solution phase):** The logo is introduced discreetly as a prop — never as a centred advertising placement, never more than ~5% of the panel area. Suggested placements to vary:
  - Discreet logo in the corner of Henrik's computer screen (as a programme icon, not a banner)
  - The logo on a coffee mug on the desk
  - A small mark in the corner of the mobile screen
  - A discreet sticker on a binder/folder labelled "navichain — operations overview"
- Because the story is about **reduced paperwork**, the props should reflect that: in the problem phase, loose notes, notepads and a classic desk telephone dominate; in the solution phase, these are replaced by a retro-styled computer screen (preferably with a slight CRT feel) and a handheld mobile — never more sheets of paper than necessary.
- No panel should show an IT department or support helping Henrik get started — navichain should appear self-explanatory. This may be stated directly in a caption (see panel 8).
