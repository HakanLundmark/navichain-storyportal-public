# — A Navichain story —

# THE COLOUR THAT WARNED IN TIME

### Kenneth and the dates that never need to turn red unnecessarily

**Angle:** The nineteenth episode in the series about connected workflows returns to Kenneth Broman, the very first protagonist of the series, for a problem that resembles the one he solved in the first episode but concerns dates instead of spare parts: knowing in good time what is about to expire, before it becomes a crisis at the roadside.

**Source:** Navichain — Trucks/Compliance dates (verified: compliance dates are colour-coded — green when more than 30 days remain, amber when 30 days or fewer remain, red when expired or overdue. This is part of the broader, verified compliance-expiry display covering, among other things, certificates and insurance.)

Kenneth solved the series' first episode by connecting workshop, stock and purchasing. This time his task is not an acute shortage, but something just as important and much easier to miss: dates that slowly draw closer, until suddenly they do not any more.

---

## Cover concept

Kenneth at his desk, a spreadsheet open on one screen, full of dates in ordinary black text. On the screen beside it: the same kinds of dates, but now in clear colour fields — green, amber, red — immediately showing what actually needs his attention. Title lettering in the same rust-red/petroleum-green combination as the rest of the series.

---

## PANEL 1 — The spreadsheet with all the dates

**Visual:** Kenneth scrolls through a large spreadsheet containing certificate and insurance dates for the entire fleet — row after row, all in the same black text, nothing standing out as more urgent than anything else. **Text:** "A hundred rows. All equally important on paper. None of them clearly the most important right now." **Status:** 🔴 Red — dates without priority

## PANEL 2 — The roadside stop

**Visual:** One of Björklunda's trucks is stopped during a routine check — the driver's certificate turns out to have expired a few days earlier, without anyone at the office noticing in time. **Text:** "Expired four days ago. Nobody knew until the police did." **Status:** 🔴 Red — a date that has already become a problem

## PANEL 3 — It had been there all along

**Visual:** Kenneth goes back to the spreadsheet after the incident — the date was there, just like all the others, buried among dozens of similar rows that nobody had had time to inspect more closely. **Text:** "It was there all along. Nobody saw it in time, among everything else." **Status:** 🔴 Red — information that existed, but was not visible

## PANEL 4 — The worry about next time

**Visual:** Kenneth looks over the entire list again with an uneasy feeling: how many other dates are approaching right now, without him knowing? **Text:** "If it happened once, quietly — what else is getting closer right now?" **Status:** 🔴 Red — unresolved worry about what is coming

## PANEL 5 — The turning point: the same dates, in colour

**Visual:** Kenneth opens the vehicle register in Navichain. The same types of dates as in the spreadsheet, but now colour-coded — most green, some amber, a couple clearly red. **Text:** "The same information. But now he does not have to read every row to see what is urgent." **Status:** 🟠 Orange — priority visible in a second

## PANEL 6 — The amber ones he did not know about

**Visual:** Kenneth scans the list and finds several amber markings — dates within thirty days that he had never actively kept track of before. Nothing is urgent yet, but each one is clearly worth acting on. **Text:** "Not a crisis. Just close enough to deserve action — before it becomes more than that." **Status:** 🟠 Orange — warning in good time, not at the last second

## PANEL 7 — The renewal is booked, calmly

**Visual:** Kenneth books the renewal for one of the amber-marked certificates, three weeks before it actually expires. A discreet sticker with Navichain's official logo is visible in the corner of the register view. **Text:** "Three weeks left. Plenty of time to do it without stress." **Status:** 🟢 Green — dealt with before it became urgent

## PANEL 8 — The insurance that never had time to turn red

**Visual:** Another vehicle's insurance date, previously amber, turns green again as soon as the renewal is complete — visible directly in the register, with no reason to wonder whether it really went through. **Text:** "From amber to green again. No reason to wonder whether it really got done." **Status:** 🟢 Green — confirmation visible, not merely assumed

## PANEL 9 — Nina checks, quickly

**Visual:** Nina Palm is planning a long cross-border run and quickly checks the vehicle's colour markings before booking it — everything is green, no reason to hesitate. **Text:** "A quick glance, not a review of the whole paper sheet. Green means green." **Status:** 🟢 Green — confident planning, built on a colour everyone understands

## PANEL 10 — The Monday routine

**Visual:** Kenneth opens the register every Monday morning and looks only for amber and red — no longer the whole list, only what actually needs him that week. **Text:** "Not a whole list to go through. Just what actually needs him this week." **Status:** 🟢 Green — a routine, not a full review

## PANEL 11 — The months that passed without another stop

**Visual:** Kenneth thinks back to the stop in panel 2 — since then, nothing similar has happened again, a quiet, unnoticed normality that is exactly what he wanted. **Text:** "Nothing happened. And that was exactly the point." **Status:** 🟢 Green — the unnoticed as the actual success

## PANEL 12 — Kenneth, on a calm Monday

**Visual:** Kenneth leans back holding a coffee mug with Navichain's official logo, the register open behind him — mostly green, a couple of amber entries, nothing red. **Text:** "Green, amber, red. No longer a hundred rows to read — just three colours to understand." **Status:** 🟢 Green — the chain complete, from buried information to a colour everyone can see

**Post-it on Kenneth's desk (teaser):** *"Next: the deletion that was stopped — and why that was actually a good thing."*

## Illustrator reference

| Element | Instruction |
| --- | --- |
| **Style** | Warm 1950s tone, Hergé/Tintin *ligne claire*: even contour line, flat colour fields, no heavy screening. Subtle offset grain, patina and soft edge wear. |
| **Colour dramaturgy** | Panels 1–4: rust red, an undifferentiated spreadsheet and a roadside stop. Panels 5–6: rich orange, the colours appear and create clear priority. Panels 7–12: petroleum green, a calm, manageable routine. |
| **Kenneth Broman** | Same recurring visual design as in his first episode. Worried and overwhelmed in front of the spreadsheet in the red panels, calm and methodical in front of the colour-coded list in the green panels. |
| **The colour system as a visual motif** | Green/amber/red must be a clear, consistent and readable element in the interface throughout the story — preferably using the same visual code as Nina's earlier fleet map for recognition across the series. |
| **Important narrative rule** | No panel may imply that Navichain automatically renews certificates or insurance — it displays and colour-codes status; Kenneth acts and books the renewal himself. |
| **Nina Palm** | Same recurring visual design as in her own episode. A short, clearly contained cameo showing how the same colour coding is used by another role, in another context. |
| **Navichain's official logo** | Use the uploaded source file exactly as it is — never redraw it by hand. Absent in panels 1–4. Introduced discreetly from panel 7 (corner of the register view) and then visible on the coffee mug in the final panel — never centred, never advertising-like, maximum roughly 5% of panel area. |
| **No IT support** | No external technician, no support case. Kenneth and Nina solve everything themselves through the interface — Navichain remains self-explanatory throughout the story. |

## Continuity note

The nineteenth episode in the series about connected workflows at Björklunda Åkeri AB, and Kenneth Broman's second lead role — a deliberate return to the series' very first character, this time with a theme that mirrors his original story (never discovering a shortage too late) but applied to dates instead of spare parts. Nina Palm returns in a short cameo showing that the same colour coding works equally well for the person planning a single run and for the person responsible for the maintenance of the entire fleet.

**Next in the series:** The deletion that was stopped, and why that was actually a good thing — how Navichain checks references before anything is deleted and clearly explains what is blocking the action, instead of allowing a mistake to happen unnecessarily. An angle not yet used in the series.
