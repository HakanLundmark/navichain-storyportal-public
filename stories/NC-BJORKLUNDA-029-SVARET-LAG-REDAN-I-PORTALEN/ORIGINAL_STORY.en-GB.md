# — A navichain story —

# THE ANSWER WAS ALREADY IN THE PORTAL
### Petra and the question she did not need to ask anyone

**Format:** 12 panels + cover concept. Illustrator brief.

**Angle:** The twenty-ninth episode in the series about connected workflows at Björklunda Åkeri AB returns to Petra Vindh, the customer who earlier in the series stopped filling in the same booking details every Monday by cloning her own orders (episode 26). This time the challenge is different, but just as everyday: weeks after a delivery, she suddenly needs to remember exactly what applied to that particular one — what the price was, what the terms were, whether it arrived on time — without remembering the details herself and without wanting to bother Björklunda with yet another email about something she really ought to be able to look up herself.

**Source:** Navichain — Customer Portal/Organizations (verified: portal users can create/track orders and view invoices. Public tracking requires no login and can show a timeline, POD photos/signature and package details. Portal invoices are documented as preview-only without PDF download.) The story is deliberately restrained: it does **not** claim that the portal shows a complete, continuous history from quote to invoice in a single view, or that it has its own document archive — it is specifically the individual order's tracking timeline and its invoice preview that Petra uses, which together are enough to answer her question.

**Important narrative instruction:** No IT department or customer service at Björklunda appears. Petra finds the answer entirely by herself in the portal, without anyone at Björklunda even knowing that she had wondered about anything.

## Cover concept

Petra is sitting at her desk with the phone in her hand, about to dial Björklunda's number — but she stops mid-motion. On the screen beside her, an order detail is already open instead: a clear timeline (Picked up → Delivered, with dates) and an invoice preview with the price visible. Her expression shifts from wanting to call to almost laughing at having forgotten that she did not need to. Title typography in the series' established rust-red/petroleum-green combination.

---

## VISUAL STYLE (applies to all panels)

Warm 1950s aesthetic in Hergé/Tintin *ligne claire*: clean contour lines of even thickness, flat colour fields without halftones or heavy hatching. Parchment/cream-coloured base with rust red, mustard yellow, petroleum green and warm gold. Captions hand-lettered, never digital sans-serif. Physical props (an old notebook, a telephone handset, a coffee mug) are prioritised over sharp UI mock-ups. Light grainy offset-print texture with patina, soft edge wear and foxing.

**Recurring motif:** a notebook with Petra's own incomplete memory notes about previous deliveries ("was it 4 pallets or 6? what was the price again?") appears in the early panels — proof of how fragile memory is compared with an actual history. The notebook is put away from the turning point onwards.

**Logo rule:** navichain's official logo is completely absent from panels 1–6. It is introduced discreetly from panel 7 — as a small icon in the corner of the portal's order-detail view, a logo in the header of the invoice preview, and finally on Petra's coffee mug in the last panel. Never centred, never more than about 5% of the panel area, never advertising-like. *The official logo is uploaded as a source file in the project — the illustrator must use that file at every specified placement, not recreate it by hand.*

**No IT support:** No support person or IT department appears anywhere, either at Petra's company or at Björklunda.

## Continuity note

The twenty-ninth episode in the series about connected workflows at Björklunda Åkeri AB, and Petra Vindh's second lead role — a direct continuation of her earlier story about cloning weekly orders (episode 26). Where that episode was about avoiding repeating herself forward in time, this one is about avoiding asking someone else backwards in time — the same self-service principle, but turned towards the history instead of the next order.

**Next in the series:** An angle not yet used — how Björklunda handles a situation where a customer wants to change an already confirmed delivery at the last minute, and how clear rules for what can still be changed prevent misunderstandings in both directions.

## Production notes for the illustrator

- **The notebook** is the episode's clearest recurring motif: full of question marks in panel 2, put away on a shelf in panel 11.
- **Colour dramaturgy:** Red dominates panels 1–3 (uncertainty, dependence on others), orange is the turning point (panels 4–5), petroleum green dominates panels 6–12.
- **Petra's manager** is a new, brief supporting role — visible only in panels 1 and 10, no additional plot line is needed.
- **Panels 6–7 must be clearly visually distinct** (timeline and invoice preview respectively), so it is clear that these are two separate views Petra is looking at, not one all-encompassing "history view".
- **Physical props to prioritise:** the notebook with uncertain memory notes, the telephone handset that is never fully lifted, the coffee mug with a discreet logo in the last panel.
