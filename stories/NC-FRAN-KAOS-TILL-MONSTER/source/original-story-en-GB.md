## panel_01 — The premium

Early morning at Kvarnviks Åkeri. Cecilia is sitting at her modern office desk. In front of her is an insurance letter with the new premium highlighted. Around her are binders, printed damage records, post-it notes and open email threads on her laptop.

**Caption:** “The insurance cost is rising again. But no one can really show why.”

## panel_02 — No big deal

In the haulage yard, Cecilia is standing with Robban beside a modern European truck. A damaged box is sitting on a pallet by the loading bay. Robban points to it and shrugs.

**Robban:** “It happens all the time at that bay. I usually just write a note.”

**Caption:** “No big deal. That is exactly the problem.”

## panel_03 — Everything gets said

Cecilia is sitting at her desk with her phone in her hand. On the phone is a messy chat stream with photos of damaged goods and short messages such as “damage again”, “bay 4?” and “call Torbjörn”. Beside the phone are handwritten notes and a notebook.

**Caption:** “Everything gets said. Almost none of it becomes structured information that can be found again.”

## panel_04 — The weekend she does not have

Late evening. Cecilia is sitting alone in the office in front of three months of printouts and binders. A modern calculator lies beside the laptop. The wall clock shows 21:30. She is trying to sort customer, bay, day of the week and damage manually.

**Caption:** “She senses a pattern. Proving it by hand would take a weekend she does not have.”

## panel_05 — The shift in perspective

The next morning. Cecilia leans back with her phone. On a sheet of paper in front of her, she crosses out the question “WHOSE FAULT?” and writes “WHAT KEEPS RECURRING?” instead. Beneath the words, she sketches three small bars.

**Caption:** “What if every exception is not only a problem — but also a data point?”

## panel_06 — Three steps

Cecilia draws three clear boxes on a notepad: **FIELD → SHARED VIEW → ACTION**. No product screens, no technician and no IT support.

**Caption:** “Record. Gather. Act. Three steps — instead of thirty phone calls.”

## panel_07 — The event becomes data

A few weeks later at the same loading bay. Robban spots a damaged box on a pallet. He takes out his modern smartphone, photographs the damage and records a short note in the digital workflow in Navichain.

**Caption:** “Photo. Note. Saved in the same workflow as the transport.”

## panel_08 — Now it can be followed

In Cecilia’s modern office, the recorded event is shown in the shared operational view. Cecilia can see the context around the transport and the exception without searching through loose chats and paper notes.

**Caption:** “When the information is gathered in the same flow, she does not have to start from zero every time.”

## panel_09 — From event to action

Cecilia is sitting at her desk with the structured information in front of her and creates two follow-up points: corrective — handle the damaged goods; preventive — investigate the loading routine at bay 4 together with the customer. Cecilia is the one who thinks, prioritises and assigns the work.

**Caption:** “Now the work does not stop at ‘what happened?’. The next question becomes: what do we do about it?”

## panel_10 — The pattern

On Cecilia’s large screen is a simple aggregated chart of recurring registrations. **BAY 4** clearly stands out, and the sorting shows that the problems recur at certain times. No invented percentage is shown.

**Caption:** “What she could only sense among the binders can now be seen as a recurring pattern.”  
Secondary narration: **“Bay 4. Again.”**

## panel_11 — Facts instead of fingers

At Sundqvists Grossist, Cecilia and Torbjörn are standing together by the loading bay. Between them is a printout of the pattern and a simple sketch of the new loading routine. Together they identify where the process needs to change and shake hands.

**Caption:** “Not accusations. Facts. Now they can tackle the root cause together.”

## panel_12 — From cost to knowledge

Back at Kvarnviks Åkeri. The office is brighter and calmer than at the beginning. A results board reads: **EXCEPTIONS — FOLLOWED UP**, **RECURRING PATTERNS — VISIBLE**, **ACTIONS — OWNED & FOLLOWED UP**. Cecilia closes the old binder and writes in a new notebook: “Next tender — show how we work systematically.” Underneath: “Sustainability data — next step?”

**Caption:** “When exceptions become learning data, the same work can strengthen both the operation and the customer’s trust.”  
Final narration: **“The problem was never that exceptions happened. The problem was that no one saw the pattern.”**
