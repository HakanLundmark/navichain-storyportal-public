Six months of tachograph history.
Twenty vehicles.
Five working days.

That was the situation when Berit Holmgren received notice of a company audit.

Under the old routine, the answer would have been scattered across envelopes, USB sticks and manual downloads. One stick could be corrupt. A week could be missing. Someone could simply have forgotten.

This time, there was another way.

The tachograph files had already been retrieved automatically and archived continuously through Navichain's telematics setup.

When Berit needed the records, she could search the required period, verify the history and prepare the vehicle-unit and driver-card files for submission.

The point was not that a system could “save” an audit.

The point was that the data was already there before anyone knew it would be needed.

That is where automation creates real operational value:

• fewer manual steps
• less risk of missing files
• searchable history when the question arrives
• better control when time is short

People should not have to be the company's memory.

They should be able to use the information to make the right decisions.

That is Berit's story in “Six Months in Five Days.”
