# — A Navichain story —

# THE YES THAT BECAME A BOOKING, INSTANTLY

### Susanne and the offer that becomes a booking the same moment the customer says yes

**Angle:** The sixteenth episode in the series about connected workflows returns to the very start of the chain — before a booking even exists, when everything is still only an offer. The task is unique because of the short, sensitive moment between a customer saying yes and the assignment actually existing in the system. The longer that moment lasts, the greater the risk of an error, a gap, or a lost time slot.

**Source:** Navichain — Offers (verified: offers reuse the booking's organisations, dates, packages, charges, route, carrier and requirements. Default validity is seven days. Sending emails a PDF and a customer link and changes status to Sent to Customer. Customer confirmation automatically creates a booking and sends a booking confirmation. Request Changes records a reason and emails the team, without changing status. Cancelling a confirmed offer also cancels the created booking. Marketing-safe wording: "Create, send and track quotes that customers can confirm online" — not autonomous quoting or acceptance.)

## Cover concept

Susanne at her desk, an offer on the screen with a clear status label "Sent to Customer". Beside it, in a small circled inset, a customer clicking "Confirm" on their own screen — and at exactly the same moment a new booking lights up on Susanne's side, as if the two screens were breathing in sync. Title lettering in the same rust-red/petroleum-green combination as the other covers in the series.

---

## PANEL 1 — The offer as a separate document

**Visual:** Susanne puts together an offer in a standalone document and emails it as a PDF to a customer. No connection to any booking yet — just a loose document on its way out. **Text:** "An offer, as its own little document. Nothing connected to anything yet." **Status:** 🔴 Red — offer and booking live completely separately

## PANEL 2 — A "yes" that means double work

**Visual:** The customer calls and says yes. Susanne opens an empty booking form and starts entering exactly the same information that was already in the offer — addresses, dates, weight — once again, by hand. **Text:** "The same information, one more time. A 'yes' that still means a completely new form." **Status:** 🔴 Red — an accepted offer, duplicate data entry remains

## PANEL 3 — The date that became wrong

**Visual:** Susanne accidentally enters the wrong date when she transfers the booking by hand — one digit differs from the offer the customer actually approved. **Text:** "A date, copied down too quickly. Not the same one the customer actually said yes to." **Status:** 🔴 Red — an error that slips in between two screens

## PANEL 4 — The time slot that almost disappeared

**Visual:** While Susanne is still entering the booking by hand, another colleague is about to place another customer's assignment in exactly the same pickup time — no booking exists yet to show that the time is already taken. **Text:** "As long as the booking does not exist, the time is not reserved either — for anyone else." **Status:** 🔴 Red — a gap where two assignments almost collide

## PANEL 5 — The turning point: the offer is created where the booking will live

**Visual:** Susanne creates the next offer directly in Navichain instead of in a separate document — the same organisations, dates, packages and route that a booking would use, already there from the start. **Text:** "The same fields a booking would have had. Just not confirmed yet." **Status:** 🟠 Orange — the offer is built on the same foundation as what it can become

## PANEL 6 — The customer asks for a change, clearly

**Visual:** The customer receives the offer by email with their own link, but clicks "Request Changes" instead of confirming immediately — and briefly writes why. Susanne receives the reason straight into her inbox, with nothing to guess at. **Text:** "No half-heard phone call to interpret. One clear reason, straight into her inbox." **Status:** 🟠 Orange — the request is documented, not implied

## PANEL 7 — Adjusted and sent again

**Visual:** Susanne adjusts the offer according to the customer's request and sends it again. A discreet sticker with Navichain's official logo is visible in the corner of the offer view. **Text:** "One change, one new version, the same traceable thread all the way through." **Status:** 🟢 Green — the right offer, ready to become real

## PANEL 8 — Yes, and the booking already exists

**Visual:** The customer clicks "Confirm" on their own screen. At exactly the same moment a completed booking appears on Susanne's screen — the same information, no new data entry, a booking confirmation already on its way to the customer. **Text:** "One click from the customer. A completed booking, at the same time, without her touching the keyboard." **Status:** 🟢 Green — booking created the same moment as the yes

## PANEL 9 — No difference between what was promised and what was booked

**Visual:** Susanne compares the new booking with the original offer — exactly the same dates, addresses and weight. Nothing has had time to drift between the two. **Text:** "What she sent and what is now booked are the same thing, word for word." **Status:** 🟢 Green — no drift between promise and reality

## PANEL 10 — The time is reserved, immediately

**Visual:** The same colleague as in panel 4 tries to place another assignment in the same time — but the booking already exists, the time is already visibly occupied. No clash this time. **Text:** "The time was reserved from the first second — not only when someone had finished typing it in by hand." **Status:** 🟢 Green — no gap left for a collision

## PANEL 11 — When a customer changes their mind

**Visual:** Another, already confirmed offer needs to be cancelled after a call with the customer. Susanne cancels the offer — the linked booking is automatically cancelled with it, with no abandoned booking left to clean up manually. **Text:** "When she cancels the offer, the booking follows. Nothing is left behind, half-finished." **Status:** 🟢 Green — the whole flow stays connected, even when something is rolled back

## PANEL 12 — Susanne, more offers, the same day

**Visual:** Susanne leans back holding a coffee mug with Navichain's official logo, while a list of several sent and confirmed offers is visible on the screen — more completed, none of them entered twice. **Text:** "More offers in the same day. None of them entered one more time just to become real." **Status:** 🟢 Green — the chain is complete, from loose document to the same instant

## Illustrator reference

| Element | Instruction |
| --- | --- |
| **Style** | Warm 1950s tone, Hergé/Tintin *ligne claire*: even contour line, flat colour fields, no heavy halftone. Discreet offset grain, patina, soft edge wear. |
| **Colour dramaturgy** | Panels 1–4: rust-red, duplicate work and an invisible gap. Panels 5–6: rich orange, the offer is built on the right foundation, a clear request is documented. Panels 7–12: petroleum green, seamless and simultaneous. |
| **Susanne Dahl** | Practical sales clothing, energetic but slightly scattered posture in the red panels — composed and efficient in the green ones. |
| **Two screens in sync** | Recurring compositional motif, especially in panel 8: Susanne's and the customer's screens must clearly be shown as two separate surfaces reacting at exactly the same moment — not a delay, not an intermediate step. |
| **Important narrative instruction** | No panel may imply that Navichain writes offers or makes decisions for Susanne or the customer — the offer is created and sent by her, confirmed or questioned by the customer. The system connects what has already been decided; it decides nothing itself. |
| **Navichain's official logo** | Use the uploaded source file exactly as it is — never recreate it by hand. Absent in panels 1–4. Introduced discreetly from panel 7 (corner of the offer view) and then visible on the coffee mug in the final panel — never centred, never advertising-like, max roughly 5% of panel area. |
| **No IT support** | No external technician, no support case. Susanne solves everything herself through the interface — Navichain is self-explanatory throughout the story. |
| **Paper vs screen** | Panels 1–4 are dominated by the standalone PDF document and manual entry. From panel 5 onwards, the offer and booking live on the same foundation inside the system — the transition should be visible in how Susanne works, not merely mentioned in text. |

## Continuity note

The sixteenth episode in the series about connected workflows at Björklunda Åkeri AB, and the first to take place at the very earliest stage of the chain — before a booking even exists. Susanne Dahl is introduced as a new sales persona, a perspective the series has not previously given its own story even though several episodes have built on bookings that already existed. The episode shares a theme with Viktor's earlier story about never entering the same information twice, but moves the principle from the boundary between two separate systems to the boundary between a promise and a confirmation within the same system.

**Next in the series:** The empty return journey that never needed to be empty — how Navichain can find return loads along an already planned route home, so that an empty truck on its way back becomes the exception rather than the rule. An angle not yet used in the series.
