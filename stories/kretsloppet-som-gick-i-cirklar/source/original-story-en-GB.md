# NAVICHAIN STORY
## "THE LOOP THAT WENT ROUND IN CIRCLES"
### Malin and the round that finally found an order

**Series:** the navichain story
**Episode:** First part in a new persona thread — **Solvik Retur & Miljö AB**, a standalone track alongside Björklunda Åkeri (transport), Fors Maskinuthyrning (equipment rental) and Kron Elteknik (field service/electrical installation)
**New market segment:** Waste, recycling and circular material flows — commercial collection (business customers: shops, restaurants, offices, property owners), not municipal household-waste procurement
**Target audience:** Owners/CEOs and traffic managers/route planners at small and medium-sized recycling and waste companies with many daily collection points
**Core angle (not previously covered in the series):** That bookings from several customers located at the **same physical address** can be placed as a single physical stop on the run — instead of accidentally being handled as separate visits at different times of day
**Visual style:** Warm 1950s Hergé/Tintin *ligne claire* aesthetic — even black contour lines, flat colour areas, parchment/cream-white base, rust-red/mustard-yellow/petroleum-green/warm-gold palette, hand-lettered captions, lightly grainy offset-print patina with soft edge wear and slightly muted/faded tones — like a well-thumbed 1950s magazine. The story is set in the present day: in the red, unresolved phase Malin works with paper maps, loose booking slips and a whiteboard; from the turning point onwards everything is handled via computer and mobile phone instead of paper.

---

## Background to the story

**Solvik Retur & Miljö AB** collects waste paper, cardboard, food waste and mixed recyclable material from business customers in a medium-sized Swedish city — shops, cafés, offices and shopping centres. Many customers are geographically dispersed, but some are physically at the same address: a shopping centre or retail area can have several separate contracted customers (different shops, same property). When each customer is registered as a separate case without anyone linking the fact that they actually share a loading bay, the same truck risks passing the same property several times during the same day — without collecting everything that was already there.

## Product grounding (verified against the project documentation)

- **Core capability:** Several bookings can represent pickup/delivery events at the **same physical stop** on a run — several customers' cases can therefore be collected under one and the same physical visit instead of automatically becoming separate stops. Source: NAVICHAIN-PRODUCT-TRUTH.md / NAVICHAIN-WORKFLOWS.md, section on Runs ("A stop event identifies booking, pickup/delivery action and exact package quantities. Several bookings can share a physical stop."), as well as NAVICHAIN-CAPABILITY-INDEX.md, entry 32: "Multi-booking events at one physical stop" (Transport/Runs, USER-INITIATED, VERIFIED).
- **Booking → run flow:** Add Bookings creates pickup/delivery events; several customers' bookings are selected into the run by the planner. This is a **user-initiated** action — the planner identifies and groups them; the system does not automatically determine which addresses are "actually" the same place.
- **Mobile execution:** The driver confirms quantities, photo and signature per stop in the mobile app; GPS and timestamp are set automatically. Source: NAVICHAIN-WORKFLOWS.md, "Driver assignment and mobile execution".
- **Status logic:** Planning status (Unplanned/Partial/Fully Planned) is derived automatically from quantities on the run's stops — there is no manual "mark as planned". Source: NAVICHAIN-PRODUCT-TRUTH.md, Bookings.

## What is NOT claimed

- Navichain does **not automatically** identify which customer addresses physically coincide — it is the planner (Malin) who recognises this and actively groups the bookings into the same stop.
- No route-optimisation algorithm (stop reordering) is the core of this story — that capability has already been dramatised in an earlier navichain story and is not reused here as the main mechanism.
- No weighing, material classification or automatic environmental reporting (CO2/circular traceability) is claimed — this is a flagged, as-yet untreated angle for a future episode in the same thread, not something shown or implied in this story.
- No municipal procurement or authority integration is mentioned — the story explicitly concerns commercial/business-customer collection, not household waste or municipal contracts.
- No IT support function appears; Malin and Pontus use the platform independently, without guidance.

## Preflight corrections made in this chat

The following minimal Product Truth clarifications were specified before image production and should be read as corrections to the original script above:

1. Panel 8: `Three bookings. One row on the run.` → `Three bookings. One stop on the run.` Reason: Product Truth verifies several booking events at the same physical stop, but not a specific UI representation as exactly one row.
2. Panel 9: `He photographs the containers and confirms the quantities for all three at the same time.` → `At the same stop he records quantities and a photo for the three bookings.` Reason: the mobile app supports stop-by-stop events, but not a specific shared "confirm all at once" action.
3. Panel 12 teaser: `exactly which bin has already been emptied` → `Next: how Pontus can see what has already been recorded at the stop — without having to call and ask.` Reason: avoids a claim about persistent bin/container identity that has not been verified.
