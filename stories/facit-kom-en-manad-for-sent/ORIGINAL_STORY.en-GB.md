# A navichain story

# THE ANSWER CAME A MONTH TOO LATE

### Ingrid & the search for a TMS built for tomorrow

**Tagline (cover):** She knew exactly what every run had cost. A month after it was already too late.

**Source:** Navichain's article "Stop choosing a TMS based on *history* — Start choosing based on the future" (blog.navichain.se/valj-tms-baserat-pa-framtiden/)

**Protagonist:** Ingrid Sund, 51 — Finance Director, Nordanby Logistik AB (45 vehicles, distribution and long-haul operations in central Sweden). Short dark-blonde hair, reading glasses on a chain, knitted blazer. Used to being the person in the room who has the numbers under control — until the numbers stopped arriving in time.

**Supporting characters:**

- Torsten Bäckman, 58 — CEO, Nordanby Logistik AB. Conservative, risk-averse, still stuck in the "safety in the familiar" mindset.
- Robban Ek — recurring driver, continuity appearance in panel 8 (his run is the one that is finally visible on the screen in real time).

**Angle (new, not previously used in the series):** The article's core theme — that TMS choices have historically been made using outdated criteria (the length of the reference list, the number of ready-made integrations) rather than what actually determines competitiveness going forward. The story makes **real-time profitability per run** (as opposed to guesswork in the monthly accounts) the main conflict, with **the rapid integration time of an API-first architecture** (days, not months) as the second decisive proof point. This is clearly different from earlier episodes that have already covered adjacent TMS themes: a pure feature comparison between competing systems (Rasmus, "The TMS Guide 2026"), hidden costs/implementation time when switching for a 10–50-vehicle haulier (Björn Wikander, "The Quote That Didn't Add Up"), general API/ERP connectivity to the customer's system (Viktor, "Connected Logistics"), and AI-driven order entry (Sandra, "The PDF That Booked Itself"). Ingrid is the series' first finance-director persona in the role of *purchase decision-maker* rather than daily user — her struggle is about redefining which questions are asked *before* a TMS contract is signed, not about one individual feature in operation.

**Format:** 12 panels + cover, in the same visual language as the series' other scripts (warm 1950s ligne claire, Hergé/Tintin-inspired, parchment/cream base with rust, mustard, petroleum green and warm gold, hand-lettered captions, grainy offset-print patina with a lightly yellowed tone and soft edge wear).

**Colour coding:** 🔴 Red = problem/crisis (panels 1–4) · 🟠 Orange = insight/turning point (panels 5–6) · 🟢 Green = solution (panels 7–12)

**Logo rule:** navichain's official logo (uploaded as a source file in the project) is completely absent from panels 1–6. It is introduced discreetly from panel 7 onwards, exclusively as environmental set dressing — never more than about 5% of the panel area, never centred or advertising-like. No IT support appears in any panel; Ingrid navigates navichain entirely on her own, reinforcing that the platform is self-explanatory.

### Panel 1 — The accounts that reveal everything, too late

**Visual:** Ingrid's office, early morning. She is sitting with a thick binder labelled "MONTH-END ACCOUNTS — MARCH" open in front of her. One specific customer row is marked in red: "Customer: Berglunds Grossist — Contribution margin: −18,400 SEK". She stares at the figure with her hand against her forehead. **Caption:** March is over. Now, only now, Ingrid sees that an entire customer account has been making a loss — every week, for five weeks. **Status:** 🔴 Red — the loss is discovered a month too late

### Panel 2 — No one knew, because no one could see

**Visual:** Ingrid calls the traffic office. An uncertain voice is suggested through the receiver (sound waves). In front of her on the desk: trip logs, fuel receipts and a printed route list — none of them connected to a single profitability figure. **Caption:** "No one has done anything wrong. No one simply had the figures needed to see it." **Status:** 🔴 Red — invisible margin, no one to blame

### Panel 3 — The renewal meeting

**Visual:** Conference room. Torsten (CEO) sits opposite Ingrid, with a thick contract binder between them bearing the blurred/anonymised name of the current TMS system. Torsten points to a piece of text: "Market-leading — the most integrations in the industry." **Caption:** "We've used them for eight years, Ingrid. They have the most integrations on the market. It's safe." **Status:** 🔴 Red — renewal based on old credentials

### Panel 4 — The question no one had asked

**Visual:** Close-up of Ingrid, pen in hand; she has written a simple question in a notebook: "But do we know what a run costs — TODAY, or four weeks from now?" Eraser crumbs can be seen behind the pen — she has rewritten the question several times. **Caption:** The reference list answers yesterday's question. Ingrid realises she has been asking the wrong question for eight years. **Status:** 🔴 Red — the right question, but no answer yet

### Panel 5 — Evening reading

**Visual:** At home at the kitchen table, late at night, coffee beside a laptop. The screen shows clean article text — no logo yet — with the headline visible: "Stop choosing a TMS based on history." One line is highlighted in yellow: "Is your system an engine for growth, or an administrative burden?" **Caption:** "Real-time profitability. Not in the monthly report — in real time." Ingrid reads the sentence twice. **Status:** 🟠 Orange — someone else finally puts the insight into words

### Panel 6 — Ingrid's own checklist

**Visual:** Ingrid at the kitchen table rewrites her old evaluation template. The old sheet (thrown into the wastepaper basket beside her) was headed "NUMBER OF INTEGRATIONS". The new sheet has four new headings: "Profitability in real time?", "New connections — days or months?", "Consultants required?", "Where is our data stored?" **Caption:** She tears up the old template. The questions that determine the future are not the same ones that determined the last purchase. **Status:** 🟠 Orange — new criteria, new basis for the decision

### Panel 7 — The demo that answers immediately

**Visual:** Ingrid's office, daytime. She is sitting at a CRT-like screen during a video call, with a navichain representative on one half of the screen and a live dashboard on the other: "PROFITABILITY PER RUN — TODAY", with green bars and one red bar. A discreet corner mark with navichain's official logo appears at the top of the screen. **Caption:** No question gets the answer "we'll see that in the next month-end accounts." The figure is already there, run by run. **Status:** 🟢 Green — real time, not guesswork

### Panel 8 — Robban's run, visible as it happens

**Visual:** Close-up of the screen: a specific row in the list, "ROUTE 4 — DRIVER: R. EK — CONTRIBUTION MARGIN: +640 SEK", updates while the truck is still on the road (a small map icon with a moving point beside it). A small navichain sticker can be seen discreetly in the corner of the screen. **Caption:** Robban's route is still under way — and Ingrid can already see that it is profitable. No one waits until month-end any more. **Status:** 🟢 Green — profitability visible while the run is in progress

### Panel 9 — The integration question

**Visual:** Ingrid and an IT colleague (brief scene, no support person from navichain) look together at a simple status list on a tablet: "Payroll — connected", "Invoicing system — connected", "Telematics — connected", all with green ticks and a date three days earlier. **Caption:** Three connections that would have taken months in the old system. Three days. No consultant involved. **Status:** 🟢 Green — API-first, no months of waiting

### Panel 10 — The second meeting with Torsten

**Visual:** The same conference room as in panel 3, but now Ingrid shows Torsten the profitability dashboard on her tablet instead of a contract binder. Torsten leans forward, impressed, pointing at the graph. The binder marked "most integrations" is closed and set to one side. **Caption:** "You asked about safety, Torsten. Here it is — and it updates every hour." **Status:** 🟢 Green — the decision turns, with the right evidence

### Panel 11 — Five weeks later, the same warning signal — but in time

**Visual:** Ingrid at her desk; the same type of customer name as in panel 1 appears in the real-time view with an amber warning flag: "Customer: Ahlqvist Bygg — Margin declining, week 2 of 4". She is already writing an action on a sticky note: "Book meeting — renegotiate price." **Caption:** The same kind of leakage as in March. This time it is visible in week two — not in the accounts five weeks later. **Status:** 🟢 Green — the problem is caught while there is still time to fix it

### Panel 12 — Epilogue: The office in afternoon light

**Visual:** Ingrid leans back in her chair, holding a cup of coffee with navichain's official logo, while the screen behind her shows a calm green overview. A final line is written in a notebook in the foreground: "Next: The exception no one could predict." Through the window, Nordanby Logistik's yard can be seen in the evening sun. **Caption:** She no longer chooses systems based on who was biggest eight years ago. She chooses based on what she can actually see — today. **Status:** 🟢 Green — ending + teaser for the next story
