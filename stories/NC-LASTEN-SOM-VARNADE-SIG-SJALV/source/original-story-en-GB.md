# Storyboard: “The Cargo That Warned About Itself”

### How Erik stopped the dangerous combination before the gate even opened

**Series:** A Navichain story (standalone instalment, in the same universe as Maria, Johan, Elin, Stefan, Yvonne, Malin, Bengan, Cecilia, Torsten, Patrik, Linda, Micke, Karin and others.)

**Source:** navichain.se/plattformen/ecmr/ — angle: **the ADR module, warning about incompatible cargo combinations** (UN number register, automatic ADR consignment note, the driver’s ADR certificate verified digitally).

**Continuity:** This story pays off the teaser from Torsten’s story ("The Paper That Couldn’t Lie"): *“Next: the driver who spotted dangerous goods before the load had even rolled out of the warehouse.”*

**Angle note for Håkan:** ADR has been touched on earlier in the series (the Maria Boström story, which was about matching the right driver’s ADR competence to an assignment under time pressure). This story deliberately covers another part of the same regulatory framework — not competence matching, but the system detecting that *two types of goods must not travel together* before the load leaves the terminal. New conflict, new feeling, new protagonist.

**Character:** Erik Wallin, 29 — driver, Frejlunds Åkeri AB. ADR-certified, used to driving mixed loads at short notice.

**Supporting characters:** Sonja Ek, terminal worker (loads the goods), Gunnar Broman, terminal manager (Erik’s immediate manager).

**Format:** 12-panel illustrator brief.

**Colour coding:** 🔴 Red = time pressure and blind loading (panels 1–6) · 🟠 Orange = the warning is detected (panels 7–8) · 🟢 Green = controlled, correct and safe departure (panels 9–12)

---

## Visual style (applies to all panels — series standard)

Warm 1950s Hergé/Tintin “ligne claire”: even black contour line, flat/even colour areas (minimal halftone screen, no heavy hatching), parchment/cream-white base colour with rust red, mustard yellow, petroleum green and warm gold. Handwritten/hand-drawn caption lettering — never digital sans-serif. The surface should have a light offset-print patina: subtle paper grain, soft edge wear, slightly faded, muted colour tones — like a well-thumbed 1950s magazine.

**Modern action, classic style:** The story takes place in the present day. Before Navichain, everything is handled manually — paper consignment notes, checklists, phone calls. From the point where Navichain enters the picture, Erik and Sonja work with their mobile phones and a tablet at the terminal — never paper, never a screen that looks like an IT support ticket. No IT department appears or is needed in the story; Navichain is self-explanatory and is used directly by Erik himself, in the middle of the work.

**Physical props before digital:** clipboard with paper consignment note, marker pen, goods labels with UN numbers and hazard symbols in the problem panels. Mobile phone/tablet with the Navichain app in the solution panels.

**Character continuity:** Erik, Sonja and Gunnar must remain visually and emotionally consistent through all 12 panels — the same facial features, the same clothes, the same terminal environment.

## Navichain logo — placement rules (mandatory)

- **The Navichain logo means Navichain’s actual official brand logo — not a generic “N” symbol or monogram.** Instruct the illustrator to use the official logo file (SVG/PNG, supplied by Håkan) at every specified placement below, in the correct colour and proportions.
- **Completely absent in panels 1–6** (the problem phase — loading takes place manually, with no system support visible).
- **Introduced discreetly from panel 7:**
  - Panel 7: as a small app-icon corner at the top of Erik’s mobile screen when the warning is shown
  - Panel 8: as a small footer logo on the tablet screen when the UN numbers are checked
  - Panels 9–10: as a discreet watermark in the corner of the digitally generated ADR consignment note
  - Panel 11: as a small decal on the driver’s door of the truck
  - Panel 12: on Erik’s and Gunnar’s coffee mugs
- **Never larger than about 5% of the panel area. Never centred. Never advertising-like** — always a natural environmental prop.

## Panel 1 — 🔴 The Morning

**Setting:** Frejlunds Åkeri AB, loading bay, early morning, misty light. **Image:** Erik arrives with his truck at the loading bay. A whiteboard by the gate shows the day’s routes — four deliveries, tight timings. He looks at the clock. **Caption:** Wednesday morning, 06:20. Four deliveries before lunch — and the truck is only half loaded. **Status:** 🔴 Time pressure

## Panel 2 — 🔴 The Loading

**Setting:** Loading bay, pallets in a row. **Image:** Sonja loads pallets at high speed, a paper clipboard in her hand as she ticks off a list. Two pallets end up close together on the truck — one marked with an orange “oxidising substance” diamond, another with a red “flammable liquid” diamond. **Caption:** Sonja ticks off the list on the move. Right number of packages. Wrong question asked. **Status:** 🔴 Manual handling

## Panel 3 — 🔴 The Labels

**Setting:** Close-up of the load compartment. **Image:** The two hazard labels in a clear close-up, side by side on the pallets, UN numbers visible (for example UN 1479 and UN 1993). No one sees them together — only one at a time. **Caption:** No one on the floor knows that the two types of goods must not travel in the same compartment. It is in the rulebook — not on the consignment note. **Status:** 🔴 Hidden risk

## Panel 4 — 🔴 The Doors

**Setting:** Rear of the load compartment. **Image:** Erik closes and locks the rear doors. He has not seen the labels — he is in a hurry. **Caption:** Erik closes the doors. The timetable is the only rule he has time to think about. **Status:** 🔴 Unaware

## Panel 5 — 🔴 The Radio

**Setting:** Driver’s cab. **Image:** Erik in the driver’s seat, hand on the ignition key. An old vehicle radio/communications radio on the dashboard carries a voice (shown as a small speech bubble): “Go now, or you’ll miss the customer’s time window.” **Caption:** “Go now, or you’ll miss the time window” crackles over the radio. No time for a double-check. **Status:** 🔴 External pressure

## Panel 6 — 🔴 The Key

**Setting:** Driver’s cab, the loading-bay gate open in front of the truck. **Image:** Erik turns the ignition key. The engine starts. The gate begins to slide open. **Caption:** The key turns. The load is on its way out through the gate. **Status:** 🔴 Nothing is stopping it — yet

## Panel 7 — 🟠 The Warning

**Setting:** Driver’s cab, engine running. **Image:** Erik’s mobile phone on the passenger seat lights up and vibrates strongly. The screen shows a clear red warning box in the Navichain app. *(Navichain logo: small app-icon corner at the top of the screen.)* **Caption:** The phone vibrates. A red warning box: “ADR warning — incompatible cargo combination detected. Check loading before departure.” **Status:** 🟠 The system reacts

## Panel 8 — 🟠 The Check

**Setting:** Outside the truck, engine switched off again, Erik outside with a tablet in his hand. **Image:** Erik stands by the reopened rear doors and holds the tablet up towards the two pallets’ labels, comparing the UN numbers with the text on the screen. *(Navichain logo: small footer logo on the tablet screen.)* **Caption:** UN 1479 and UN 1993 — oxidising substance and flammable liquid. The system recognised the combination in a second. **Status:** 🟠 Cause identified

## Panel 9 — 🟢 The Reloading

**Setting:** Loading bay. **Image:** Erik and Sonja together lift one of the pallets off and place it on a separate trolley, marked for another run later the same day. Relief on their faces. **Caption:** The pallet is lifted off. Next run, another truck, another combination — the problem is solved before it ever becomes one. **Status:** 🟢 Corrected

## Panel 10 — 🟢 The Consignment Note

**Setting:** Loading bay, tablet in Sonja’s hand. **Image:** The screen shows an automatically generated ADR consignment note containing only the goods that are actually allowed to travel together, plus a green tick beside “Driver’s ADR certificate — verified”. *(Navichain logo: discreet watermark in the corner of the consignment note.)* **Caption:** A new ADR consignment note is created automatically — only the goods that are actually allowed to travel together. Erik is already certified and approved in the system. **Status:** 🟢 Documented and correct

## Panel 11 — 🟢 The Departure

**Setting:** Outside the terminal, clearing morning sky. **Image:** The truck rolls out through the gate, Erik calm behind the wheel. A discreet decal is visible on the driver’s door. *(Navichain logo: small decal on the driver’s door.)* **Caption:** The truck rolls out — ten minutes late, but with the right load. No dangerous combination in the load compartment, just real control. **Status:** 🟢 Safe departure

## Panel 12 — 🟢 The Evening (teaser)

**Setting:** Terminal office, late afternoon, warm golden light through the window. **Image:** Gunnar and Erik stand with a coffee mug each, laughing a little about the morning’s drama. *(Navichain logo: discreet on both coffee mugs.)* A yellow note with a handwritten teaser phrase is visible in the corner of the panel. **Caption:** Gunnar shakes his head, almost amused: “We would never have spotted that by hand.” Erik smiles and slips the phone into his pocket. **Teaser note (handwritten):** “Next: the booking that was ready before the coffee had time to cool.” **Status:** 🟢 Confidence, every day

## Production note for the illustrator

- **Theme:** From blind manual loading to a system that itself recognises dangerous combinations and stops them in time — without anyone needing to call IT or read a rulebook.
- **Colour dramaturgy:** Red (time pressure, hidden risk) → orange (the warning is detected, the cause becomes clear) → petroleum green (corrected, documented, safe).
- **Navichain is always self-explanatory:** Erik and Sonja solve everything themselves, directly in the app/tablet, in the middle of the work — no screen that looks like a support ticket, no third person who “helps with the technology”.
- **Physical before digital:** Clipboard, marker pen and paper labels in panels 1–6 should feel palpably manual and vulnerable — the contrast with the mobile/tablet in panels 7–12 should be clear without becoming a screenshot collage.
- **Navichain logo:** Only Navichain’s actual official logo (the file exists in the project source) — never an invented “N” symbol or placeholder.
