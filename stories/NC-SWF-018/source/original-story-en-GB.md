# — A navichain story —

# NO ONE ON SITE, STILL A PLAN

### Kalle, Erik and the delivery that found a clear way forward

**Angle:** The eighteenth episode in the series about connected workflows is about what does not go according to plan — not because anyone has done something wrong, but because reality sometimes refuses to cooperate: a closed gate, no recipient, the wrong address. This situation is unique because it requires the chaos of the moment to still land in something structured: a recorded reason and a clear next step, without the driver having to improvise on site.

**Source:** Navichain — Bookings/Other transport functions (verified: a failed delivery records a reason, and a user can create a reattempt. Delivered bookings can create returns with reversed addresses.)

Erik, who previously built the board for follow-up tasks, is the person who makes sure that this type of deviation always gets a clear continuation.

## Cover concept

Kalle at a closed gate, phone in hand, a parcel in his arms with no one there to receive it. In a small circled inset beside him, Erik at his desk, where the same case has already appeared, ready to be handled. Title lettering in the same rust-red/petroleum-green combination as the rest of the series covers.

---

## PANEL 1 — No one at the gate

**Visual:** Kalle Nordin arrives at the delivery address — the gate is locked, no one answers the bell, no recipient is in sight. He remains standing there with the parcel, unsure what happens next. **Text:** "Right address. Wrong situation. No one there to hand it to." **Status:** 🔴 Red — a delivery that cannot be completed

## PANEL 2 — The call no one quite knows what to do with

**Visual:** Kalle calls the office and explains the situation verbally. The person on the other end listens, writes something down on a loose sheet of paper, uncertain about exactly what the next step should be. **Text:** "One call, one loose sheet of paper, and no one quite sure what happens next." **Status:** 🔴 Red — improvisation instead of a clear process

## PANEL 3 — The parcel that stays in the vehicle

**Visual:** Kalle drives on with the parcel still in the cargo area, with no clear plan for when or how it will be delivered. It takes up space for the rest of the week's jobs. **Text:** "A parcel with no plan, left in the vehicle. No one really knows when it will be resolved." **Status:** 🔴 Red — an unresolved case that disrupts the rest of the work

## PANEL 4 — The question no one could answer

**Visual:** A previous, similar situation comes back to mind: a customer who later asked why a delivery was delayed, and no one at Björklunda could say with certainty what had actually happened. **Text:** "'What actually happened?' Last time, there was no certain answer." **Status:** 🔴 Red — an old pattern without documentation

## PANEL 5 — The turning point: a reason, recorded immediately

**Visual:** Kalle records the failed delivery attempt directly in the mobile app and selects a clear reason: "No recipient on site." The case now exists in black and white before he has even driven away. **Text:** "Not a loose sheet of paper. A reason, recorded right where it happened." **Status:** 🟠 Orange — the deviation documented, not merely described

## PANEL 6 — Erik sees it straight away

**Visual:** Erik sees the failed delivery attempt appear in his case flow, with Kalle's recorded reason clearly visible. He immediately starts considering what the next step should be. **Text:** "No waiting for a call that may or may not come. The case was already where he looks first." **Status:** 🟠 Orange — follow-up begins without delay

## PANEL 7 — A new attempt, planned

**Visual:** Erik creates a new delivery attempt linked to the same booking, with a new, confirmed time. Kalle receives a clear notification of when and where he should try again. A discreet sticker with Navichain's official logo is visible in the corner of the form. **Text:** "Not 'drive past again and see'. A new, fixed time — known in advance." **Status:** 🟢 Green — a new attempt, not a guess

## PANEL 8 — A different situation, a different solution

**Visual:** For another, similar case, Erik instead chooses to create a return — the customer no longer wants the delivery. The addresses are reversed automatically, ready to be sent back without having to be rebuilt from scratch. **Text:** "The same type of deviation. But this time, a different solution — without starting again from zero." **Status:** 🟢 Green — the right way forward, chosen for this specific case

## PANEL 9 — Kalle delivers, the second time

**Visual:** Kalle drives back to the same address at the new, confirmed time — this time someone is there, and the delivery is completed without problems. **Text:** "Same address. This time, the right time, the right person there." **Status:** 🟢 Green — the delivery completed, without improvisation

## PANEL 10 — The return, on its way back

**Visual:** The other case's return rolls back towards the original address, with the addresses correctly reversed — nothing for anyone to work out manually about where it should actually go. **Text:** "Back to where it came from, without anyone having to work out the route again." **Status:** 🟢 Green — the return, as simple as the delivery was meant to be

## PANEL 11 — The answer that now exists

**Visual:** A customer gets in touch and asks why a delivery was delayed. Erik opens the case: reason, new attempt, time, result — everything clear and in order. **Text:** "'What actually happened?' This time, there is an answer straight away." **Status:** 🟢 Green — the question that previously had no answer, answered in seconds

## PANEL 12 — Kalle and Erik, an ordinary afternoon

**Visual:** Kalle parks for the day, with nothing left in the cargo area that does not belong there. Erik, in a small circled inset, leans back holding a coffee mug with Navichain's official logo. **Text:** "Not every delivery goes to plan. But now everyone knows what happens when it doesn't." **Status:** 🟢 Green — the chain complete, from improvisation to a clear way forward

**Post-it on Erik's desk (teaser):** *"Next: the certificate that was about to expire — and that no one had to discover at the last second."*
