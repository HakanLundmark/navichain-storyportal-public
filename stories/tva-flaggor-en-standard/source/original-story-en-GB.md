## Storyboard: "Two flags, one standard" — the Trelleborg–Rostock deal that almost fell apart

**Format:** 12-panel storyboard, editorial flat illustration style (warm 1950s Hergé/Tintin *ligne claire*)  
**Colour coding:** 🔴 = problem/crisis, 🟠 = transition/uncertainty, 🟢 = solved/secured

**Characters:**
- **Klaus Vogel**, 47, dispatcher at **Rhein-Nord Spedition GmbH** (Rostock, Germany)
- **Birgitta Ahl**, 39, CEO of **Trelleborg Fraktpartner AB** (Trelleborg, Sweden)
- Robban Ek in a brief cameo as a driver on the ferry route (continuity character, in the background, not focal)

**Angle:** Unlike earlier episodes in the series (which focused on individual companies in one country), this story is built around a **cross-border partnership between a German and a Swedish haulage company** operating a joint route via the Trelleborg–Rostock ferry. What makes the angle compelling specifically for German and Swedish companies is that, within a single story, it shows that navichain can meet **both countries’ requirements at the same time** — without either party having to compromise:

- **Germany:** Works council co-determination for monitoring technology (§87 BetrVG) — a German employer cannot simply introduce vehicle tracking; the Betriebsrat must approve how and why it is used.
- **Sweden/EU:** The requirement to be able to show exactly where data is stored and under which legislation, without doubt during an audit.

The story shows that these are not two separate problems that happen to share a solution — it is **the same standard** that holds up to the most demanding bodies on both sides.

## Illustrator notes
- **The logo’s role and timing:** Completely absent from panels 1–4 (the first half of the problem/turning point), introduced discreetly from panels 5–7 as the solution is presented to each party, and progressively more present in panels 8–12. Maximum about 5% of panel area, never centred or advertisement-like. The illustrator must use navichain’s official logo (uploaded as a source file in the project) at each specified placement — not a stylised replacement.
- **No IT support in any panel:** The system should appear self-explanatory throughout — neither Klaus, Birgitta, the Betriebsrat representative nor the auditor needs help from a support function to understand or use navichain.
- **Emotional arc:** Klaus and Birgitta should be warm, expressive and genuinely relieved/happy in the solution panels (7–12) — not merely neutral or "the problem is solved" relaxed.
- **Robban Ek cameo:** He should be visible in the background in panels 4 and 9, without taking focus from the protagonists — a recognition detail for readers who have followed the series, not a speaking supporting character in the foreground.
- **Legal basis (for accuracy in dialogue/signage):** §87 Abs. 1 Nr. 6 BetrVG is the German statutory provision that gives the Betriebsrat co-determination rights when technical systems capable of monitoring employees’ behaviour or performance are introduced. This is the actual legal hook behind panels 1, 5 and 7 — keep the wording factual and avoid portraying the Betriebsrat as an opponent that is "defeated"; it should appear as a party whose requirements are reasonable and which navichain is already built to meet.
- **Differentiation from earlier episodes:** This story is the series’ first cross-border two-country story (compared with earlier single-company-in-one-country episodes such as the Micke/Anders story about driver privacy or the Petra story about data sovereignty). The topics of "driver privacy" and "data storage in the EU" are mentioned here briefly as already established functions rather than as the main tension, to avoid repetition — the main tension is instead **that both countries’ requirements must be met simultaneously within the same business relationship**.
