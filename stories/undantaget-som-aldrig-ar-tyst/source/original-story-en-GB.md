# — A Navichain story —

# THE EXCEPTION THAT IS NEVER SILENT

### Görel and the lock that requires a reason, not just a button press

**Angle:** The eleventh episode in the series about connected workflows deals with the opposite problem from several earlier episodes: not making something easier to change, but making sure that a booking whose CMR has already been issued and whose goods have already been picked up **cannot** be changed silently — while still allowing a real, justified exception when there is good reason, with a trace left behind. The task is unique because it requires both things at once: a strong safeguard and a functioning, traceable way to break it for good reason.

**Source:** Navichain — Bookings/Users & Roles, BookingLockOverride (verified: In Transit cannot be edited, Delivered is locked, and bookings with an issued CMR or already picked up are locked unless the user has the BookingLockOverride permission and provides a reason — which is recorded in the booking history.)

Görel is one of very few people at Björklunda who are allowed to touch a locked booking at all — a permission granted deliberately, not by default, and one that can never be used without leaving a trace behind.

Sara at her screen, with a booking in front of her marked by a clear padlock icon and the text "The booking is locked". ## PANEL 1 — The lock that stops Sara

**Visual:** Sara tries to correct an incorrect package count on a booking where the CMR has already been issued and the goods have already been picked up. The screen responds with a clear safeguard: "The booking is locked." **Text:** "An error she wants to correct. A lock that says no." **Status:** 🔴 Red — a necessary change, blocked

## PANEL 2 — The old habit

**Visual:** Sara remembers how it used to work in an earlier system — anyone could change anything, at any time, without it being visible afterwards who had done what. **Text:** "Before, anyone could change anything. Nobody knew who, or why." **Status:** 🔴 Red — unrestricted editing, no traceability

## PANEL 3 — The memory of a dispute

**Visual:** A short flashback: an old dispute where a customer questioned a delivered quantity, and nobody at the haulage company could say for certain whether the figure had been changed after the goods left the warehouse or not. **Text:** "Nobody could prove either one thing or the other. Only that nobody knew for sure." **Status:** 🔴 Red — an old problem with no evidence either way

## PANEL 4 — Sara does not have the permission

**Visual:** Sara checks her own permissions — nothing gives her the right to override the lock herself. Instead, she passes the case on to Görel with a short explanation. **Text:** "She can see the error. She cannot correct it herself. That is how it should be." **Status:** 🔴 Red — the right safeguard, but still an unresolved error

## PANEL 5 — The turning point: Görel opens the case

**Visual:** Görel reads Sara's explanation and opens the locked booking in Navichain. The same padlock icon is visible — but because she has the BookingLockOverride permission, she is offered a way forward, not just a no. **Text:** "The same lock. But for her there is a way forward — if she can explain why." **Status:** 🟠 Orange — a real exception within reach

## PANEL 6 — The reason that must be written

**Visual:** Before anything can be edited, Görel is met by a mandatory text field: "Enter reason for unlocking." She writes something short and specific: incorrect package count discovered before invoicing, corrected after checking with the warehouse. **Text:** "Not a tick in a box. A real sentence, written by a real person." **Status:** 🟠 Orange — no exception without an explanation

## PANEL 7 — The correction, exactly

**Visual:** Görel adjusts the incorrect package count to the correct figure and saves the change. The booking automatically returns to locked status immediately afterwards. A discreet sticker with Navichain's official logo is visible in the corner of the form. **Text:** "One figure corrected. The lock closes itself again straight afterwards." **Status:** 🟢 Green — the exception is over, the safeguard restored

## PANEL 8 — The history that remembers everything

**Visual:** Görel opens the booking's history tab: the time, her name and the written reason remain there, clearly timestamped beside the change itself. **Text:** "Who, when and why — all still there, exactly where the change was made." **Status:** 🟢 Green — traceability, not just a safeguard

## PANEL 9 — Sara sees the result

**Visual:** Sara opens the same booking again — the package count is now correct and the invoicing basis matches. She can also see the history and read exactly what happened without having to ask. **Text:** "The right figure. And the whole path to it, visible to her too." **Status:** 🟢 Green — resolved, and understandable afterwards

## PANEL 10 — The question that no longer lacks an answer

**Visual:** Weeks later, a customer gets in touch and asks whether anything was changed in this particular booking after the goods were picked up. Görel shows the history tab immediately — nothing to guess about. **Text:** "'Was anything changed?' Yes — and exactly what, by whom, and why. Nothing left to wonder about." **Status:** 🟢 Green — the dispute that never became a dispute

## PANEL 11 — The lock still holds for everyone else

**Visual:** Another colleague without the permission later that same day tries to change another locked booking — the same clear safeguard as in panel 1 stops him. Görel's exception was exactly that: an exception, not a loophole for everyone. **Text:** "The same lock, for everyone else. Görel's way around it was never a shortcut — only a documented option for the few who genuinely need it." **Status:** 🟢 Green — the safeguard still applies, as it should

## PANEL 12 — Görel, a cup of coffee and a calm afternoon

**Visual:** Görel leans back holding a coffee mug with Navichain's official logo, while the screen behind her shows an ordinary locked booking — exactly as it should be, undisturbed. **Text:** "A lock that holds. ## Continuity note

The eleventh episode in the series about connected workflows at Björklunda Åkeri AB, and the first deliberately about a safeguard rather than a tool for making something faster — an important balance in a series otherwise built around reducing friction. Görel Ask is introduced as a new lead character in a role defined by limited, responsible trust rather than broad access. Sara Lund returns in a supporting role, showing that the right action when faced with a locked case is to escalate, not look for a shortcut.

**Next in the series:** Several locations, one common foundation — how Navichain's department structure lets permissions inherit down through a growing organisation, without every new location or department having to be built from scratch and granted access from the ground up. An angle not yet used in the series.
