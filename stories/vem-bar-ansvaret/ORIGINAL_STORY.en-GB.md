# NAVICHAIN STORY
## "Who bears the responsibility? — The answer that was there before the question was asked"

**Series:** the navichain story
**Episode:** Follow-up to "Word against word — the damage that proved itself" (same persona thread: Fors Maskinuthyrning AB)
**Target audience:** Owners/CEOs of rental companies with their own or hired transport, dispatchers, and haulage companies carrying for rental businesses
**Core angle (not previously covered in the series):** The chain of responsibility for ADR/dangerous goods when the equipment is only temporarily hired out — who is legally responsible: the rental company, the carrier or the driver?
**Visual style:** Warm 1950s Hergé/Tintin *ligne claire* aesthetic — evenly weighted black outlines, flat colour areas, parchment/cream background, rust-red/mustard-yellow/petroleum-green/warm-gold palette, hand-lettered captions, physical attributes (certificates, stamps, signs, clipboards) ahead of screenshots where possible, light grainy offset print with patina and soft edge wear — like a well-thumbed 1950s magazine. The story is set in the present day: the characters work with computers and mobile phones, not paper.

---

## Main characters

- **Mikael Fors**, 44 — owner and day-to-day manager of **Fors Maskinuthyrning AB** (returning from the previous episode). He has just brought the damage documentation under control — now he faces a new, more dangerous problem.
- **Lena Bark**, 29 — handover/customer service manager at Fors Maskinuthyrning.
- **Robban Ek** — the series' recurring driver/haulier (continuity character), now driving for Fors Maskinuthyrning as a hired carrier on larger deliveries.
- **Inspector Anette Sund**, 38 — police officer/roadside inspector at a routine check. Factual, not hostile — she represents the system outside the company, not an antagonist to convince but a reality to meet prepared.

---

## Story background
Fors Maskinuthyrning does not only rent out excavators and construction hoists — they also rent out diesel-powered generators, air compressors and smaller fuel tanks to construction sites. Several of these count as dangerous goods in transport (ADR). When the equipment is to be delivered or collected, it is not always the same person who knows that it is ADR-classified, who is certified to drive it, or what may be loaded together with what.

## Colour dramaturgy
- **Panels 1–6 (red):** Responsibility is invisible until it is too late — nobody knows for certain who should have checked what.
- **Panels 6/7 (orange):** The turning point — Mikael realises that the question is not "whose fault was it", but "why wasn't the answer there before the load left the yard".
- **Panels 8–12 (petroleum green):** Resolution — ADR status, driver certificates and co-loading rules are visible to everyone before anything leaves the yard.

**Logo placement:** navichain's official logo is entirely absent from panels 1–6. It is introduced discreetly from panel 7 onwards as environmental set dressing (app header on the phone/screen, a decal on the truck door, a binder spine in the office) — never centred, never advert-like, never more than about 5% of the panel area. The illustrator uses navichain's official logo from the uploaded source file in the project at every specified placement.

**No IT support characters appear.** The platform should come across as self-explanatory in all resolution panels — Mikael, Lena and Robban configure and use navichain entirely by themselves.

**Terminology used (grounded in Navichain's product documentation):** ADR classification, certificates and equipment, together with a documented segregation rule as part of compliance support; driver profile with ADR type and expiry date; compliance expiry dates are displayed but never renewed automatically — that is always a human action. Navichain calculates, validates, flags and records; the platform does not replace the dispatcher's or driver's own responsibility and does not "guarantee" compliance.
