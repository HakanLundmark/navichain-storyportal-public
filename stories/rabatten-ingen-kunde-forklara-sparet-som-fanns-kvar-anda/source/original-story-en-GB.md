# NAVICHAIN STORY
## “The discount no one could explain — the trail that remained anyway”

**Series:** the Navichain story
**Episode:** Fourth part in the same persona thread (Fors Maskinuthyrning AB) — after “Word against word”, “Who carries the responsibility?” and “Expired inspection”
**Target audience:** Owners/CEOs of rental and transport companies with several employees who have authority to adjust prices, finance managers
**Core angle (not previously covered in the series):** Internal traceability when prices are changed on already confirmed orders — not a customer trust problem, but an internal governance problem: who gave the discount, and why?
**Visual style:** Warm 1950s Hergé/Tintin *ligne claire* aesthetic — evenly weighted black outlines, flat colour areas, parchment/cream background, rust red/mustard yellow/petroleum green/warm gold palette, hand-lettered captions, physical attributes (quarterly reports, adding machines, notebooks) ahead of screenshots where possible, light grainy offset print with patina and soft edge wear — like a well-thumbed 1950s magazine. The story takes place in the present day: the characters work with computers and mobile phones, not paper.

---

## Main characters

- **Mikael Fors**, 44 — owner and day-to-day manager of **Fors Maskinuthyrning AB** (recurring). He has solved damage documentation, ADR responsibility and inspection checks — now he discovers that margin is leaking in a fourth, invisible way.
- **Lena Bark**, 29 — handover manager/customer service at Fors Maskinuthyrning (recurring).
- **Emil Strand**, 22 — summer temp and rental assistant, new character. Ambitious and customer-friendly, but used to resolving conflicts by giving way on price — without anyone having told him to stop.
- **Robban Ek** — the series’ recurring driver/haulier (continuity character), makes a brief cameo in the resolution.
- **Kenneth Dahl**, 51 — building contractor and regular customer (recurring), this time in the role of the person who — without bad intent — unknowingly triggers the problem by always bargaining.

---

## Story background
Fors Maskinuthyrning has grown to a point where Mikael no longer handles every booking himself. Emil and Lena have authority to confirm orders and sometimes adjust the price to appease a dissatisfied customer. The problem is not that they are doing something wrong — it is that no one, including Mikael, can see afterwards who gave which discount, and why.

---

## Colour dramaturgy
- **Panels 1–6 (red):** Margin leaks quietly — no single discount is large, but no one can explain the total.
- **Panels 6/7 (orange):** The turning point — Mikael realises the solution is not to remove Emil’s authority, but to make every exception visible and justified.
- **Panels 8–12 (petroleum green):** Resolution — price changes on confirmed orders require a stated reason, visible in the history — without killing human flexibility.

**Logo placement:** Navichain’s official logo is completely absent from panels 1–6. It is introduced discreetly from panel 7 onwards as environmental props (app header on the screen, a binder spine in the office, a coffee mug) — never centred, never advertisement-like, never more than about 5% of the panel area. The illustrator uses Navichain’s official logo from the uploaded source file in the project at every specified placement.

**No IT support characters appear.** The platform must come across as self-explanatory in all resolution panels — Mikael, Lena and Emil configure and use Navichain entirely by themselves.

**Important narrative instruction:** Emil must never be portrayed as dishonest or incompetent. He is doing exactly what he believes is expected of him — keeping customers happy. The solution is about visibility and accountability, not about casting suspicion on an individual employee.

## Cover concept

A quarterly statement on Mikael’s desk, with a column of red minus signs that do not add up to what he remembers selling. Beside it: a blurred, out-of-focus image of several hands signing different receipts, none of them clearly identifiable. To the right in the same composition, in warm evening light: the same statement, but now with a clear column of named reasons beside every adjusted price. The title “THE DISCOUNT NO ONE COULD EXPLAIN — THE TRAIL THAT REMAINED ANYWAY” in hand-lettered typography, with the word “EXPLAIN” gradually receiving a clear underline towards the right-hand side.

## Summary table for the illustrator

| Before Navichain | With Navichain |
|---|---|
| Price changes on confirmed bookings happen without a trail | A change to a locked booking requires a stated reason, saved in the history |
| No one remembers why a particular customer received a discount | Every exception is searchable and linked to a person and a reason |
| Mikael discovers the gap only at the quarterly close | Patterns are visible continuously, not only afterwards |
| Staff flexibility feels like a risk that has to be removed | The flexibility remains — but becomes visible and accountable |
| The auditor asks questions no one can answer | Mikael already has the answer, built into every exception |
| No IT department is needed — Mikael builds the rules himself | Still no IT dependency — entirely self-explanatory |

**Terminology used (grounded in Navichain’s product documentation):** Confirmed/issued/picked-up bookings are locked for editing; a change requires the BookingLockOverride permission and a stated reason, which is recorded in the booking history. This is a permissions and traceability function — not an automatic approval or warning system — and the platform makes no decisions for Mikael about which discounts are reasonable.

**Next in the series:** “The equipment that existed — just in the wrong yard.” — about having the right machine in the fleet, but at the wrong depot when the customer needs it.
