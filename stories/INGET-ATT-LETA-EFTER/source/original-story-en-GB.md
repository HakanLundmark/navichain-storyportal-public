# — A Navichain story —

# NOTHING TO SEARCH FOR
### Anders and the inspection that took under a minute

**Format:** 12 panels + cover concept. Illustrator brief.

**Angle:** The twenty-fifth episode in the series about connected workflows at Björklunda Åkeri AB deliberately differs from the series' two earlier border-control stories: Bengan's story was about a physical CMR paper document that was damaged by rain and became unreadable (the document's *durability*), while Robban Ek's customs story was about a customs officer cryptographically verifying the document's authenticity via hash and timestamp (the document's *authenticity*). This story is about something much more ordinary and common: a completely routine inspection where the driver himself, not the inspector, is the one who needs to find the right document quickly — and where the problem is not that the paper is missing or damaged, but that it is *somewhere* among twenty other papers in the glove compartment.

**Source:** Navichain — Mobile App (verified: navichain 365 on iOS/Android/Web gives the driver run execution including CMR viewing in the app. The driver has a separate login linked to their profile.) The story is deliberately restrained: it specifically claims that the driver can show the CMR directly in the mobile app — which is verified — rather than generally claiming that *all* conceivable documents (driving licence, ADR certificate, insurance) are shown there, which is not documented as clearly.

**Important narrative instruction:** No IT department or support appears. Anders resolves the entire situation himself, with his own phone, without calling the office for help.

## Cover concept

Split image: on the left, a softly framed memory fragment of an older glove compartment crammed with loose papers, receipts and folders in disorder — a memory of how it used to be. On the right, in the present day, Anders calmly holding up his phone to the inspector, the screen clearly visible with a single document open and ready. Title lettering in the series' established rust-red/petroleum-green combination.

---

## VISUAL STYLE (applies to all panels)

Warm 1950s aesthetic in Hergé/Tintin *ligne claire*: clean contour lines of even thickness, flat colour fields without halftones or heavy cross-hatching. Parchment/cream base with rust red, mustard yellow, petroleum green and warm gold. Captions hand-lettered, never digital sans-serif. Physical props (the overfilled glove compartment, a high-visibility vest, a yellow highlighter) are prioritised over crisp UI mock-ups. Light grainy offset-print texture with patina, soft edge wear and foxing.

**The story takes place in the present day** (mobile phones, digital documents) but **is drawn in the classic style above**.

**Recurring motif:** the overfilled glove compartment dominates the early panels — loose papers in different formats, some folded, some with coffee stains. From the turning point onward, the glove compartment is barely visible, replaced by the simple, calm phone screen.

**Logo rule:** Navichain's official logo is completely absent in panels 1–6. It is introduced discreetly from panel 7 — as a small icon in the corner of the app interface, a sticker on Anders's phone case, and finally as a small logo on his thermos mug in the cab in the last panel. Never centred, never more than about 5% of the panel area, never advertisement-like. *The official logo is uploaded as a source file in the project — the illustrator must use that file in every specified placement, not redraw it by hand.*

**No IT support:** No support person or IT department appears anywhere. Anders solves everything himself, from the driver's seat.

## Continuity note

The twenty-fifth episode in the series about connected workflows at Björklunda Åkeri AB introduces Anders Kling as a new recurring driver persona, with a theme deliberately distinct from the series' earlier border-control stories: Bengan's story (paper damage) and Robban Ek's story (cryptographic authenticity verification by a customs officer) were both about the condition or authenticity of the document. This story is instead about the driver's own everyday convenience — never having to search.

## Production notes for the illustrator

- **Colour dramaturgy:** Red/orange dominates panels 1–3 (routine stress, memory of the old way), petroleum green dominates panels 4–12 — a faster transition than the series' usual pattern, to reflect how quickly the inspection itself actually proceeds.
- **The memory fragment in panel 2** should be visually separated (softer edge line or lighter tone) so it clearly reads as a memory/contrast, not a present-day scene.
- **The glove compartment in panel 10** should be a clear, brief visual parenthesis to panel 2 — the same angle, drastically emptier.
- **Anders Kling** is a new recurring driver persona alongside Robban Ek — note his appearance clearly for possible future cameos.
- **Physical props to prioritise:** the overfilled glove compartment in the memory image, the inspector's tablet, the thermos mug with discreet logo in the final panel.
