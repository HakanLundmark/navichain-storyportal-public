# A navichain story

# THE CALL THAT NEVER CAME

### Åsa & navichain's automatic notifications

**Tagline (cover):** The phone rang every eleven minutes. Until customers no longer needed to call.

**Source:** demosite.navichain.se/plattformen/orderhantering/ — angle: **Automatic notifications** — the customer is automatically informed by SMS or email at booking, departure, arrival and delivery, without manual handling.

**Angle note for Håkan:** The order management page has now given the series three distinct angles: Magic Drop order intake (Sandra's story), the customer portal's real-time tracking (Fredrik's story about the cargo owner's view), and now **the proactive notifications** — the mechanism itself that means the customer never needs to log in or call to ask, because the message is already on its way to them. A new protagonist with a job role not previously used in the series: customer service/support, not traffic control, finance or order intake. This story builds directly on the teaser in Sandra's closing panel ("the notification that arrives before the customer even has time to ask") and shares her workplace for continuity.

**Protagonist:** Åsa Ekström, 41 — customer service employee, Holmqvist Fjärrtransport AB (the same company as Sandra Lindkvist in the previous story). Short red hair, headset around her neck, always a pen at the ready beside a notepad full of torn paper slips.

**Supporting characters:** Sandra Lindkvist — order administrator (established character from the previous story, seen in the background/office). Robban Ek — driver (established continuity character, his delivery triggers the notification in the resolution). An irritated customer on the phone (panels 2–4), the same customer calm and satisfied in the final panels.

**Format:** 12-panel illustrator brief + cover concept.

---

## Visual style (for the illustrator — series standard)

- Warm 1950s style in Hergé/Tintin "ligne claire": even-thickness black contour lines, flat colour areas without halftone shading or heavy cross-hatching.
- Parchment/cream-white base, palette in rust red, mustard yellow, petroleum green and warm gold.
- Hand-lettered captions — never digital sans-serif.
- Lightly grainy offset-print texture with patina: subtle raster grain, paper texture, soft edge wear/foxing, muted and slightly faded tones — like a well-thumbed 1950s magazine found in a drawer.
- Physical props (headset, notepad, torn paper slips, a whiteboard full of sticky notes) are preferred to UI mock-ups in the problem panels. The story takes place in the present day, but the visual idiom is always that of the 1950s — retro CRT screens/period-shaped screens where screens are unavoidable.
- The characters are warm, expressive and sympathetic throughout, genuinely happy and smiling in the solution panels.
- **No IT support appears in any panel.** Åsa navigates navichain entirely on her own — no consultant, no support line, no training day. The system is self-explanatory.
- **From paper to screen:** In the problem panels (1–6), the telephone handset, sticky notes and a notepad full of crossed-out order numbers dominate. From panel 7 onwards, these are replaced by Åsa working at the computer and on the mobile phone — she no longer needs to call or write a single message to the customer herself.

### Logo rules (series standard)

- Always called **"navichain's official logo"** — never "the navichain logo", "the N mark", "the N logotype", "the N symbol" or "the N monogram".
- **Completely absent in panels 1–6** — deliberately, to strengthen the before/after contrast.
- Introduced discreetly from panel 7 onwards as environmental props: the corner of a screen, a coffee mug, a headset case, a mobile screen, a trolley decal.
- Maximum approximately 5% of the panel area. Never centred, never advertising-like.
- The official logo file is uploaded as a project source file. The illustrator must use that file exactly as it is in every specified placement — never an invented, interpreted or stylised replacement.

A whiteboard behind her is full of sticky notes with order numbers and question marks. **Caption:** Tuesday, 09:14. Sixth call this hour — and the clock has barely struck nine. **Status:** 🔴 Red — the phone is ringing without pause

## Panel 2

**Visual description:** Close-up of Åsa with the telephone handset to her ear, her face tense. A speech bubble from the customer at the other end: "You were supposed to deliver this morning. Where are the goods now?" **Caption:** "Where are my goods?" — the same question, for the fifth time today. **Status:** 🔴 Red — the same question, over and over again

## Panel 3

**Visual description:** Åsa holds the telephone handset against her chest and calls across the office to Sandra at her computer: "Do you know where booking 3042 is now?" Sandra stressfully flips between tabs to find the answer. **Caption:** To answer, she has to ask someone else — who in turn has to search. **Status:** 🔴 Red — the answer exists, but nobody has it to hand

## Panel 4

**Visual description:** Åsa gives the customer the wrong information — she reads an old note on the desk instead of the current status. The customer on the phone looks irritated in a small thought bubble: "It wasn't right last time either." **Caption:** An old piece of paper, a wrong answer — and trust that shrinks a little more. **Status:** 🔴 Red — incorrect information, lost trust

## Panel 5

**Visual description:** The clock shows 16:45. Åsa sits with her head in her hands, a long row of torn paper slips in front of her — each one a call about "where are my goods". **Caption:** A whole day. Forty calls. The same question, forty times. **Status:** 🔴 Red — exhaustion, no end in sight

## Panel 6

**Visual description:** Sandra comes past Åsa's desk with her computer in her hand, sees the pile of paper slips and nods in recognition. She turns the screen towards Åsa and points at something. **Caption:** "Åsa. I never had to copy orders by hand again. You don't need to call customers back any more either." **Status:** 🔴 Red — the final straw, but a solution is in sight

## Panel 7

**Visual description:** Transition panel in mustard-yellow light. Åsa sits at the computer and ticks settings for a booking: "Notify customer at: ☑ Booking ☑ Departure ☑ Arrival ☑ Delivery". navichain's official logo is visible discreetly in the corner of the interface — the first time in the story. **Caption:** Four tick boxes. The customer never needs to ask again. **Status:** 🟠 Orange — turning point, notifications are switched on

## Panel 8

**Visual description:** Robban Ek in the cab presses "Departure" in the driver app. At the same time, in a small mobile-screen close-up, an SMS is sent to the customer's phone: "Your delivery is on its way. Estimated arrival 14:20." navichain's official logo is visible discreetly in the SMS app's sender field. **Caption:** Robban presses one button. The customer knows — before they have even had time to wonder. **Status:** 🟢 Green — automatic notification at departure

## Panel 9

**Visual description:** Åsa's phone on the desk is noticeably silent. She looks at it, almost suspiciously, while drinking coffee from a mug with navichain's official logo discreetly visible. **Caption:** It is 11:00. The phone has not rung once. **Status:** 🟢 Green — the silence as proof

## Panel 10

**Visual description:** Split panel. Left: Robban delivers and signs POD digitally in the driver app. Right: the same customer who called angrily in panel 2, now smiling at their mobile screen where a delivery notification with a timestamp has just arrived. **Caption:** The delivery is signed — and in the same second the customer knows it has arrived safely. **Status:** 🟢 Green — the notification chain is completed

## Panel 11

**Visual description:** Åsa uses the time she has gained for something else: helping a new customer with a quotation instead of chasing status updates. The whiteboard behind her is now clean, with only one note left: "Notifications: ON." **Caption:** Same working day. Zero calls about "where are my goods". Time left for real questions. **Status:** 🟢 Green — freed-up time, new focus

## Panel 12

**Visual description:** Åsa leaves the office in good time, headset off and set down on the counter, with a light shrug and a genuine smile. Her bag has navichain's official logo discreetly visible as a small metal badge. A small note on the edge of the screen can just be seen: "Next: when five different principals share the same system — without seeing each other's goods." **Caption:** Forty calls became zero. Not because the customers stopped caring — but because they never needed to wonder. **Status:** 🟢 Green — result & teaser for the next story

## Cover concept

**Motif:** Åsa in the foreground holding the headset in her hand instead of wearing it, with a silent telephone beside her. In the blurred background: a row of old, torn paper slips "blowing away" and being replaced by a clean row of SMS icons moving towards a smiling customer on the horizon. navichain's official logo is discreetly visible in one of the SMS bubbles. **Title:** THE CALL THAT NEVER CAME **Subtitle:** Åsa & navichain's automatic notifications **Kicker (as on the other covers in the series):** "Forty calls a day. None of them necessary."
